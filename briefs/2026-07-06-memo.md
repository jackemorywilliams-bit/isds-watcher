## 1. This week's screened item — Suez v. Argentina (drop, but note the tension)

*Looking Back: In Suez v. Argentina (1), ICSID tribunal upheld jurisdiction over shareholders' treaty-based claims and used MFN clause to bypass a local litigation requirement* (iareporter, relevance 25, paywalled to headline). A retrospective piece on a decade-old jurisdictional decision. It touches only Ring 3, via one mechanic — MFN used to bypass a local-litigation requirement. There is **no IP asserted as investment** (Ring 1) and **no regulatory or judicial measure** as the wrong (Ring 2): the underlying dispute is a water-concession/shareholder-standing matter. It does not reach the research structure and merits no substantive analysis. **Drop.**

One use only: it is a *contrary data point* on the MFN question. Suez is a canonical example of MFN being used **successfully** to bypass a procedural gate — cutting against any belief that MFN-forum-bypass is spent. See §3.

## 2. Backsource cleanup carried from the daily notes (Santiago salmon)

The daily researcher fully backsourced the June 29 "Santiago salmon award set aside" item as **Joyvio Group v. Isidoro Quiroga** — a *domestic* Chilean commercial arbitration over alleged concealment/falsification of salmon-production data during the ~USD 921m acquisition of Australis Seafoods, with the Court of Appeal of Santiago annulling a ~USD 292m award on *ultra petita* grounds (SeafoodSource; Law360; GAR, per daily notes). Recorded, not counted as an insight.

## 3. PRIORITY — Okuashvili v. Georgia: the MFN thread moves (new, sourced)

The week's material advance. Investment Arbitration Reporter has published a piece whose headline states the holding directly: Swedish Supreme Court finds that claimant in Okuashvili v. Georgia can use MFN detour to access arbitration under SCC Arbitration Rules (iareporter.com). Read against the confirmed three-instance chain (SCC Partial Award Aug 2022 → Svea annulment Nov 2024 → Supreme Court reversal 26 June 2026), this confirms the *direction* of the reversal: the Supreme Court permitted the claimant to invoke an **MFN clause to reach the SCC forum**, restoring SCC jurisdiction. The GAR November 2024 report — Swedish court sides with Georgia on MFN clause (globalarbitrationreview.com) — corroborates the *lower*-court (Svea) posture that has now been reversed.

**Backsourcing discipline (Council check 2):** I confirm the *fact and direction* of the holding from the IAReporter title/URL plus the GAR corroboration of the procedural chain; I have **not** accessed the full Swedish Supreme Court text or its NJA citation (prior weeks noted the NJA cite is not expected until ~December 2026). Backsourced from the title via IAReporter + GAR; the judgment text itself was not accessible this session.

**Why this complicates our carried thread.** We had been carrying, at MEDIUM confidence, that "the Okuashvili MFN-forum-bypass pathway is architecturally closed for China BITs because Chinese BITs explicitly exclude MFN from ISDS." The new finding does not overturn the carve-out logic, but forces two corrections:

- **Okuashvili is a *pro*-MFN-detour precedent, not an anti-MFN one.** The Supreme Court *upheld* the detour, so the case is authority for the *vitality* of MFN forum-access where the base treaty does not exclude it. The "closed for China" conclusion is a narrow corollary that depends *entirely* on China-specific MFN-exclusion clauses — not on any general judicial hostility to the detour.
- **This week's Suez item points the same way** (MFN used to bypass a procedural gate, upheld). Two contemporary signals now indicate MFN-forum-bypass is *live* wherever treaty text permits it.

**Net for the research question:** the "China-BIT closure" claim should stay at **MEDIUM confidence and be re-labelled treaty-text-contingent, not doctrinally settled** — the chairman's skepticism is vindicated. On Ring 3 this means MFN remains a plausible route *into* a forum for an IP-as-investment claimant under a non-Chinese BIT: exactly the forum-access maneuver our theme watches.

**Outstanding:** whether any Svea remand remains open or the SCC arbitration is fully restored on the merits is **not resolved** by what I could access (the headline confirms jurisdiction restored; not the disposition of any remand). Carry forward. Practitioner analysis (Vinge, Baker Botts, GBS Disputes) was **not** yet indexed — continue to harvest.
