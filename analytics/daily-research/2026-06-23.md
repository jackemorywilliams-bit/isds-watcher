### A. Einarsson v. Canada (UNCT/20/6) — The Closest Contemporary Three-Ring Case

The case formally captioned *Theodore David Einarsson, Harold Paul Einarsson and Russell John Einarsson (on behalf of Geophysical Services Incorporated) v. Government of Canada* (ICSID Case No. UNCT/20/6) was filed in April 2019 under NAFTA Chapter 11. The claimants — three US-citizen shareholders of Geophysical Services Incorporated (GSI), a Canadian company — allege that Canada's regulatory regime governing offshore marine seismic data effectively confiscated GSI's proprietary data by authorising its disclosure to third parties (oil and gas explorers) without adequate compensation. GSI had acquired and licensed non-exclusive marine seismic data in the Canadian Atlantic and Arctic; licensing revenues were the company's primary value. The claim characterises this data as protected by copyright and trade-secret law, and asserts that its confiscation constituted expropriation, breach of fair and equitable treatment, and violations of NAFTA Articles 1116 and 1117.

**Three-ring fit:** Einarsson presents a partial but genuine fit with the research question's intersection:

- *Ring 1 (IP as covered investment):* GSI's seismic data is asserted as an investment on the basis of copyright and trade-secret protections — intangible assets. This is the first publicly documented ISDS case to rest a claim centrally on proprietary data as a trade-secret–protected investment. [(SSRN, Upreti, "Data, Copyright, and Investor-State-Arbitration: Insights from Einarsson v. Canada", 2023)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4328312); [(EFILA Blog, Oct 2024)](https://efilablog.org/2024/10/06/data-as-protected-investment-in-the-background-of-einarsson-v-canada/)

- *Ring 2 (Regulatory or judicial measure):* The challenged measure is Canada's regulatory regime — specifically, the Canada Petroleum Resources Act and related provisions permitting forced disclosure of proprietary seismic survey data. A Federal Court of Canada judgment dated 20 October 2020 (2020 FC 984) also features in the record, though the claim is primarily directed at the regulatory framework rather than a judicial act. [(Jusmundi, Einarsson and GSI v. Canada, Judgment 2020 FC 984)](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-theodore-david-einarsson-harold-paul-einarsson-and-russell-john-einarsson-geophysical-service-incorporated-v-government-of-canada-judgment-of-the-federal-court-of-canada-2020-fc-984-tuesday-20th-october-2020)

- *Ring 3 (Jurisdictional / admissibility):* Canada has maintained a jurisdictional objection throughout: the claimants allegedly failed to comply with the waiver requirement under NAFTA Article 1121, which is a condition of the state's consent to arbitration. This is a threshold admissibility-type issue, though of a different doctrinal character than the abuse-of-right / treaty-shopping cluster that anchors the Philip Morris line. [(UNCTAD Navigator; Jusmundi PO No. 10, July 2024)](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-theodore-david-einarsson-harold-paul-einarsson-and-russell-john-einarsson-geophysical-service-incorporated-v-government-of-canada-procedural-order-no-10-monday-8th-july-2024)

**Status:** The hearing on the merits and damages took place 3–13 March 2025 in Calgary. As of April 2025, updated party representatives were filed — the case is post-hearing and awaiting deliberation. Theodore David Einarsson died on 2 January 2024; succession issues have been briefed. No final award has been announced. [(Canada, Global Affairs — Geophysical Service case page)](https://www.international.gc.ca/trade-commerce/trade_topics-domaines_commerce/disp-diff/geophysical-service.aspx?lang=eng); [(Jusmundi, Procedural Order No. 10)](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-theodore-david-einarsson-harold-paul-einarsson-and-russell-john-einarsson-geophysical-service-incorporated-v-government-of-canada-procedural-order-no-10-monday-8th-july-2024)

**Calibration note:** Seismic data in an oil-and-gas context is a fingerprint negative signal, and this case should not be inflated to an on-theme match for the digest. The doctrinal framework — IP (data/trade secrets) as covered investment, regulatory measure, jurisdictional threshold — maps onto the three-ring structure conceptually, but the sector, the type of IP, and the jurisdictional doctrine are all distinct from the pharma/clinical-data seed cases. It is a *structurally analogous* case for scholarly purposes, not a direct successor.

### B. Post-Eli Lilly Landscape: USMCA's ISDS Architecture and the Pharmaceutical IP Gap

*Eli Lilly & Co. v. Government of Canada* (ICSID Case No. UNCT/14/2), Final Award, 16 March 2017, remains the paradigmatic three-ring case for pharmaceutical IP in ISDS: patents as covered investments, Canadian courts' application of the promise utility doctrine as the judicial measure, and the minimum-standard / fair-and-equitable-treatment standard applied. The tribunal dismissed the claim and awarded costs against Eli Lilly.

**USMCA sunset:** USMCA (in force July 1, 2020) eliminated ISDS between Canada and the United States. A three-year legacy period expired on 30 June 2023, after which US investors cannot file new ISDS claims against Canada under any USMCA provision, and Canadian investors face the same bar against the US. [(CRS, "USMCA: Investment Provisions", IF11167)](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11167); [(Kluwer Arbitration Blog, "ISDS Under the USMCA: The First Three Years at a Glance", Nov 2023)](https://arbitrationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/2023/11/25/isds-under-the-usmca-the-first-three-years-at-a-glance/)

This has a direct doctrinal implication: **no successor to Eli Lilly v. Canada can now be filed under the principal North American treaty framework.** A US pharmaceutical company alleging Canadian patent invalidation as denial of justice under a NAFTA-equivalent treaty has no forum. Pre-USMCA NAFTA claims (legacy period) remain pending where filed before July 2023; Einarsson is one such case.

Canada's domestic resolution of the trigger issue is also notable: three months after Canada's 2017 Eli Lilly win, the Supreme Court of Canada relaxed the promise utility doctrine in *AstraZeneca Canada Inc. v. Apotex Inc.*, [2017] 1 SCR 536 (decided 30 June 2017), adopting a more permissive utility standard. The regulatory condition that animated the Eli Lilly claim is itself largely gone. [(SSRN, Baker & Geddes, "The Incredible Shrinking Victory: Eli Lilly v. Canada, Success, Judicial Reversal, and Continuing Threats from Pharmaceutical ISDS")](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3012538)

**Mexico pathway:** Under USMCA Chapter 14, Mexico retains ISDS but primarily for government contracts in oil/gas, power, infrastructure, and telecommunications sectors. Other investors must exhaust local remedies first and wait 30 months before initiating arbitration. No publicly documented pharmaceutical IP ISDS case against Mexico has emerged under USMCA. [(Norton Rose Fulbright, "Major changes for ISDS in USMCA")](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/91d41adf/major-changes-for-investor-state-dispute-settlement-in-new-united-states-mexico-canada-agreement)

**Residual pathways:** Pharmaceutical IP claims against third states remain open under older BITs and ICSID-forum treaties. The USMCA sunset is North America–specific; it does not affect claims under EU-member bilateral investment treaties, the CPTPP, or bilateral arrangements with states that retain ISDS. The research question's three-ring structure therefore remains live for pharmaceutical IP claims against non-USMCA states, but the flagship North American forum is now closed.

### C. Trade-Secret and Clinical-Data Gap: Still Theoretical

Searches across the UNCTAD ISDS Navigator update (through July 2024), italaw, and practitioner commentary confirm the prior assessment in the Ferguson and Kim memos: **no publicly known ISDS case has yet been filed or decided with trade secrets or pharmaceutical clinical trial data as the primary investment asset.** 

The "data exclusivity" provision under USMCA (Chapter 20) — which requires states to provide test data exclusivity for pharmaceutical products — creates a treaty obligation that could in principle underpin an ISDS claim, but no such claim has been filed. [(CRS, "USMCA: Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)", IF11314)](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11314) This remains a prospective risk, not a live case.

Einarsson is the nearest analogue (proprietary data / trade-secret layer + regulatory measure + jurisdictional objection), but the data involved is industrial seismic survey data, not pharmaceutical clinical data, and the regulatory mechanism differs fundamentally. The doctrinal gap identified in the seed memos — how a tribunal would characterise pharmaceutical test data as "covered investment," quantify its value, and assess a regulatory measure that permits data reliance by generic competitors as the "violation" — remains unresolved in the public case law.

### D. Recent Denial-of-Justice Awards: Bachar Kiwan v. Kuwait (2025)

The ICSID tribunal in *Bachar Kiwan v. State of Kuwait* (ICSID Case No. ARB/20/53) issued its Final Award on 10 March 2025 under the France–Kuwait BIT, formally finding due process violations and a denial of justice. [(Jusmundi, Kiwan v. Kuwait, Award, 10 Mar 2025)](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-bachar-kiwan-v-state-of-kuwait-award-monday-10th-march-2025) The search record does not reveal an IP component in this case; it appears to be a Ring 2 (judicial/regulatory measure) matter only, without an IP-as-investment nexus. It is mentioned here for calibration: the fact that a denial-of-justice finding was made in 2025 shows the doctrine remains active, but this case does not advance the three-ring intersection.

### E. Structural Observations on the Field (2023–2026)

The UNCTAD ISDS Navigator recorded 1,401 known cases as of July 2024. Academic commentary confirms that IP-as-investment cases remain rare: there have been a "number of high-profile" cases in the last decade, but the field is not crowded. [(Oxford Academic, ICSID Review, "International Intellectual Property Law as Applicable Law in Investment Disputes", 2025)](https://academic.oup.com/icsidreview/article/40/1/174/8121539) The abuse-of-right / treaty-shopping line from Philip Morris (2015) is now established precedent on the critical-date / reasonably-foreseeable-dispute test, but no recent case has applied that doctrine specifically to an IP-as-investment restructuring. [(Arbitration Blog, "Turning Away at the Gate: Treaty Shopping in Light of the Philip Morris Case")](https://www.arbitrationblog.org/post/turning-away-at-the-gate-treaty-shopping-in-light-of-the-philip-morris-case)
