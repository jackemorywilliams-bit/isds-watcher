## Part V — Full-Council Discussion — Optimize & Seek a Breakthrough

**On the Hela Schwarz tribunal composition:**

The confirmation that Professor Campbell McLachlan KC served as an arbitrator alongside Sir Daniel Bethlehem KC (presiding) and Roland Ziadé enriches the project record's understanding of the Award's authoritativeness. McLachlan is co-author of *Investment Treaty Arbitration* (Cambridge University Press, third edition), which is the foundational English-language text on the field. His tribunal's unanimous application of Protocol Article 9's three sequential pre-ICSID gate conditions — mandatory administrative review (9(a)), three-month temporal wait (9(b)), and concurrent-proceedings bar (9(c)) — carries interpretive weight beyond that of most arbitral panels. This is not to assert that ICSID tribunals cite each other as precedent in a formal sense, but a panel of this composition is likely to produce a methodologically rigorous reading of the Protocol's requirements. The implication for the research question: the three-condition pre-ICSID architecture for China-Germany BIT (2003) claims was established by a tribunal whose composition reinforces that the reading is defensible and not an outlier. This does not change the legal analysis but confirms the finding's robustness.

**On the China-France BIT Protocol advance:**

Three independent search queries today returned consistent synthesis suggesting the China-France BIT (2007) uses "may request" permissive language — the same formulation used in the China-Switzerland BIT (2009) and the China-Sweden BIT (1982/2004 Protocol). The Netherlands ILR article on Chinese BIT ISDS evolution frames this distinction clearly: "may" provisions are discretionary requests by the host state, not treaty-mandated exhaustion requirements. This is methodologically consistent with the BIT's generation: the China-France BIT (2007) is a third-generation treaty, signed the same year as the China-South Korea BIT (2007), and the academic literature consistently classifies third-generation Chinese BITs as using permissive rather than mandatory administrative exhaustion language.

The council notes, however, that the security officer is correct to maintain UNCONFIRMED status. The consistent signals raise the probability that the permissive characterization is correct, but probability is not confirmation. The cross-BIT typology table (proposed July 19, optimization log) cannot be marked complete until the China-France BIT entry is confirmed from a named source. The path of least resistance remains the operator IIA Mapping Navigator query (see optimization proposal below).
