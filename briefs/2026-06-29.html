What is verifiable on A/CN.9/1246: submitted, not adopted

The chairman asked whether A/CN.9/1246 was adopted versus referred back or merely noted. The advance copy is real and was retrieved: it is the "Draft supplementary provisions on the conduct of proceedings to resolve international investment disputes (advance copy)," and the retrieved PDF carries the distribution header dated 15 April 2026. Crucially, the 59th session runs 29 June – 10 July 2026 in New York — it opens on the week-ending date itself. The Commission landing page already lists the session inputs, but no post-session report exists yet because the session has not concluded.

The disposition must therefore be logged as: submitted to the Commission as an advance copy, with disposition pending. Any secondary source claiming "adoption" this week would be reporting a future or an inference and should be rejected. Note: a companion document said to compile comments on A/CN.9/1246, and the provisional agenda document number, are referenced in the analyst's working notes but could not be confirmed against retrieved sources this run and are treated as unverified. Paragraph-level pinpoint cites to the draft for the Protocol/UAR disposition could not be confirmed against the document body and are likewise not relied upon here.

The dual-nationality / natural-person gap: no evidence it was added

On the agenda's second question — whether the dual-nationality / natural-person gap (the Jason Yu Song v China natural-person gap from prior weeks) stayed off-package — the available record shows no evidence the gap was added, and it appears off-package on the present record. A/CN.9/1246 is the procedural-conduct instrument (covering evidence, interim measures, early dismissal, costs, third-party funding, counterclaims); the cross-cutting and substantive-jurisdiction items sit on separate tracks. The characterizations of what the preceding 53rd and 54th sessions covered — counterclaims scope, a third-party-funding disclosure compromise, deferred damages discussions, and the standing-tribunal/appellate statutes — derive from secondary reporting that was not verifiably retrieved this run and are flagged as unverified.

The interpretive significance for the project, stated within those limits: the reform deliverable most likely to advance in 2026 does not, on the available record, touch the abuse-of-right, treaty-shopping, or nationality-planning doctrines that dispose of the project's seed cases. The jurisdictional gate the project studies appears to remain judge-made and case-by-case, even as the surrounding procedural framework is rebuilt.

Context point: opt-in, non-retroactive architecture (inference, not established feature)

A structural point worth carrying as context — not as a logged doctrinal insight — is that the reform package may operate on an opt-in, non-retroactive basis. This is an inference drawn by analogy to the 2013 Transparency Rules and the Transparency Convention precedent, under which a supplement does not automatically apply to proceedings arising under pre-existing treaties unless States opt in. It is not an established feature of A/CN.9/1246 on the verified record, and the supporting secondary sources were not confirmed this run.

If that architecture holds, the implication for Ring 3 is that even an adopted early-dismissal or manifest-lack-of-merit provision would not retro-apply to the BIT-era treaties under which IP-as-investment claims (Philip Morris, Eli Lilly, Bridgestone, Hela Schwarz) arise. On that reading the reform would largely not harmonize the jurisdictional gate for the legacy treaty stock that generates IP-investment disputes. This is advanced as a prospective lead to test, consistent with quiet-week discipline, rather than a project takeaway.
