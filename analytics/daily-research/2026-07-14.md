### 2B. B-Mex v. Mexico — Backsource: Near-Miss, No IP Nexus

**Confirmed gaming/casino sector; no IP nexus.** B-Mex, LLC and Others v. United Mexican States (ICSID Case No. ARB(AF)/16/3) is a NAFTA Chapter 11 arbitration brought by US casino investors (B-Mex, LLC; B-Mex II, LLC; Palmas South, LLC; Oaxaca Investments, LLC; Santa Fe Mexico Investments, LLC; Gordon Burr; Erin Burr; John Conley — Colorado LLCs and US nationals). The underlying dispute concerns the Mexican government's 2014 closure and raiding of the claimants' casino operations: on April 24, 2014, Mexico's SEGOB (Secretaría de Gobernación), aided by federal police, raided and closed all of the claimants' casinos, seized equipment and bank accounts, and invalidated gaming permits. The claimants alleged violations of NAFTA Article 1105 (minimum standard of treatment) and Article 1110 (expropriation). Final Award issued June 21, 2024, ordering Mexico to pay more than USD 130 million. No IP, trade secret, or pharmaceutical nexus.

**Ontario court litigation chain:** Mexico sought to set aside the award in Ontario. The Ontario Superior Court (April 2026) set aside the award on two grounds: (1) the tribunal had wrongly declined jurisdiction over certain claims, and (2) an arbitrator failed to disclose a similar mandate creating a reasonable apprehension of bias. On **July 7, 2026**, the **Ontario Court of Appeal reversed** that set-aside, restoring the award and finding that Mexico failed to establish grounds for annulment. Mexico failed to overturn the NAFTA award. The award (USD 130M+) now stands following the Court of Appeal decision.

**Research question relevance:** The B-Mex litigation chain (NAFTA award + Ontario set-aside attempt + CA reversal) is procedurally interesting as an example of post-award judicial review of a NAFTA arbitration in the seat court. It is a near-miss on Ring 3 (the CA decision involves a set-aside challenge addressing a jurisdiction and arbitrator-bias ground), but has no IP-as-investment nexus, no pharmaceutical element, and no trade-secret dimension. The digest's WATCH score of 25 was correctly calibrated. The item is closed as a near-miss.

Key content from search-engine snippets:

- No investment treaty mentions AI, algorithms, machine learning, trained models, or training data.
- No ISDS case has been decided involving an AI asset as the covered investment.
- AI datasets and algorithms face the same jurisdictional recognition challenge pharmaceutical clinical trial data faces: the investor must demonstrate that the asset satisfies domestic IP law requirements for recognition (patentability, trade-secret status, copyright subsistence) before it can constitute a "covered investment."

This is structurally parallel to the Ferguson/Kim pharmaceutical-data challenge (the "threshold investment problem") but is AI-specific, not pharmaceutical. The project record already captures the "no decided ISDS case for pharmaceutical data as investment" finding (Ring 1 gap) and the jurisdictional recognition challenge (Kim's structural skepticism).
