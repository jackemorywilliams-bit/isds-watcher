### 2B. Hela Schwarz v. China — Sector Clarification and New Academic Source

**Subject-matter confirmed (medium-high confidence):** Hela Schwarz GmbH was a German manufacturer of compound spices and food additives — not a pharmaceutical company. The investment at issue was land use rights held by its Chinese subsidiary (JHSF) in Shandong Province. The expropriation: the Jinan Municipal Government issued a Housing Expropriation Decision on September 11, 2014, to expropriate the land and buildings as part of the Huashan area renovation project (environmental improvement along the Xiaoqing river). ICSID arbitration filed May 2, 2017; merits hearing London, December 2022; Award December 10, 2025. Sources: [(A&O Shearman, "Landmark ICSID victory")](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/news/landmark-icsid-victory-for-the-peoples-republic-of-china); search-engine snippets of italaw award description.

**Doctrinal consequence:** The Article 9(c) of the Protocol to the China-Germany BIT (2003) — the concurrent-proceedings provision — is a cross-sector BIT provision, confirmed in a food additives/real estate dispute. Its applicability to a future pharmaceutical trade-secret claim under the same BIT is by structural analogy, not by sector-specific precedent. This does not weaken the precedent's Ring 3 relevance (the provision is textually universal), but a future pharmaceutical claimant and counsel should note that the only decided case applying it arose in a wholly different industry.

**Concurrent-proceedings detail confirmed (medium confidence):** "Hela is the only case touching upon the parallel proceedings issue among less than a dozen Chinese BIT-related investment arbitration cases filed with the ICSID." The claimant's Chinese subsidiary (JHSF) had initiated Chinese court proceedings before the ICSID claim was filed; the claimant argued Article 9(c) was "irrelevant, given that no domestic proceedings were pending at the time it filed the request for arbitration" — the domestic proceedings had concluded before the ICSID filing. The tribunal dismissed for lack of jurisdiction regardless. This confirms the July 11 reading: the "has been brought" language of Article 9(c) appears to capture completed-and-concluded domestic proceedings, not only pending ones. Primary Award PDF remains 403-blocked. Source: search-engine snippet identifying the case as addressing "whether a foreign investor may concurrently initiate proceedings before an ICSID tribunal and domestic courts."

**New academic source identified (medium-high confidence for existence):** A second peer-reviewed article on concurrent proceedings under Chinese BITs is now in the project record: "Analysis of Concurrent Proceedings under Chinese BITs," *ICSID Review — Foreign Investment Law Journal*, vol. 36, no. 3, p. 636 (Oxford Academic, 2021). This is distinct from the JIDS 2020 article ("Parallel Proceedings under Chinese BITs: The Case of Hela Schwarz GmbH v PR China," JIDS vol. 11(2), p. 335) already in the project record. Both articles predate the December 2025 Award; neither has been superseded by a post-Award case note (none found). The ICSID Review 2021 article may contain verbatim Article 9(c) Protocol language or alternative doctrinal framing. Body 403-blocked (Oxford Academic). Source: [(ICSID Review, Oxford Academic, vol. 36(3), p. 636)](https://academic.oup.com/icsidreview/article-abstract/36/3/636/6499203).
