This is the substance the agenda flagged as directly relevant to our jurisdictional-disposal pillar, now recoverable from a **primary source** rather than an AI paraphrase: <cite index="1-4">early dismissal of frivolous claims, security for costs and counterclaims were among the procedural concerns the Working Group set out to address, and the SPs are the vehicle. The interpretive point for the project: the doctrines our seed awards deployed case-by-case at the jurisdictional gate (foreseeability, abuse, manifest deficiency) are being codified as standing procedural tools — "Manifest lack of legal merit" is the early-dismissal analogue, bifurcation the vehicle for taking jurisdiction first. Note the applicability limits: the SPs are being developed to function primarily in an ad hoc setting, with disputing parties able to agree to preferred institutional rules as complemented by the SPs, and per secondary characterization the supplement would not automatically apply to ISDS proceedings arising under pre-existing investment treaties, allowing States that oppose it to avoid its application. So even once adopted, the early-dismissal tool would not retroactively govern the older BITs under which IP-as-investment disputes typically arise — a limit worth carrying forward.

Confirmed procedurally: these texts were submitted to the Commission at its fifty-ninth session in 2026, alongside A/CN.9/1265 and related documents on the Advisory Centre's operationalization.
