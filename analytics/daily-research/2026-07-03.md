### A. Thread T06 — Okuashvili v. Georgia: Swedish Supreme Court Rules MFN Can Confer Forum Jurisdiction

#### A.1 The three-layer Swedish court chain — full procedural picture

The *Okuashvili v. Georgia* case (SCC Case No. V 2019/058) has passed through three judicial layers.
All three layers are now confirmed from accessible sources.

**Underlying dispute.** Mr. Zaza Okuashvili, a dual Georgian-British national, initiated SCC arbitration in
May 2019 against Georgia over unpaid taxes and compulsory sale of his company (LLC OGT). His dual nationality
barred him from ICSID arbitration (ICSID excludes claims by nationals against their own state). He therefore
invoked the MFN clause in the Georgia-UK BIT to import the dispute resolution provision from the
Georgia-BLEU BIT, which allows SCC arbitration and grants dual nationals standing.
Source: [(AJIL, Cambridge Core, Okuashvili case note)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-journal-of-international-law/article/zaza-okuashvili-v-georgia-case-v-2019058/452908C87583F9F2CB54BAF0AD70CBF7);
[(arbitration.org case entry)](https://arbitration.org/award/1058).

**Layer 1 — SCC Partial Final Award on Jurisdiction (August 31, 2022):** The tribunal upheld jurisdiction.
It found that Article 3(2) of the Georgia-UK BIT's MFN clause permitted access to SCC arbitration under the
Georgia-BLEU BIT. Article 3(3), which "for the avoidance of doubt" applies MFN treatment to all provisions
of Articles 1–11 of the Georgia-UK BIT including Article 8 (dispute resolution), was key to the majority's
reasoning. Prof. Dr. Rolf Knieper dissented, arguing the MFN clause cannot expand consent to a different
arbitral forum. Sources: [(Jusmundi, Knieper Dissenting Opinion)](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/opinion/en-zaza-okuashvili-v-georgia-concurring-and-dissenting-opinion-of-professor-dr-rolf-knieper-wednesday-31st-august-2022);
[(italaw, Svea Court judgment document)](https://www.italaw.com/cases/documents/13202).

**Layer 2 — Svea Court of Appeal judgment (November 12, 2024):** The Svea Court annulled the SCC award for
want of jurisdiction, siding with Georgia. The court held that the MFN clause in the Georgia-UK BIT was
"incapable of extending Georgia's consent to ICSID arbitration under the Georgia-UK BIT to include SCC
arbitration under the Georgia-BLEU BIT." While the court acknowledged MFN clauses can apply to certain
dispute-resolution related procedures, it drew the line at importing wholesale consent to a different
arbitral institution. The Svea Court granted leave of appeal to the Swedish Supreme Court, recognising
the landmark importance of the question. Counsel for Georgia: Gaillard Banifatemi Shelbaya.
Sources: [(GBS Disputes, "major victory for Georgia at Svea")](https://www.gbsdisputes.com/gaillard-banifatemi-shelbaya-disputes-scores-major-victory-for-georgia-at-the-svea-court-of-appeal/);
[(Baker Botts, "No Consent on MFN" — headline and summary confirmed; body 403-blocked)](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2024/december/no-consent-on-mfn);
[(italaw, Svea Court judgment)](https://www.italaw.com/cases/documents/13202).

**Layer 3 — Swedish Supreme Court (leave granted September 22, 2025; ruling June 26, 2026):** The Swedish
Supreme Court reversed the Svea Court of Appeal. The ruling is confirmed from two independent, accessible
sources:

- The Swedish Supreme Court's own news page (domstol.se) carried a news release dated June 26, 2026,
  recovered via search engine summary: "An arbitral tribunal has been deemed competent on the basis of a
  so-called MFN clause." The page itself was 403-blocked; the text is drawn from the search engine snippet
  of the Swedish Supreme Court news service, not a directly-read page — marked accordingly
  (search-engine-recovered, not verbatim from the page).
  Source: [(Swedish Supreme Court news, June 26, 2026 — search engine summary)](https://www.domstol.se/en/supreme-court/news/).

- The IAReporter headline (June 29, 2026, paywalled body): "Swedish Supreme Court finds that claimant in
  Okuashvili v. Georgia can use MFN detour to access arbitration under SCC Arbitration Rules."
  Source: [(IAReporter, June 29, 2026)](https://www.iareporter.com/articles/swedish-supreme-court-finds-that-claimant-in-okuashvili-v-georgia-could-use-mfn-detour-to-access-arbitration-under-scc-arbitration-rules/).

The leave-grant date (September 22, 2025) is from a Vinge law firm note (2025) — page 403-blocked; date
recovered from search engine summary.
Source: [(Vinge, 2025, "Swedish Supreme Court to hear landmark MFN case" — summary)](https://www.vinge.se/en/insights/2025/swedish-supreme-court-to-hear-a-landmark-investment-treaty-case-regarding-mfn-clauses/).

#### A.3 Ring 3 significance for the research question

*Okuashvili* has NO IP content. The underlying claim is a tax/expropriation dispute by a dual-national
investor — no pharmaceutical data, no trade secrets, no regulatory challenge to IP rights. It sits outside
the three-ring thematic intersection.

Its Ring 3 relevance is doctrinal and marginal: the Swedish Supreme Court has confirmed that an MFN clause
in a BIT can confer forum competence by importing a dispute resolution provision from another treaty — at
least in SCC-seated, Swedish-law arbitrations. For the research question, this has the following
speculative (not established) inference: a dual-national IP investor who is excluded from ICSID could use
an MFN clause to access SCC or UNCITRAL arbitration under a third BIT as an alternative forum for a
Ring 1/Ring 2 IP-as-investment claim. This inference is presented as speculative — no such scenario has
been attempted or litigated.

**What this is not:** This ruling does not create a new jurisdictional gate applicable to pharmaceutical IP
claims, does not address trade secrets or clinical data, and does not alter the Ring 1 or Ring 2 analysis.
It is a Ring 3 toolkit addition for dual-national investors in SCC-seated arbitrations.

development. The project's intake is discovery-based (what's new in the feeds?), not lifecycle-based
(what has happened to cases we already know about?). Is there a concrete, new optimization idea here?

**Research Analyst:** The gap is structural. Once a case is tagged in the insight ledger with a ring
assignment and case identifier, the project has no mechanism to query for updates on that case — no
periodic docket watch. The UNCTAD Navigator, italaw, and ICSID each maintain case pages that update
when new documents or proceedings are registered. A "tagged-case posture tracker" module would: (1) maintain
a list of cases with ring assignments from the insight ledger; (2) periodically query those case pages
(UNCTAD Navigator, italaw, ICSID, and for Swedish-seated cases domstol.se) for new document postings;
(3) surface any new document to the chairman's agenda for that week. This would have caught the Svea Court
ruling in November 2024 and the Supreme Court leave-grant in September 2025, rather than waiting for the
final ruling to appear in an IAReporter headline.

**Security Officer:** Is this genuinely new relative to the optimization log? Checking the log: the three
existing ideas are (1) cross-model verifier [quality]; (2) full-text intake source [recall breadth]; (3)
agent-SDK subagents [architecture]. None of them specifically addresses lifecycle tracking of known cases.
This is a distinct idea — it concerns *temporal depth* (following a case across its institutional life)
rather than *intake breadth* (finding new cases) or *processing quality* (verifying what the analyst says).
New idea confirmed.

**Analytics Officer:** Source-level implementation: UNCTAD Navigator case pages already have a structured
URL pattern by case number. ICSID case pages update similarly. The tracker would not need to re-scrape the
web broadly — it would only need to query N known-case URLs periodically (perhaps weekly). Low incremental
cost, high recall on known-case progression. The only implementation challenge is that some case pages
(italaw, UNCTAD Navigator) are 403-blocked to automated fetch; this might require using search engines
to surface updates on a given case citation rather than direct page fetches.

**Chairman decision:** Log the optimization idea. Exact framing: *Tagged-case posture tracker — a periodic
docket-watch module that queries known case pages (UNCTAD Navigator, italaw, ICSID) for cases already in
the project record (tagged by ring assignment and case identifier), surfacing new documents or proceedings
to the weekly chairman's agenda. Addresses the temporal-depth gap: the Svea Court (November 2024) and
Swedish Supreme Court leave-grant (September 2025) in Okuashvili were not surfaced until the final ruling
appeared in the June 2026 digest.* Status: proposed.

**Is there a breakthrough?** Not today. The Okuashvili ruling is a confirmed new Ring 3 data point but
