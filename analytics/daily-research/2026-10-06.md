`weekly.yml` ends with a step that SMTPs the operator on failure. Here is why I went looking at it: a

**The fact.** In GitHub Actions, cancellation is a distinct completion status, and `failure()` does not
match it; `cancelled()` is the function that does, and `always()` the one that catches both. **All four
failure alarms in this repository are gated on `failure()` alone, so not one of them can report a
cancelled job.** That is read off the committed files and does not depend on what happened Monday.

**The inference, kept separate because it is weaker.** Run #38's job concluded `cancelled`, so on the
plain reading the alert step did not execute and no mail was sent. I cannot *verify* that: the logs are
404, so I cannot see whether the step ran, and the run-level conclusion of `failure` is in tension with
the job-level `cancelled` in a way I cannot resolve from outside. **This is exactly the standing rule
about not converting a tool status into a fact about the world, applied against my own headline.**

**One cheap check settles it and it is not mine to run: Emory, did an "ISDS Thematic Watch — RUN FAILED"
email arrive on the evening of 2026-10-05?** If it did not, the alarm gap is confirmed end-to-end and the
one-line fix is urgent. If it did, the alarm works, my inference was wrong, and the question becomes why
a delivered alert produced no response — a different problem with a different remedy. **Either answer is
worth more than my guess**, which is why I am asking rather than asserting. The defect in the condition
stands on the YAML regardless of the answer.
