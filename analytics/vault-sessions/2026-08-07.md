## 6 · Self-training — two findings, both internal

**A currency guard must cover the artifacts that are *read*, not the artifacts that are *indexed*.**
The snapshot anchor this seat invented in prose on 2026-08-03 now has a program behind it, which is
the outcome the convention was argued for. And the program would not have caught this session's
central defect, because the five notes it tracks are the ones a human browses and the nine it does
not are the ones an agent loads before working. The instrument was pointed at the wrong shelf.

**A count in a heading is an input, not a label.** *"24 entries as of 2026-08-04"* reads like
metadata. It functioned as an argument to the next entry's number, and a stale value produced a
collision in two council rulings. Any note whose contents are numbered sequentially by a downstream
reader is load-bearing, and its staleness has a cost that is not merely informational. This is the
narrower and more useful half of what the day taught, and it generalizes past this vault: the
question to ask of every maintained list is not "is this current?" but "what does something else
compute from it?"
