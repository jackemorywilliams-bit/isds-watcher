An automated thematic-surveillance instrument

The ISDS Thematic Watcher

A zero-cost, weekly monitor for investor–State dispute settlement at one precise doctrinal intersection: where intellectual property is asserted as a protected investment, a regulatory or judicial measure is the disputed conduct, and a jurisdiction or admissibility doctrine is live and litigated.

Status

Exploratory research prototype. 11 archived runs; 347 candidates screened; 0 above-threshold matches. Validation is preliminary. Output is machine-assisted and remains provisional unless individually marked human-verified — what that means.

What it watches

The object of surveillance is not a word but a relationship between three doctrines. The theme was induced verbatim from three seed awards (Philip Morris v. Australia, Eli Lilly v. Canada, and Bridgestone v. Panama) and operationalised as the overlap of three doctrinal “rings.”

Ring I

IP as a protected investment

Patents, trademarks and licences, copyrights, geographical indications, data exclusivity, and brand value asserted as a covered investment. The question is most acute where the asset is informational or split across holding and operating entities.

Ring II weighted

A regulatory or judicial measure

Public-interest legislation, a domestic court judgment, or judicial conduct itself as the disputed conduct. A credible challenge to a court judgment reaches the MEDIUM band on its own.

Ring III

Jurisdiction & admissibility

Abuse of right, treaty-shopping, foreseeability of the dispute, restructuring for treaty protection, and the contested definition of “investor” or covered “investment” — the threshold dimension anchored by Philip Morris, dispositive there and a recurring doctrinal risk across the broader seed pattern.

The three rings

Each ring is one doctrinal requirement the case must touch. Point at — or tab to — a ring to read what it covers, or the centre to see how the rings combine into a relevance band.

How it works

Each stage is defensive: a failing source or item is logged and skipped, never fatal. With no network the sources return empty; with no API key the classifier falls back to a deterministic keyword scorer, so a dry run works fully offline.

1
Gather new items
Each week the watcher pulls the latest awards, filings, news, and scholarship from open ISDS sources: the ICSID, UNCTAD, italaw, IISD Investment Treaty News, and IAReporter repositories, plus the project's own Google Alerts and Google Scholar feeds.
↓
2
Set aside what we have already seen
It fetches each item courteously, following every site’s access rules, and discards anything that appeared in an earlier week so nothing is reported twice.
↓
3
Read the most promising in full
Items are ranked by how closely their wording matches the project’s doctrinal vocabulary, and the strongest are fetched so that more than a case name can be read. Where a source is paywalled, only the headline is available and the item is scored from that.
↓
4
Judge relevance to the theme
A language model reads each item and rates how strongly it touches the three rings: IP as a protected investment, a judicial or regulatory measure, and a jurisdictional doctrine.
↓
5
Score and shortlist
Each item receives a relevance score, High, Medium, or Low. Those that meet the threshold make the digest. Near-misses at or above the relevance floor of 25 are shown separately as watch-list leads, not as matches; a genuinely quiet week reports zero items and says so.
↓
6
Write up and deliver
The shortlist is written as an annotated bibliography, saved to a dated archive, published to this website, and emailed to the recipients.

Figure 1. The weekly workflow, from gathering new material to delivering the digest. Every run is saved as a dated, version-controlled archive.

What you receive

Every Monday, an annotated-bibliography digest: each surfaced development as a citation line, a short descriptive-and-evaluative annotation, the rings it matched, and — where the source text was reachable — a quoted notable line drawn from it. Entries that came from a paywalled feed carry no quotation, are marked headline-only, and were assessed from the headline alone. The same content is committed to the repository under a dated archive folder, browsable and citable.

  • A citation in Chicago form, with the source named and the URL supplied liberally.
  • An annotation that is both descriptive and evaluative, reflecting on which rings the development implicates and why it matters to the project.
  • A verbatim notable line where the source text was reachable, so the reader can proceed directly to backsourcing. Headline-only entries carry no quotation and say so.
  • A direct link to the original on every entry, with the full URL printed alongside it.
