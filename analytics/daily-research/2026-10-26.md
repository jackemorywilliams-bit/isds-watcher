### 2.2 The one genuinely new thing the instrument learned today — and I kill my own insight candidate before the officer has to

Row 1's window is **exact-string-identical across all three firings** of this anchor on this docket
(`2026-10-24-daily.json records[1]`, `2026-10-25-daily.json records[2]`, `2026-10-26-daily.json
records[1]`): one distinct excerpt hash over three rows. The page's byte length is **constant at
85,351 bytes** on all three days. **And the page `sha256` differs on every one of the three:**
`9ba2c202ec18…`, `bf51e5ed5302…`, `6425a2e7aef3…`.

The same pattern holds on the other docket. `UNCT/20/6`'s `Status of Proceeding` row has fired twenty
times; across the last six it carries byte lengths 105,769, 105,769, 105,811, 106,083, 106,083,
106,083 and **six distinct hashes**, while the last seven firings share **one** distinct excerpt hash.

**So on this host a `sha256` inequality between two fetches is not evidence that anything the council
reads has changed, and a byte-length equality is not evidence that the document is the same
document.** The consequence for practice is the one that matters: **a successor must establish window
stability on the identity of the excerpt text, never on the page hash** — and conversely must not
read a hash change as a development.

**I am not logging this as an insight, because it is prior art and I checked before I wrote it
down.** `analytics/insights.jsonl:50`, logged 2026-08-04, already holds it in terms: *"equal byte
length across fetches with differing sha256 is MOST CONSISTENT WITH an origin-side constant-width
varying field (a nonce) rather than a content change, but it is not proof and it is not load-bearing
— confirmation should rest on identity of the retrieved excerpt text, and near-simultaneous same-run
fetches are one time point, not several observations."* The one thing today adds is that the ledger's
own caveat is now discharged on its own terms: that entry's instances were **same-run** fetches, one
time point, and today's are **three separate days** on one docket and six on another. **That is a
strengthening of an existing entry's evidence, not a new insight, and the honest disposition is to
say so and log nothing.**

**What it does change is a recommendation of mine from §1.5.** R-a's surviving justification was
chrome-change detection *"on the first batch after any observed change in a case-detail window"*.
**That trigger is now measurably unusable if a successor reads "change" off the hash**, because the
hash changes daily on both dockets while the window does not. The trigger must be phrased on the
**excerpt text**, and I correct my own §1.5 wording to that effect here rather than leave it to be
