### 2B. AsiaPhos and Norwest v. China (ICSID ADM/21/1) — New Background Ring 3 Case Discovered (GENUINE NEW INSIGHT)

**Case not previously in project record.** *AsiaPhos Limited and Norwest Chemicals Pte Ltd v. People's Republic of China* (ICSID Case No. ADM/21/1) is a completed ICSID arbitration — the second known decided ICSID case in which China successfully obtained a jurisdictional dismissal. It is the most-sourced China BIT ICSID decision currently absent from the project record.

**Facts:** Two Singaporean-incorporated companies (AsiaPhos Limited, its wholly-owned subsidiary Norwest Chemicals Pte Ltd) brought expropriation, FET, and full protection and security claims against China under the 1985 China-Singapore BIT (ADM = Additional Mechanism, as China has objected to full ICSID jurisdiction). The dispute arose from alleged violations relating to the prohibition of phosphate mining operations in Sichuan province. Award: **February 16, 2023** — all claims dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.

**Ring 3 mechanism — first-generation BIT narrow ISDS scope:** The 1985 China-Singapore BIT is a first-generation Chinese BIT with an exceptionally restricted ISDS provision: under Article 13(3) of the BIT, the arbitral tribunal's jurisdiction was "limited to disputes regarding the amount of compensation for expropriation." The tribunal found it lacked jurisdiction over all claims — including FET and FPS claims — because those claims exceeded the narrow scope of the BIT's arbitration clause. China argued (and the tribunal accepted) that issues concerning the occurrence, legality, or nature of expropriation fall within the mandate of national courts; only the quantum of compensation, once expropriation is proclaimed or undisputed, falls within ICSID jurisdiction. This mechanism is conceptually different from and independent of both the Hela Schwarz concurrent-proceedings/court-withdrawal mechanism (Protocol Art. 9(c)) and the Philip Morris abuse-of-rights/critical-date mechanism.

**Fork-in-the-road dimension:** China clarified at hearing that investors were not required to go to domestic courts prior to arbitration; rather, the fork-in-the-road clause was limited to preventing investors from submitting compensation disputes (the only ICSID-arbitrable disputes) to both domestic courts and ICSID. The tribunal confirmed this narrow construction of the fork-in-the-road clause. This is a different application of fork-in-the-road from the Hela Schwarz no-U-turn structure (which concerns court withdrawal as a pre-filing condition).

**Swiss Federal Supreme Court confirmation:** The FSC (4A_172/2023, January 11, 2024) affirmed the tribunal's restrictive jurisdictional rationale, confirming that Swiss courts will not disturb Chinese BIT arbitral jurisdiction findings under the deferential approach to substantive arbitral jurisdiction review.

**ICSID Review academic case note:** *"AsiaPhos Limited and Norwest Chemicals Pte Ltd v People's Republic of China: Unpredictable Jurisdiction under the Restrictive ISDS Mechanism"* — ICSID Review — Foreign Investment Law Journal, vol. 39, no. 1, p. 29 (Oxford Academic, 2024). Body 403-blocked; indexed title confirms a peer-reviewed case note exists in ICSID Review. The publication approximately 20 months after the February 2023 Award suggests a similar case note for Hela Schwarz (December 2025 Award) would appear in ICSID Review approximately August–October 2027 — later than previously estimated.

**IISD ITN article:** "ICSID tribunal upholds China's jurisdictional objection" — IISD Investment Treaty News, July 1, 2023. Body 403-blocked.

**Relevance assessment:** This is a Ring 3 background case. It is NOT on-theme for the pharmaceutical trade-secret scenario: (a) the China-Singapore BIT (1985) is a first-generation BIT with narrow ISDS scope; the three European China BITs in the project's primary scope (Germany 2003, Switzerland 2009, France 2007) are second/third-generation BITs with broader ISDS access — the first-generation ISDS scope limitation that disposed of AsiaPhos does not apply to them; (b) AsiaPhos involves phosphate mining licenses, not IP, trade secrets, or pharmaceutical data. The Ring 3 significance is structural: this case confirms a **third jurisdictional mechanism** in Chinese BIT ISDS (first-generation BIT ISDS scope limitation), distinct from the Hela Schwarz concurrent-proceedings mechanism and the Philip Morris abuse-of-rights mechanism. A comprehensive Ring 3 taxonomy for China BIT claims now has four mechanisms: (1) Philip Morris abuse-of-rights; (2) NAFTA Art. 1121 waiver (NAFTA only); (3) Hela Schwarz concurrent-proceedings/court-withdrawal; (4) first-generation BIT ISDS scope limitation (inapplicable to third-generation BITs). For the pharmaceutical trade-secret scenario under the China-Germany BIT (2003), mechanism (4) does not apply; mechanisms (1) and (3) remain the operative gates.

### 2C. China-Netherlands BIT Protocol — Administrative Exhaustion Requirement Confirmed (Partial New Detail)

**New detail added to existing thread (T-Wingtech).** Today's Protocol scan of the China-Netherlands BIT (2001) recovered, from a search-engine result summary of the BIT text, the following Protocol/Exchange of Notes provision:

> *"The Kingdom of the Netherlands took note that the People's Republic of China requires investors to exhaust the domestic administrative review procedure specified by Chinese laws and regulations before submission of a dispute to international arbitration under Article 10, paragraph 3, with such procedure taking a maximum period of three months."*

And the concurrent-proceedings/domestic court withdrawal condition from Article 10:

> *"if a legal dispute concerning an investment in China has been submitted to a domestic court, it may only be submitted to international dispute settlement if the investor has withdrawn its case from the domestic court."*

**What is new:** The July 9, 2026 project record documented the court withdrawal requirement and the asymmetric Article 10 structure (investments in China: withdrawal required before ICSID; investments in Netherlands: submit "at any time"). It did NOT document the administrative review exhaustion requirement. Today's finding adds a second pre-ICSID gate condition to the China-Netherlands BIT architecture for Chinese-side investments.

**Combined China-Netherlands BIT (2001) pre-ICSID gate for investments in China:**

| Step | Source | Condition |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | Protocol/Exchange of Notes | Exhaust Chinese administrative review (max 3 months) |
| 2 | Art. 10(3) | Six-month cooling-off from date amicable settlement requested |
| 3 | Art. 10 | Withdraw any concurrent domestic court proceedings before ICSID filing |

**Cross-BIT architectural confirmation:** The administrative review exhaustion requirement is now confirmed as a shared architectural feature of both the China-Germany BIT (2003) (Protocol Art. 9(a), MEDIUM confidence) and the China-Netherlands BIT (2001) (Protocol/Exchange of Notes, MEDIUM confidence). This cross-BIT consistency supports the MEDIUM-confidence level of both — they are independent sources converging on the same structural point.

**Applicability to Wingtech/Nexperia:** The asymmetric architecture means this requirement applies to investments *in China*; Wingtech is a Chinese investor with an investment *in the Netherlands*. The administrative exhaustion requirement is therefore inapplicable to Wingtech's claim. This does not change the July 9 finding on Wingtech.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** Text from search-engine result summary of ASEAN Briefing PDF (https://www.aseanbriefing.com/userfiles/resources-pdfs/China/BIT/Asia_BIT_Netherlands_China.pdf, 403-blocked for direct fetch); primary Protocol text not directly read. The "took note" formulation is characteristic of Exchange of Notes/Protocol provisions in Chinese BITs. Consistent with: [(UNCTAD Navigator, China-Netherlands BIT 2001)](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/international-investment-agreements/treaties/bilateral-investment-treaties/946/china---netherlands-bit-2001-); [(Netherlands ILR, Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40802-020-00182-3); [(Kluwer, "A Crash Foretold?")](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/arbitration-blog/a-crash-foretold-where-dutch-economic-security-meets-chinas-potential-investment-arbitration-leverage-the-nexperia-case/)
