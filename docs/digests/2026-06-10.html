    Telefónica challenged Colombia's reversion of telecommunications assets under a BIT, alleging breaches of fair and equitable treatment stemming from legislative changes and a Constitutional Court judgment. Although the case involves a judicial measure (Constitutional Court Sentencia C-555) and state regulatory conduct, it does not involve intellectual property as a covered investment, and the jurisdictional objections (res judicata, denial of justice) were dismissed on the merits rather than decided as threshold admissibility doctrines; thus it fails to meet the intersection of two rings required for HIGH or the focused admissibility-doctrine triggers that would elevate a judicial-measure case to MEDIUM.

    Notable line

    “It affirmed its jurisdiction to assess whether state measures, including judicial acts, complied with the treaty, irrespective of a denial of justice claim.”

    Source Italaw Read the original ↗ https://www.italaw.com/cases/3958 26 May 2026

    This case concerns provisional measures and revision proceedings in a dispute between Hydro S.r.l. and Albania involving criminal proceedings, tax investigations, and extradition requests, with the revision tribunal rejecting Albania's argument that a Tirana court criminal conviction constituted a discoverable pre-award fact. Although it involves a domestic court judgment and judicial conduct, the underlying investment dispute concerns neither intellectual property nor any jurisdictional/admissibility doctrine targeting IP or corporate restructuring; the judicial measure here is incidental to retaliation allegations rather than central to an IP claim.

    Notable line

    “The Tribunal held that the Tirana Judgment, being a post-award event, could not constitute a pre-award 'fact' und”

    This case concerns a breach-of-contract dispute between a solar company and Lesotho over a renewable energy supply agreement, resolved through commercial arbitration and subsequently reviewed by South African courts on enforcement and validity grounds. It involves no intellectual property, no challenge to a judicial measure or denial of justice on treaty grounds, and no jurisdictional-admissibility doctrine (abuse of right, treaty-shopping, or standing), placing it outside the three-ring ISDS-IP theme entirely.

    Notable line

    “The Court found compelling evidence that the agreement was concluded without the requisite authority from the Minister of Finance or the Cabinet and in breach of Lesotho's procurement and financial laws.”
