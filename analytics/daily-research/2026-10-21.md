## 3. SECOND LIMB — the exact scope of the defect in `analytics/insights.jsonl:270`

**My ruling: the hedge should be NARROW.** It belongs on one sentence. The "fortnight denominator is 18, not 35" conclusion survives the defect and must not be hedged with it.

### 3.1 The clause that carries the defect, quoted

> "**The twelve ITN articles carry their own dates in their URL paths and run 2009-03-03 to 2024-01-13; NOT ONE is in the run window and the newest predates it by about 32 months.**"

Four distinct propositions are packed into that sentence and they fail differently:

1. *"carry their own dates in their URL paths"* — **this is the error.** The segment is an ITN **issue**-date segment, not a per-article publication date. The correct wording is "path-dated", per `1020-C17`.
2. *"run 2009-03-03 to 2024-01-13"* — salvageable verbatim **if relabelled** as the range of path segments rather than of publication dates. The endpoints are real strings in real URLs.
3. *"NOT ONE is in the run window"* — **strongly indicated, not established**, on exactly the footing `1020-C17` and line 291 adopt.
4. *"the newest predates it by about 32 months"* — **the most exposed clause of the four**, because it converts a path segment into a stated duration. It should go, or be rewritten as a statement about path segments.

### 3.2 What survives untouched, and why

> "**SECOND, DATE-BLINDNESS: 17 of the 35 telemetry rows carry no publication date, and they are exactly the 12 iisd_itn and 5 italaw archive-recovered rows — by construction, src/source_recovery.py building each with published=None under '# capture date != decision date' and recover_with_report's docstring stating '``since`` is accepted for signature symmetry with ``Source.fetch`` and is not used to filter'.**"

Measured and code-grounded. No path date anywhere in it. Untouched.

> "**So the fortnight denominator is 18, not 35.**"

**This survives, and the proof is inside line 270 itself.** Five of the seventeen rows the line excludes are `italaw` rows, and italaw URLs carry no date segment at all — I measured it: of the **74** italaw URLs in `state/seen.json`, **zero** contain a `yyyy/mm/dd` segment, and the recovery spec's `path_regex` for italaw is `/cases/(?:documents/)?[0-9]+$`. So the line already excludes five of its seventeen rows **with no path-date argument available to it**. The ground on which it does that — `published=None`, and a recovery path whose `since` parameter "*is not used to filter*" — excludes the twelve ITN rows on identical terms. A row that was never window-filtered and carries no publication date cannot be counted into a fortnight denominator whatever its URL says.

The distinction to hold onto: line 270 would need the path dates only if it were claiming the seventeen rows are **affirmatively out of window**. It does make that claim, in clause 3 above, and that claim takes the hedge. The denominator claim is the weaker and different one — that those rows are **not known to be in window** — and it stands on date-blindness alone.

### 3.3 One strengthening I can offer, measured today

`1020-C17` rested on a single sibling article at the identical `/2018/10/18/` path. The committed record is far stronger than that. Grouping all **54** dated `iisd_itn` URLs in `state/seen.json` by path date gives **22 distinct path dates**, of which **7 carry more than one article**; the `/2018/10/18/` family holds **four** — `metalclad-v-mexico`, `methanex-v-united-states`, `pac-rim-v-el-salvador`, `sgs-v-pakistan` — and the largest families are `/2026/09/09/` with **17** articles and `/2026/04/21/` with **10**. Seventeen distinct articles at one path date is not a publication date in any sense that could be per-article. **The issue-date reading is now a measured structural property of the source, not an inference from one coincidence.**

Two consequences, and they point in opposite directions, so I state both. For the *window* question — "was this published inside the fortnight?" — an issue segment of 2018 remains strong evidence of "no", which is why the hedge is "strongly indicated" and not a strike. For the *staleness* question — "how old is this item?" — the `/2018/10/18/` family's four slugs all name matters that substantially predate 2018, so for those pages the path segment dates neither the content nor, demonstrably, anything about the underlying matter. Clause 4's "about 32 months" rides on the second reading and is the clause I would most want gone.

The cleanest way to settle whether an ITN path date is a publication date is to read one ITN page's dateline.
