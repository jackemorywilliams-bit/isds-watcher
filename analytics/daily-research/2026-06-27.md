### A. Thread T05 — 59th Commission Session: Agenda Broader Than Documented

#### A.1 Full session scope: four tracks, not one

The 59th Commission session (June 29–July 10, 2026, New York) carries a materially broader agenda than STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER.md characterised. The session encompasses four distinct ISDS reform tracks:

1. **Procedural Protocol (A/CN.9/1246)** — "Draft supplementary provisions on the conduct of proceedings to resolve international investment disputes": evidence, bifurcation, interim measures, manifest lack of legal merit, security for costs, TPF disclosure, counterclaims, cost allocation. This is the WG III-prepared procedural text, to be considered for adoption. State comments compiled in A/CN.9/1269. Sources: [(UNCITRAL, A/CN.9/1246)](https://uncitral.un.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/a_cn9_1246_advance_copy.pdf); [(UNCITRAL, Procedural Rules Reform page)](https://uncitral.un.org/en/proceduralrulesreformandcrosscuttingissues)

2. **Draft statutes for standing first-tier and appellate mechanisms** — Prepared by the Secretariat for discussion at the 54th WG III session (March 23–27, 2026, Vienna). The 54th session debated scope (whether to include state-to-state dispute settlement), jurisdiction, and how to operationalise exclusivity. These statutes are referred to the Commission. Sources: [(UNCITRAL, Multilateral Permanent Investment Court page)](https://uncitral.un.org/en/multilateralpermanentinvestmentcourt); [(UNCITRAL Working Group III page)](https://uncitral.un.org/en/working_groups/3/investor-state)

3. **MIIR (Multilateral Instrument to Implement Reforms)** — The draft framework convention that would make ISDS reforms applicable to existing investment agreements and to proceedings under other arbitration rules. First full draft published July 2024; discussed at WG III 51st session (February 2025). On the 59th Commission agenda as part of the reform delivery package. Sources: [(UNCITRAL, MIIR page)](https://uncitral.un.org/en/reformimplementation); [(Kluwer, "2025 in Review")](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/arbitration-blog/2025-in-review-isds-reforms-in-review/)

4. **Advisory Centre on International Investment Dispute Resolution** — Documents A/CN.9/1265 (third operationalisation meeting summary) and A/CN.9/1266 (interim secretariat issues) are before the Commission. Source: [(UNCITRAL, 59th Session Indico page)](https://indico.un.org/event/1022838/)

**Calibration note:** No direct access to the provisional agenda document (A/CN.9/1235) — PDF 403-blocked. The four-track characterisation is built from UNCITRAL's own thematic pages, the session Indico overview, and practitioner commentary (Kluwer, IISD). Accept at medium-high confidence.

**Significance for T05:** The 59th Commission session is not only a procedural-Protocol delivery moment — it is the broadest ISDS reform milestone in several years. The standing-mechanism statutes and MIIR are structural deliverables for the post-arbitration investment court model. None of these tracks addresses the denial-of-benefits provision (Draft Provision 9, WP.253) or the natural-person nationality-planning gap from *Jason Yu Song*. That gap persists.

### B. Thread T04/China — June 5, 2026 Transitional Deadline: Crystallisation of Ring 1

#### B.1 Deadline confirmed: waiver is definitive

China's NMPA Implementation Measures for Drug Trial Data Protection (effective May 15, 2026) required applicants with already-accepted but not-yet-complete drug registration applications to submit data protection requests to the CDE by **June 5, 2026**. Failure to submit by that date is treated as a **definitive waiver** of pharmaceutical trial data protection rights for that application. As of today (June 27, 2026), the deadline is 22 days past. Sources: confirmed from search summaries citing Jones Day ("China's New Regulatory Data Protection Regime Becomes Effective Immediately," May 2026) [(Jones Day, May 2026)](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/chinas-new-regulatory-data-protection-regime-becomes-effective-immediately) and Ropes & Gray [(Ropes DataPhiles, May 2026)](https://www.ropesdataphiles.com/2026/05/chinas-nmpa-issues-final-measures-on-regulatory-data-protection/).

**Significance for Ring 1:** The June 5 closure crystallises the covered-investment set. Multinational pharmaceutical investors who filed by June 5 now hold formal, legally defined data exclusivity entitlements under Chinese domestic law. Those entitlements are precisely the type of "protectable and tradable" IP assets that — if subsequently restricted or discriminatorily applied — could ground a Ring 1 investment claim under European BITs (China-Germany 2003: "trade and business secrets"; China-Switzerland 2009: "know-how"; China-France 2007: "know-how"). The threshold question for any future ISDS claim is now sharper: did the relevant European pharmaceutical investor file by June 5?

#### B.2 Two distinct regulatory layers now fully in force

STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER.md references the NMPA Implementation Measures (May 15, 2026) as the "China NMPA 2026" ring-1 foundation. A second, broader regulatory layer is now also in force:

- **State Council: Implementing Regulations of the Drug Administration Law** (issued January 2026, effective May 2026) — the primary statutory implementing measure. Sets the six-year maximum data exclusivity ceiling; does not address protection period for drugs first approved outside China. Source: [(Ropes & Gray, January 2026)](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/china-announces-the-implementing-regulation-of-the-drug-administration-law)
- **NMPA: Implementation Measures for Drug Trial Data Protection** (issued and effective May 15, 2026) — the detailed administrative implementation, including the tiered protection schedule, application procedures, and the June 5 transitional deadline.

The ISDS-relevant asset (the pharmaceutical trial data exclusivity entitlement) is now grounded in both layers.

#### B.3 USTR 2026 Special 301 Report — "unfair and discriminatory" characterisation

The 2026 USTR Special 301 Report (May 2026) identifies China as retaining Priority Watch List status. On pharmaceutical data specifically, the Report states that China continues to "impose unfair and discriminatory conditions on the effective protection against unfair commercial use, as well as unauthorized disclosure, of undisclosed test or other data generated to obtain marketing approval for pharmaceutical products." The specific concern is that China's final measures do not adequately address the protection period for drugs *first approved outside China*, and the US and China have agreed to address this in future negotiations. Sources: [(National Law Review, May 2026)](https://natlawreview.com/article/china-retains-priority-watch-list-status-ustrs-2026-special-301-report-continuing); [(China IP Law Update, May 2026)](https://www.chinaiplawupdate.com/2026/05/china-retains-priority-watch-list-status-in-ustrs-2026-special-301-report-with-continuing-concerns-over-technology-transfer-trade-secrets-and-counterfeiting/); [(2026 Special 301 Report PDF)](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/2026%20Special%20301%20Report.pdf)

**Calibration:** Direct PDF access 403-blocked. The cited characterisations are drawn from secondary reporting (National Law Review, China IP Law Update) and the PDF URL itself (confirming existence and title). Both secondary sources reproduce the same specific USTR language in their summaries. Medium-high confidence.

**Significance for three-ring analysis:** The USTR's "unfair and discriminatory conditions" language is the US government's formal articulation of the same discriminatory treatment argument that would ground a Ring 2 regulatory-measure claim. The US has no BIT with China and cannot pursue investor-state arbitration against China. European pharmaceutical investors — who can claim under the China-Germany, China-Switzerland, and China-France BITs — could in principle reference the USTR's characterisation as corroborating evidence that China's pharmaceutical data regime applies unfair and discriminatory conditions. This is the first identified government-level characterisation of China's pharmaceutical data measures as potentially discriminatory, adding a new dimension to the Ring 2 analysis.
