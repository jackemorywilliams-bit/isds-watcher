# The Carrying-Span Rule (binding)

Adopted by the council 2026-08-03 **as amended**. The text below is R3 of
`analytics/council-sessions/2026-08-03-proposition-rule.md` — the final form, with
the officer's four amendments folded in. Read the record for why each clause is
worded as it is; do not lift the draft at Part 3 §1 of that record, which predates
the amendments.

**Status: ADOPTED in text, UNVALIDATED in practice** (R8). See "When this becomes
validated" below. A rule already existed when the failures occurred and did not bind,
so the council refused to record this one as working on the strength of its adoption.

---

## THE RULE

**Scope.** Every source cited for a proposition — in a memo, a bibliography, or a
council record — whether the source is one the work under review cited or one you
found yourself. **Your own citations are not exempt.** (Separate from
`METHODOLOGY.md` §VII, whose "at least one direct verbatim quote" is an *anchor* so a
digest reader can find the source, not *support* for a proposition. That standard
does not carry this one.)

1. **Proposition first.** Before opening the source, write the one sentence you are
   citing it for. That sentence is what gets verified. If it changes, it is a new
   proposition: restart.
2. **Screen the whole text.** Search the retrieved document for the proposition's
   operative terms — at least three, including one **rigid designator** (a name the
   field cannot paraphrase away: TRIPS, the Trade Secrets Act, Art. 39(3), "fork in
   the road", a party name) and one truncated stem. If the proposition has no rigid
   designator, **write that down**; the verdict is weaker. A zero is not a verdict; a
   nonzero is not a pass. Zero on a descriptive word: try its synonym. A hit: go read
   the hit.
3. **Quote the carrying span.** Put the source's own words that carry the proposition
   into the entry, with a pinpoint. A pinpoint alone is not compliance. If nothing
   carries it, take one exit and name it, **in this order**: (i) retrieve a different
   document **in the same matter** — another opinion, order, decision or award, at
   another stage or date — and restart at 2; (ii) narrow the proposition to what the
   span does carry; (iii) find another source; (iv) drop it. If you cannot retrieve
   enough to look, the source is **unread**: say so and assert nothing from it.
4. **Cite the document, not the case.** Name the opinion, award or decision you
   actually read, and its date.
5. **Card every case:** what the dispute was about, the stage cited, and how it came
   out **on the point you cite** — "joined to the merits" and "undecided" are
   outcomes. Add what happened after **only where your proposition depends on
   finality.**
6. **Annotation claims are propositions.** Any relational or superlative claim about
   a source — strongest, closest, most on point — needs its own span, or is rewritten
   as your own judgement. **Labelling it as judgement does not make it true.**
7. **Record the rejects.** List what you screened and did not use, and why.

**Every entry carries four marks:**

```
P — the one sentence
Q — "the carrying span" [pinpoint]
D — document + date; what the dispute was about; stage; outcome on the point cited;
    [what happened after — only if the proposition depends on finality]
V — checked <date> at <where retrieved>; screened <term:count>;
    for every screened term with a nonzero count whose occurrences are NOT the source of Q,
    one clause in your own words saying what the source is discussing there;
    <exit taken, if any>
```

Worked V line: `screened: TRIPS 0; intellectual property 0; recognition 2 — both
occurrences concern recognition and enforcement of arbitral awards, not municipal
recognition of a protected asset.`

The spine, for holding in the head while writing: **Proposition, Screen, Span,
Document, Disposition, Annotation, Rejects.** Seven items, four marks.

## Where it binds (R7)

**It binds** every place a source is attached to a proposition in prose: the memos
and bibliography, the daily research records, session records,
`STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER.md`, and analyst and officer returns.

**It does not bind** the pipeline's structured `candidate_claims`, which the
2026-08-03 pre-ledger system governs. Two rules on one artifact is how the
`METHODOLOGY.md:56` confusion arose, and the council would not reproduce it.

## Why it exists (R1, R2)

Not because the previous rule's scope excluded the failing entries. The chairman
carried that framing into the session and it was **overturned**: limb (c) of the §VII
verification note is unscoped, so it covered these citations and **was not applied to
them**. This is a discipline failure, with limb (b)'s scoping — and a section headed
"*Backsourced* Annotated Bibliography" sitting over self-recruited entries — as a
drafting aggravator, not a defence.

The population is **1 verified, 2 strongly indicated, 0 excluded**. Only Vanda is
verified author-supplied. Absence from an expressly non-exhaustive list ("including
Tethyan Copper", "Siag, Burlington, A11Y, etc.") proves nothing — the chairman's
initial 3/3 claim was corrected on that ground.

## What it closes, and what it only makes visible (R6)

**Closed at the class level — a proposition whose operative term does not occur in
the cited document.** This is the whole of the Vanda failure, the whole of the H&H
trade-secret failure, and the subject-matter half of Bonnitcha. Under step 2 with a
rigid designator these do not survive to become entries: no span can exist, so no `Q`
can be written. Mechanical, cheap, and it fires before any expensive step.

**Made visible, not closed — the status class.** Right source, right passage, wrong
legal weight: joined to the merits, dicta, dissent, argument of counsel, vacated.
Item 5 puts "joined to the merits, not decided" on the page next to a proposition
claiming the doctrine was applied. **A second reader sees the contradiction; the rule
does not stop the writer producing it.**

**Made visible, not closed — the nonzero/homonym branch.** Amendment 2 forces the
referent into the record. It does not adjudicate the referent.

**Not closed, and named:** entailment (the rule's declared load); **annotation
vagueness**, the predicted compliance-and-defeat path, for which neither seat has a
mechanism; **narrow in the entry, wide in the body**; **aggregation**;
**apt-but-not-authority**; **docket-history recruitment** at item 5's finality field,
which gets no carrying span; **inaccessible and redacted sources**, where a zero
means nothing; and **self-judged sufficiency**, against which the only structural
break is a second reader.

The honest summary: **the rule closes one failure class outright, makes two
detectable by a third party, and leaves the judgement at the centre exactly where it
was.**

## Replayed against the five uses (R4)

The unit is *uses*, not *failures* — "failures" concealed that H&H is two uses with
two different fates.

- **H&H, trade-secret use** — dies at **step 2**. The rigid designator returns zero
  in the published Rule 48(4) excerpts.
- **Vanda** — dies at **step 2**, and the rule reaches it through **step 4** and exit
  (i): the pin cite moves to another document in the same matter. That is what the
  corrected entry did — `kim-memo.md:192-202`, now the 6 May 2021 opinion. (The
  screen the corrected entry actually records on the abandoned 2020 opinion is *trade
  secret, confidential, proprietary, disclosure* — all zero. The session record's own
  table writes this designator as "Trade Secrets Act"; the stem screened subsumes it,
  but the terms above are what the entry attests.)
- **Bonnitcha, subject-matter use** — dies at **step 2**. Zero for "intellectual
  property", "trade secret", "patent", "trademark", "TRIPS".
- **H&H, fork-in-the-road use** — **not caught.** Made third-party-detectable by item
  5, which is not the same thing.
- **Bonnitcha's superlative** — **not caught by item 6 at all.** Item 6 is a
  labelling rule, not a catch: the writer prefixes "in my judgement" and the claim
  passes while remaining false.

Three of five uses fall to a mechanical test. That is the number; do not round it up.

## Enforcement, in descending strength (R5)

Rules bind in this project when they are attached to an artifact that fails — and the
officer's correction stands: because this was a discipline failure, the remedy is
enforcement **and** a carrier, not a carrier instead of enforcement.

1. **Transcription.** A carrying span cannot be produced without opening the source at
   that place. Works in a word processor, with no repo, no CLI, no agent. The
   strongest mechanism in the set.
2. **Mark parity, countable by anyone.** Entry count against `V —` count,
   `P`/`Q`/`D`/`V` presence, `Q` carrying a quotation pair and a pinpoint token.
3. **Nonzero-referent parity** (Amendment 2) — the highest-value mechanical addition
   in the session. Every screened term with a nonzero count must appear either as the
   source of `Q` or in a referent clause. Set membership over one line, no judgement.
4. **Span-in-source on demand.** Any `Q` pastes back into the source's find field.
5. **Degenerate uniformity.** String equality across `V` lines and across `D` lines —
   the only check in the set that detects performance directly rather than inferring
   it.

**Weak, and named as weak:** the reject list is a signal, not a gate.

**Exhortation, and not dressed up:** step 1's ordering — no artifact records which of
two sentences was written first — and entailment.

`scripts/check_marks.py` carries part of tiers 2, 3 and 5. **Its module docstring is
the single statement of what it checks — read it there rather than trust a summary,
including this one.** Two things it says that matter here: coverage is conditional on
an entry using the four marks, and the CLI reports what was *exercised* on the files
you gave it, not what is implemented. Tier 1 needs no code and is the strongest thing
in the set; tier 4 needs the source document.

Per R9 the rule gains **no repository precondition**: it applies whether or not the
work is committed. The CI job is scoped to `lit-review/**` because that is where a
check can read; the rule is not scoped to it.

**The sentence the council does not get to avoid.** The enforcement surface for the
failure that actually occurred was Dr. Benavides's margin. It still is. This rule
makes her target smaller. It does not remove her from the loop, and any reading that
implies otherwise is overclaiming.

## When this becomes validated (R8)

One number: **`N changed / M screened`** — entries where the rule fired and the
citation actually changed (source dropped, proposition narrowed, pin cite moved to
another document in the same matter), over entries screened, with the reject count as
`M`.

Read it the officer's way, which inverts the naive reading: **a bibliography
reporting M > 0 and N = 0 is a positive detection of performance, not evidence of
clean work.** On the three known instances the true rate is at least 3/3. A first
report of 0/25 means the rule was complied with and never executed.
