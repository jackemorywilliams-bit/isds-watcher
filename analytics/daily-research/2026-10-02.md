### 7.6 Where this leaves the research question, in plain language for a reader with no context

**The question is in the same place it was this morning, and the project is in a better place. Those are
different things and I will not blur them.**

The research question asks where three conditions meet: intellectual property — including trade secrets
and clinical-trial data — asserted as a protected investment under a treaty; a government regulation or
a court decision challenged as the violation; and a live, argued dispute about whether the tribunal may
hear the case at all. The project's central finding has always been a **negative**: nothing like that
has ever been litigated in public. That is what makes this a study of a gap that might open rather than
of cases already decided, and **every conclusion downstream of it leans on that one sentence.**

That sentence carried the date **June 2026** and nobody had re-tested it in four months. **Today it was
re-tested and it survived.** Five searches in five different vocabularies returned 63 results, and not
one of them names a claim, a notice or an award in which pharmaceutical trade secrets or clinical data
are the asset being protected. **So the negative holds, and it now carries today's date instead of a
four-month-old one.** For the operator's faculty mentor, that is the practical outcome: the premise the
work rests on has been checked rather than assumed.

**What we must not pretend that means.** Nobody opened a single document today — our ability to fetch
pages was dead all day, on both of the tools that can do it, and we proved that on a neutral test page
before spending anything. A search engine returning nothing is a fact about the search engine. **And we
discovered something uncomfortable about our own instrument in the process, which is the day's real
intellectual product.** We have been routinely checking that the search tool *works* — that it answers
when asked. We have **never once** checked that it would *find* the thing we are looking for if the
thing existed. Those are different tests, the second is the one that matters for a negative finding,
and this council did not have the vocabulary to tell them apart until today. So the honest form of the
finding is now written into the permanent record as **"not found by an uncalibrated instrument"** — and
the test that would calibrate it is designed, written down, and will be fired first next time.

**And there is a reason to expect our instrument to be weakest exactly where this project looks.** A
patent is registered in a public register, so a fight about one gets reported. A trade secret is only a
trade secret while nobody knows it — and the literature this project relies on has no account of how a
company could even prove it owned one without giving it away. So the cases we are watching for are the
cases most likely to be settled quietly, arbitrated confidentially, or published with the important
parts blacked out. **Our detector is worst precisely where our question lives.** That is not a reason
to stop; it is a reason to stop treating one more quiet month as one more piece of evidence.
