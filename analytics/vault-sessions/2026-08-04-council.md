### 1.5 — The verdict, stated plainly

1. **A discipline failure, and it is the more serious finding.** Rows 2, 5 and 7 were inside
   the terms this seat wrote for itself and were not caught. Row 5 in particular — the
   homepage saying "never empty" while `README.md` says the opposite — sits in two files the
   08-03 sweep named by name. Under R1, a rule that covered the failing population and was
   not applied is not repaired by widening the rule.
2. **And a real scope defect, which R1's own logic says to fix by widening.** Rows 1, 8, 9
   and 10 are genuinely outside every clause. So is `HUMAN_REVIEW.md` (row 6). Widening is
   warranted for those.
3. **But the widening that is warranted is not "read more files."** The 08-03 sweep already
   tried "read every human-facing surface", and it produced a finding list that is now lost
   and a pass that missed the two loudest contradictions on the site. A per-deployment sweep
   over a growing surface is unfalsifiable: nothing says what it must cover, so nothing can
   say it failed.

   **The missing thing is an artifact, not a wider licence.** The seat maintains a register of
   *seats* (`agents/Agent Registry.md`) and a register of *changes*
   (`agents/Project Change Log.md`). It has never maintained a register of *claims*. Eight of
   the reviewer's ten are one fact stated twice and drifted. A per-claim index converts an
   open-ended reading task into a closed lookup: *before you edit this file, here is every
   other file that states the same fact.*

4. **The concrete rule I would put to the council**, phrased so it can fail:

   > **The claim map is part of this seat's owned memory, alongside the registry and the
   > change log. A claim is not changed until every file listed against it is changed in the
   > same change set. A change set that edits a file named in the claim map without touching
   > the map is a defect the archivist owns.**

   And, from §1.3, one narrower rule that would have caught the lost sweep:

   > **A cross-reference is a claim. A session that cites its own evidence to another note's
   > section must verify that the section exists on `main` before the record is closed.**

5. **One thing the complaint's framing gets wrong, and it should be said rather than
   flattered:** "active twice in the last two days" describes two sessions whose slices were
   pointed elsewhere. Session 1 audited agent-note currency, the flowchart manifest,
   `HANDOFF.md` and unmerged branches; Session 2 audited the Carrying-Span change set. Neither
   was a claim-consistency pass, and the method — "audit **one slice** per deployment" — makes
   it a near-certainty that any given surface is not the slice. That is a real limitation of
   the mandate's design, not an excuse for §1.2 and §1.3, which stand.
