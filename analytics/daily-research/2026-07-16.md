### 2A. UNCITRAL 59th Commission Session — ADOPTION CONFIRMED (MEDIUM confidence)

**Major advance.** A post-session civil-society analysis by Fernando Hernández Espino (Head of Trade and Investment Policy, Both ENDS, published by AFTINET — Australian Fair Trade and Investment Network) confirms that the 59th Commission session adopted two instruments:

> "The two new instruments adopted were the Statute of the Advisory Centre on International Investment Dispute Resolution and the Supplementary Provisions on the Conduct of Proceedings."

The article's title — "Analysis says UNCITRAL's ISDS reforms don't address fundamentals" — is dated July 2026 and characterises the adopted instruments as "UNCITRAL's recently adopted investor-State dispute settlement (ISDS) reforms." This is the first post-session source in the project record to confirm the adoption in named, specific terms.

**What was adopted:**
1. **Supplementary Provisions on the Conduct of Proceedings** (the A/CN.9/1246 instrument). The pre-session description characterised these as covering: evidence, bifurcation, interim measures, manifest lack of legal merit, security for costs, TPF disclosure, counterclaims, cost allocation. The AFTINET article snippet specifically notes that the Supplementary Provisions include "mandatory disclosure of third-party litigation funding, loser-pays principle on costs... and procedures to dismiss unmeritorious claims at an early stage" — consistent with the project's pre-session description of the draft.
2. **Statute of the Advisory Centre on International Investment Dispute Resolution** — consistent with the July 6 finding that the Statute had been adopted "in principle" at the 57th session and was being finalized at the 59th.

**What was NOT adopted (consistent with project record):**
- The MIC/MIIR statutes (draft statutes for permanent first-tier and appellate mechanisms) were not adopted at this session; the MIIR as the named vehicle for legacy-BIT coverage was not adopted.
- The Denial of Benefits provision (Draft Provision 9 / WP.253) was not in the A/CN.9/1246 package and remains unadopted.

**Ring 3 implications:** None. For pharmaceutical trade-secret claims under the China-Germany BIT (2003), the pre-ICSID gate architecture — Protocol Article 9(a) administrative exhaustion; Article 9(c) concurrent-proceedings bar; Philip Morris abuse-of-rights — is unchanged by the Supplementary Provisions adoption. The natural-person nationality planning gap (Jason Yu Song scenario) is unaddressed. The adopted instruments improve ISDS procedure (TPF disclosure, cost allocation) but do not alter jurisdictional or admissibility doctrine.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. The AFTINET source is a civil-society advocacy organisation (not IISD ITN, EJIL:Talk, or an authoritative law firm); it is specifically biased toward criticising the inadequacy of the reforms (so likely to describe what was adopted accurately, since understatement would undermine its argument). The specific naming of two instruments is more credible than a vague "reforms adopted" characterisation. IISD ITN and EJIL:Talk post-session analysis is not yet indexed (as of today, Day 6 post-session). Upgrading T5-UNCITRAL from "no confirmed outcome" to "adoption confirmed at MEDIUM confidence."

Sources: [(AFTINET, "Analysis says UNCITRAL's ISDS reforms don't address fundamentals")](https://aftinet.org.au/analysis-uncitral-isds-reforms) [body 403-blocked; content from search-engine snippet]; [(UNCITRAL, A/CN.9/1246)](https://uncitral.un.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/a_cn9_1246_advance_copy.pdf) [403-blocked; pre-session]; [(UNCITRAL WG III page)](https://uncitral.un.org/en/working_groups/3/investor-state)

### 2C. Hela Schwarz Award Outcome — Minor Clarification; "Ad Article" Labeling Confirmed

**Minor clarification.** An entry at arbitration.org (arbitration.org/award/785) characterised the Hela Schwarz Award as: "the arbitral tribunal found that the respondent State committed one or more breaches of the applicable IIA but did not award monetary compensation or non-pecuniary relief to the claimant investor." This is potentially inconsistent with the project record (which characterises the case as "dismissed on jurisdiction and merits").

Today's search produced a more authoritative characterisation directly consistent with the project record: "the Tribunal dismissed all of Hela Schwarz's claims, worth over EUR90 million, on jurisdiction and merits grounds" — confirmed again via A&O Shearman's characterisation that the case represents a "landmark ICSID victory for the People's Republic of China." The arbitration.org entry appears to contain a database error or misclassification; the dismissal-on-jurisdiction-and-merits characterisation is confirmed from the more authoritative sources in the record and is upheld.

**"Ad Article" prefix confirmed.** Today's search surfaced the following snippet (from Hela Schwarz search results): "China objected to jurisdiction, arguing that domestic administrative review of the issues in the international legal proceedings is a compulsory precondition qualifying the Respondent's offer to arbitrate, based on **Ad Article 9(a) of the Protocol** and Article 26 of the ICSID Convention." A separate snippet confirms: "The Fourth Objection is based on **Ad Article 9(c) of the Protocol**..." The provisions are formally labeled "Ad Articles" — an "Ad Article" being an additional (supplementary) provision within the Protocol, as distinct from the BIT's main articles. This does not change the substance of the findings from July 11 (Art. 9(c)) and July 15 (Art. 9(a)).
