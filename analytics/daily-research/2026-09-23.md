### 4.3 What the research condition actually is

Stated as I would state it if the drafting were mine, which it is not:

> **Can this council, through a retrieval channel it actually possesses, put the operative words of a
> numbered NAFTA Chapter Eleven article in front of a seat — attributable to a named host and a named
> locator, in a form a seat can quote with a pinpoint?**

That condition has four elements and **content type is not one of them**: (i) a locator at a host the
relay may fetch; (ii) an `ok` row; (iii) **evidence inside the reduction that the retrieved bytes
contain the article's own operative words** — a readable span, never an inference from a title, a
byte length or a hash; (iv) enough of that span to quote.

### 4.4 The minimal instrument change, and the second limit that the PDF question is hiding

**Minimal change to meet (iii) for PDFs:** add a PDF branch to `excerpt_of` that extracts text with a
library (`pypdf` or `pdfminer.six`) and then falls through to the existing tag-strip, whitespace-
normalise, `find`-centre and 400-character-cap path unchanged. Roughly ten lines plus one entry in
`requirements.txt`. Everything the council cares about survives untouched: the no-body rule, the
excerpt cap, the sha256, the allowlist, `polite_get`, the user agent. **What travels is still the
reduction.**

**And the cheap version does not work — I name it so nobody reaches for it.** A zero-dependency
`re.search(b"1110", content)` over raw PDF bytes will fail on most real PDFs, because page text lives
inside Flate-compressed streams; it would return either nothing or noise, and noise here would be
worse than nothing, because it would be *a non-empty excerpt at an allowlisted host* and would
therefore satisfy the condition's letter.

**Now the limit that matters more, and it binds HTML rows too.** `EXCERPT_CHARS = 400`
(`scripts/fetch_relay.py:42`). **Even a perfect `text/html` row at a host serving NAFTA Chapter Eleven
returns at most 400 characters, centred on `find`.** NAFTA Article 1110(1), with its chapeau and its
four lettered subparagraphs, is longer than that. **So under the instrument as it stands, no single row
of any content type can deliver the full operative text of the article.** The consequence is sharp and
I want it on the record before any closure is recorded: **the gap as currently drafted is satisfiable
by a 400-character window, and closing it would therefore establish that this council can reach a page,
not that it can read an article.** If what the council wants to establish is the second thing, the
current condition does not test it at any content type, and raising the cap runs into the standing
"what travels is the reduction, never the document" rule — at some length a reduction becomes a copy,
and where that line sits is an operator question, not mine.

**A third artefact, smaller but in the same family.** `excerpt_of` also returns `""` when `find` is
supplied and does not match (`scripts/fetch_relay.py:132-133`). So a page that genuinely *is* NAFTA
Chapter Eleven, fetched with `find="Article 1110"`, returns an **empty excerpt** — and thus fails the
condition's "non-empty excerpt" limb — if the page happens to render `ARTICLE 1110` in a heading and
`Article 1110.` in the body, or renders the number inside a table cell our tag-stripper separates with
a space. **Three of the amended condition's limbs — content type, non-empty excerpt, and implicitly the
span length — are properties of `excerpt_of` rather than properties of the world.**
