**Reconciliation attempt:** The two signals are potentially consistent rather than flatly contradictory. Under a permissive "may request" structure (Signal A — today's AI synthesis), China can invoke the provision and, once invoked, the investor must comply — so from the investor's perspective, "you may need to exhaust" (practical risk) is accurate. Gáspár-Szilágyi may be describing the practical risk rather than the treaty text's mandatory/permissive character. The Manjiao Chi JIDS article ("Administrative Review Provisions in Chinese Investment Treaties," JIDS vol. 12(1), pp. 125–150, 2021) — a distinct article from the "Gilding the Lily in Disguise" article also in JIDS vol. 12(1) — examines this distinction across Chinese BITs and finds "nearly half" contain such provisions, documenting "an emerging trend of abandoning administrative review in China's recent IIA-making." The China-France BIT (2007) may fall in the older cohort (mandatory) or the newer cohort (permissive "may require"). [(JIDS, Oxford Academic, 12(1):125)](https://academic.oup.com/jids/article-abstract/12/1/125/6064838).

**Data gap assessment:** The China-France BIT Protocol formulation remains UNCONFIRMED.
