### 2B. Hela Schwarz v. China — Article 9(a) Administrative-Exhaustion Pre-Condition: NEW FINDING

Today's targeted research on the Hela Schwarz Award has surfaced a significant new element not previously in the project record.

**Context:** The project has documented the concurrent-proceedings/court-withdrawal bar in Protocol Article 9(c) of the China-Germany BIT (2003) as the operative Ring 3 jurisdictional mechanism in Hela Schwarz. Today's searches surface a second sub-provision — **Article 9(a) of the Protocol** — that China raised as a separate and distinct jurisdictional bar in the same case.

**Article 9(a) — Text reconstructed from JIDS 2020 article snippets (MEDIUM confidence):** Protocol Article 9(a) requires that an investor of the Federal Republic of Germany "may submit a dispute for arbitration only if the investor has referred the issue to an administrative review procedure according to Chinese law." China argued this constitutes "a compulsory precondition qualifying the Respondent's offer to arbitrate." The specific characterization: "domestic administrative review of the issues averred in the international legal proceedings is a compulsory precondition." China further argued the Claimant "did not even allege in the Memorial to have complied with the administrative review requirement."

**Relationship between 9(a) and 9(c):** Both are Protocol provisions. Article 9(a) operates *before* ICSID filing: the investor must exhaust Chinese administrative review first. Article 9(c) operates *concurrently*: if the investor has filed in Chinese courts, those proceedings must be withdrawn. Together, they create a two-track gate architecture: (1) administrative review exhaustion (9(a) — first), then (2) no concurrent Chinese court proceedings (9(c) — ongoing).

**What the Award addressed (MEDIUM confidence, from search-engine summaries):** The Award (December 10, 2025) expressly addressed China's "third objection to jurisdiction" concerning Article 9(a)'s administrative review requirement. The Award also addressed the Article 9(c) concurrent-proceedings objection. Whether the tribunal actually *upheld* the Article 9(a) bar — as distinct from the 9(c) bar — is **unknown**: the Award PDF remains 403-blocked across all retrieval routes (italaw, icsidfiles, jusmundi). The CDR News headline ("court withdrawal conundrum") and A&O Shearman's coverage emphasised the concurrent-proceedings issue, suggesting Article 9(c) was the headline jurisdictional finding. Whether Article 9(a) was independently sustained, or merely addressed and rejected, cannot be determined from accessible sources.

**Implication for the pharmaceutical China-Germany BIT scenario:** Even if only Article 9(c) was upheld, the Article 9(a) administrative review requirement is part of the Protocol's pre-ICSID gate architecture and would be raised as a jurisdictional bar in any future claim under the China-Germany BIT. A European pharmaceutical investor challenging a Chinese NMPA pharmaceutical data exclusivity decision would face: (a) the requirement to first exhaust Chinese administrative review of the NMPA decision (Article 9(a)) — the natural first step in any regulatory challenge; (b) the requirement that any Chinese court proceedings on the same issue be withdrawn before ICSID filing (Article 9(c)). The administrative exhaustion requirement under 9(a) is itself the kind of domestic proceeding that Article 9(c) might subsequently bar, creating a structural conflict in the investor's pre-filing strategy.
