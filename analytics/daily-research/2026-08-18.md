### 3. The same expert report is rendered twice on this page, in two different forms

Comparing this case's four consecutive windows shows something neither window shows alone. The
2026-08-15 window carries `Expert Report of “Chip” Gordon C. Gill` **bare**. Today's window carries
`CER-05, Expert Report of “Chip” Gordon C. Gill` **designator-prefixed**. Same expert, same page, two
renderings — and the 2026-08-15 window also carries `Expert Report of Nigel Bankes` and
`Expert Report of Paul Sharp, Pricewaterhouse Coopers LLP (Public Version)` in the same bare form.

This matters because the relay centres its window on the **first** case-insensitive match. A seat
anchoring on the bare title `Expert Report of “Chip” Gordon C. Gill` would land in the earlier region
and would never see the `CER-05` entry — and would have no way of knowing there was a second one.
**On this page a bare title is not a unique locator; the designator prefix is.** That is the exact
converse of yesterday's lesson, which was that a designator like `Procedural Order No. 4` is a homonym
*across cases* and needs the case to disambiguate it. Put together: **across cases the designator is
ambiguous and the title disambiguates; within a page the title is ambiguous and the designator
disambiguates. Neither is a rigid designator on its own.** This is the day's optimization item and it
is developed in Part V.
