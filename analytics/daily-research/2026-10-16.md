## 11. Method note

**What produced today's findings was executing a pointer rather than reading around it.** The chair handed me three line numbers and a quotation. The quotations were verbatim; the addresses were empty. I found that only because the first thing I did was `awk 'NR==N'` on each cited line instead of grepping for the quoted text and assuming the pointer agreed — and the agreement was the thing in question.

Four formulation lessons, for the self-training record:

1. **For a claim about a file's own structure, enumerate with a script and print the target, not the match.** `grep -n` on the quoted text would have found `:438` and told me nothing was wrong. Printing `lines[tgt-1]` is what exposed it. A screen that only looks where it expects content cannot find an absence.
2. **Put the positive and negative control in the same command as the count.** Three times today a control fired in the same output as the number it governed, and twice the number was wrong. A control in a separate call is a control you can forget to read.

Had I computed the register population first I would have published a true number with a false scope.
4. **Re-derive a cited figure from the artefact before building on it.** Re-deriving the six 2026-10-15 deltas was meant to be a verification step; it is what exposed that the three intervals are baseline artefacts. The verification found the finding — which is `optimization-log.md:119`'s observation in another instance: *"every screen run today returned something the seat running it did not expect."*
