### A. CPTPP as Alternative Pharmaceutical IP ISDS Vehicle — Thread T03

#### A.1 Pharmaceutical data exclusivity provisions: suspended

The CPTPP suspended a substantial block of pharmaceutical IP obligations that appeared in the original TPP text. Specifically, the provisions on **pharmaceutical test data protection (data exclusivity)** — derived from what would have been Article 18.50 of the TPP — were among the provisions suspended when the US withdrew and the eleven remaining parties concluded the CPTPP protocol in March 2018. Australian Government DFAT confirms: "a number of intellectual property provisions from the original TPP were suspended, including certain provisions relating to pharmaceutical products (including biologics), copyright and patents." This suspension covers market exclusivity for pharmaceuticals, patent term extension for regulatory delay, patent term adjustment, and — critically — **protection of undisclosed test or other data** for pharmaceutical products. [(DFAT, CPTPP Intellectual Property outcomes)](https://www.dfat.gov.au/trade/agreements/in-force/cptpp/outcomes-documents/Pages/cptpp-intellectual-property); [(Sterne Kessler, "Trans-Pacific Partnership Lost Important IP Provisions")](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/trans-pacific-partnership-lost-important-ip-provisions/)

**Consequence:** CPTPP does not impose any treaty obligation on member states to protect undisclosed pharmaceutical test data (clinical trial data). A CPTPP member state that permits a generic applicant to rely on a pharmaceutical originator's clinical trial data for regulatory approval is not breaching any Chapter 18 obligation. There is therefore **no Chapter 18 hook for a three-ring ISDS pharmaceutical data claim** under CPTPP.

#### A.2 Chapter 9 residual pathway: narrow and untested for pharmaceutical data

Even if Chapter 18 does not directly provide an ISDS hook, an investor holding clinical trial data could in principle argue that state measures deprive it of an investment protected under Chapter 9 (Investment), specifically under the FET or expropriation standards. However: (1) Chapter 18 obligations cannot be directly enforced via ISDS — only Chapter 9 Section A standards can be submitted to ISDS; (2) with the data exclusivity obligation suspended, a state has no treaty duty to create the entitlement in the first place, making it harder to characterize its absence or removal as an ISDS-actionable measure; (3) no pharmaceutical IP ISDS case under CPTPP has been filed. The two known CPTPP ISDS cases are CDPQ v. Mexico (pension/infrastructure, November 2023, suspended by agreement) and Almaden Minerals v. Mexico (mining). Neither involves pharmaceutical IP. [(Kluwer Arbitration Blog, "CPTPP and ISDS: Three Years On", 2021)](https://arbitrationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/2021/08/12/cptpp-and-isds-three-years-on/); [UNCTAD ISDS Navigator](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-dispute-settlement)

#### A.3 UK CPTPP accession (December 2024)

The United Kingdom formally joined CPTPP on 15 December 2024. This adds UK pharmaceutical investors to the pool of potential ISDS claimants against CPTPP member states (and vice versa), but does not restore the suspended pharmaceutical data provisions. The UK negotiated IP side letters allowing derogation from certain patent obligations pending international harmonisation. ISDS between the UK and Australia and New Zealand is excluded under bilateral FTA commitments. [(Watson Farley & Williams, "UK Accession to CPTPP Part 1")](https://www.wfw.com/articles/uk-accession-to-the-cptpp-part-1-benefits-for-investors-and-traders/); [(Daily Jus, "UK Accession to CPTPP: Examining Key Provisions and ISDS Impact", April 2024)](https://dailyjus.com/world/2024/04/the-united-kingdoms-accession-to-the-cptpp-examining-key-provisions-and-isds-impact)

**T03 assessment:** CPTPP does not provide a viable direct pathway for pharmaceutical clinical data ISDS claims. The relevant data exclusivity obligations were suspended and cannot be enforced via ISDS. A residual Chapter 9 route exists in theory, but requires the investor to establish that its clinical trial data constitutes a "covered investment" independently of any CPTPP Chapter 18 obligation — an unlitigated doctrinal question. Combined with yesterday's USMCA finding, the conclusion is: **neither the principal North American treaty framework nor the principal Asia-Pacific multilateral framework now provides a direct ISDS pathway for pharmaceutical clinical data exclusivity claims.** The only remaining routes are older bilateral BITs between pharmaceutical-IP-investor states and respondent states that have not modernised their ISDS provisions (pre-USMCA era BITs, EU member-state bilateral BITs where still operative), and the pharmaceutical investment is held in a qualifying vehicle.

### B. Data-as-Investment Scholarship: "Data in Dispute" (Jus Mundi, Dec 2024)

A December 2024 article by Javier Manuel Tordesillas Rodríguez and Nour Yazbeck Morell (Cuatrecasas) — "Data in Dispute: ISDS and the Future of Digital Investments" — provides contemporaneous scholarly framing directly relevant to the project's central question. The article identifies three conceptual pathways for asserting data assets as protected investments under ISDS: (a) **underlying contractual rights** dealing with data use (licenses, concessions); (b) **intellectual property rights over data** protected by copyright or trade secrets; and (c) **data as part of a business enterprise** where it is integral to the enterprise's assets. For pharmaceutical clinical trial data, pathway (b) is the most relevant. The article articulates the expropriation standard that would apply: to constitute an expropriation, a state measure must substantially deprive the investor of value, use, or control — with listed examples including "taking or seizing data, using or copying data without authorization, disclosing or sharing data without consent, or restricting or prohibiting data without justification." The pharmaceutical data exclusivity context maps directly onto the "disclosing or sharing data without consent" and "permitting reliance without authorization" scenarios. [(Daily Jus, "Data in Dispute: ISDS and the Future of Digital Investments", 3 Dec 2024)](https://dailyjus.com/world/2024/12/data-in-dispute-isds-and-the-future-of-digital-investments)

**Calibration note:** This article was located via web search summary and not fully read (the URL resolves to a Daily Jus page that requires authentication). The article's framework and authorship are confirmed from search summaries; the specific quote on expropriation standards is derived from the search result excerpt. No holding or verbatim claim is stated beyond what the search summary reports. The article provides scholarly structure for the trade-secret/clinical-data problem but does not report any decided cases.

### C. UNCITRAL WG III Draft Provision 17 — Denial of Benefits Reform Trajectory (Thread T05)

The ITA Roundtable at the September 2024 UNCITRAL WG III session focused on **Draft Provision 17** of Working Paper A/CN.9/WG.III/WP.244 — the denial of benefits clause. The draft allows states to deny treaty benefits to: investors owned/controlled by a non-contracting-party entity, shell companies without genuine economic activity, sanctioned entities, or investors misusing the treaty. [(Kluwer Arbitration Blog, "ITA Roundtable at UNCITRAL Working Group III: Draft Provision 17")](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/arbitration-blog/ita-roundtable-at-uncitral-working-group-iii-draft-provision-17-and-the-denial-of-benefits-clause/); [(Mondaq report)](https://www.mondaq.com/austria/arbitration-dispute-resolution/1560098/ita-roundtable-at-uncitral-working-group-iii-draft-provision-17-and-the-denial-of-benefits-clause)

At the **April 2025 session** (the most recent session of the WG), procedural disputes emerged, and denial-of-benefits reform faced challenges to remaining on the reform agenda. The risk flagged in commentary: Draft Provision 17 may be **"reframed to merely codify existing practice"** rather than achieving any substantive extension of the doctrine. [(Columbia CCSI, "ISDS Reform Without Remedy: Report from the 51st Session")](https://ccsi.columbia.edu/news/part-i-isds-reform-without-remedy-l-report-51st-session-uncitral-wgiii)

**Significance for Thread T05:** If Draft Provision 17 merely codifies existing DOB clause practice, it does not close the natural-person nationality-planning gap identified in Jason Yu Song (PCA 2019-39, January 2025). Existing DOB clauses are structured around corporate entities; the gap is the absence of an equivalent mechanism for natural persons who acquire citizenship to access treaty protection. Without a substantive reform, that gap persists. This matters for the project because the Philip Morris / treaty-restructuring scenario (critical-date doctrine) remains the only established Ring 3 mechanism in the seed cases, and the Jason Yu Song case shows that DOB clauses as currently drafted cannot reliably replicate its effect for natural persons. A frustrated WG III reform process therefore leaves the Ring 3 doctrine architecture unchanged.
