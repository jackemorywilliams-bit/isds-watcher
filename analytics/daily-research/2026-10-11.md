**What I found when I stopped restating the prescription and read the collector.** This council has
told the operator for several days that `21d` is the correct look-back and `28d` from 2026-10-20.
The arithmetic is right. The implied retrieval reach is not, and the difference is material:

- `parse_since` (`src/main.py:177-188`) turns `21d` into a UTC cutoff, and `src.fetch(since)`
  (`src/main.py:421`) hands that cutoff to each source. **Every source but one then fetches its
  current listing and discards what is older.** `src/sources/icsid.py:45` calls
  `fetch_html(BASE_URL)` — the live news-events page — and filters at `:59-66`. `italaw.py:99`,
  `pca_press.py:67`, `gdelt.py:77`, `google_alerts.py:97` and `bing_news.py:68` do the same thing.
  **A wider cutoff cannot make any of those pages carry entries they no longer list.**
- **`iareporter_headlines` ignores `--since` entirely.** It fetches the homepage and returns
  everything, with the comment *"we do not date-filter here"* (`src/sources/iareporter_headlines.py:35-51`).
  The `since` value appears only in its log line.
- **`gmail_scholar` is the only source that asks its source for history.** It issues a genuine
  server-side query — `conn.search(None, "SINCE", date_str, "FROM", sender)`
  (`src/sources/gmail_scholar.py:236-240`) — against a mailbox that retains messages. Widening the
  window genuinely reaches back **there and nowhere else**.
- Dedup does not stand in the way: items are skipped only when `state.is_seen(source, source_id)`
  is true (`src/main.py:433`), and the gap's items were never seen, so a later run is not blocked
  by the seen-state.

**So the honest statement, which differs from the one escalated yesterday.** Widening the window is
still the right thing to do and still buys real recovery — for the dated archive-style pages
(ICSID, italaw, PCA, UNCTAD), a `21d` cutoff keeps gap-dated entries that a `7d` cutoff would throw
away, and for the Scholar mailbox it reaches back properly. But what a wide cutoff **cannot** do is
reach content that has scrolled off a live listing. **Rolloff, not Monday's run, is what destroys
the window, and it is doing so every day.** I record this as a correction to my own council's
standing escalation and I weaken one of its sentences in Part 6 accordingly.
