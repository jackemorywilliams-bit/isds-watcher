## 4. B4 — THE DRAFTING QUESTION

### 4.1 The answer

**The PDF exclusion in the amended §6.2 is an INSTRUMENT condition, not a research condition.** It
does not describe anything the research question needs. It describes one `if` statement in our reducer.

### 4.2 Three grounds, the second of which corrects the premise of your own §1.6(3)

**(a) The exclusion's stated reason is instrumental on its face.** `excerpt_of`, at
`scripts/fetch_relay.py:124-125`, reads `if "html" not in content_type and "text" not in content_type:
return ""`. The condition's content-type limb tracks that branch exactly. **A PDF served from an
allowlisted host, carrying NAFTA Article 1110 in its pages, would answer every research purpose of the
gap completely** — the gap asks whether this council can obtain the chapter's operative text from a
host it is permitted to fetch, and a treaty does not become less obtained for arriving as a PDF. The
format is a property of our renderer, not of the answer.

**(b) The premise that the instrument only gets PDFs is TRUE AT TWO HOSTS AND FALSE OF THE
INSTRUMENT, and the arithmetic is against the agenda's framing.** I censused all **568 rows** in
`analytics/fetch-results/`. On `ok` rows, excluding the `example.com` control:

| Host | `ok` rows | Content types on `ok` |
|---|---|---|
| `icsid.worldbank.org` | 250 | `text/html` **250** |
| `uncitral.un.org` | 66 | `text/html` 65, `application/pdf` 1 |
| `investmentpolicy.unctad.org` | 15 | `text/html` **15** |
| `pca-cpa.org` | 14 | `text/html` **14** |
| `undocs.org` | 8 | `text/html` **8** |
| `www.italaw.com` | 6 | `application/pdf` 6 |
| `digitallibrary.un.org` | 2 | `text/plain` 2 |
| `icsidfiles.worldbank.org` | 2 | `application/pdf` 2 |
| `www.iisd.org` | 2 | `text/html` 1, `application/pdf` 1 |
| `www.pca-cpa.org` | 1 | `text/html` 1 |

**355 of 366 non-control `ok` rows are `text/html` or `text/plain`. This relay is not a PDF-only
instrument; it is overwhelmingly an HTML instrument.** Your §1.6(3) says *"at the two hosts we have
measured most, the only content type the instrument can actually obtain is exactly the one the closure
condition excludes."* **The clause "the two hosts we have measured most" is not right and the error
runs against the agenda's own conclusion.** By row count the two hosts we have measured most are
`icsid.worldbank.org` (251 rows) and `uncitral.un.org` (66), and both serve this relay HTML almost
exclusively. `www.italaw.com` (20 rows) and `www.iisd.org` (24) are the hosts where the *signature*
was found, not the hosts most measured. **The tension you identified is real, but it is local to those
two hosts and it is a statement about how they treat this user agent — not about our reducer's reach.**
So the PDF exclusion is not forced on us by the instrument's general capability. It is forced by one
branch in one function, which makes it an instrument condition in the narrow and fixable sense.

**(c) The exclusion does work the condition does not acknowledge.** As drafted, the content-type limb
silently performs the reducer's job: it converts *"our renderer returns an empty string for this byte
stream"* into *"this locator does not satisfy the research question."* **That is the same move as
converting a 403 into an absence** — the move `0922-B4` was sustained on, one layer down the stack. A
403 is the origin's answer, a PDF is the origin's answer too, and in both cases the condition is
recording our own limit as a property of the source. I do not think the drafting intended it and I do
not raise it as a gotcha; I raise it because the council has already named the pattern and this is an
instance of it that our own rule did not catch.

### 4.3 What the research condition actually is

Stated as I would state it if the drafting were mine, which it is not:

> **Can this council, through a retrieval channel it actually possesses, put the operative words of a
> numbered NAFTA Chapter Eleven article in front of a seat — attributable to a named host and a named
> locator, in a form a seat can quote with a pinpoint?**

That condition has four elements and **content type is not one of them**: (i) a locator at a host the
relay may fetch; (ii) an `ok` row; (iii) **evidence inside the reduction that the retrieved bytes
contain the article's own operative words** — a readable span, never an inference from a title, a
byte length or a hash; (iv) enough of that span to quote.

### 4.4 The minimal instrument change, and the second limit that the PDF question is hiding

**Minimal change to meet (iii) for PDFs:** add a PDF branch to `excerpt_of` that extracts text with a
library (`pypdf` or `pdfminer.six`) and then falls through to the existing tag-strip, whitespace-
normalise, `find`-centre and 400-character-cap path unchanged. Roughly ten lines plus one entry in
`requirements.txt`. Everything the council cares about survives untouched: the no-body rule, the
excerpt cap, the sha256, the allowlist, `polite_get`, the user agent. **What travels is still the
reduction.**

**And the cheap version does not work — I name it so nobody reaches for it.** A zero-dependency
`re.search(b"1110", content)` over raw PDF bytes will fail on most real PDFs, because page text lives
inside Flate-compressed streams; it would return either nothing or noise, and noise here would be
worse than nothing, because it would be *a non-empty excerpt at an allowlisted host* and would
therefore satisfy the condition's letter.

**Now the limit that matters more, and it binds HTML rows too.** `EXCERPT_CHARS = 400`
(`scripts/fetch_relay.py:42`). **Even a perfect `text/html` row at a host serving NAFTA Chapter Eleven
returns at most 400 characters, centred on `find`.** NAFTA Article 1110(1), with its chapeau and its
four lettered subparagraphs, is longer than that. **So under the instrument as it stands, no single row
of any content type can deliver the full operative text of the article.** The consequence is sharp and
I want it on the record before any closure is recorded: **the gap as currently drafted is satisfiable
by a 400-character window, and closing it would therefore establish that this council can reach a page,
not that it can read an article.** If what the council wants to establish is the second thing, the
current condition does not test it at any content type, and raising the cap runs into the standing
"what travels is the reduction, never the document" rule — at some length a reduction becomes a copy,
and where that line sits is an operator question, not mine.

**A third artefact, smaller but in the same family.** `excerpt_of` also returns `""` when `find` is
supplied and does not match (`scripts/fetch_relay.py:132-133`). So a page that genuinely *is* NAFTA
Chapter Eleven, fetched with `find="Article 1110"`, returns an **empty excerpt** — and thus fails the
condition's "non-empty excerpt" limb — if the page happens to render `ARTICLE 1110` in a heading and
`Article 1110.` in the body, or renders the number inside a table cell our tag-stripper separates with
a space. **Three of the amended condition's limbs — content type, non-empty excerpt, and implicitly the
span length — are properties of `excerpt_of` rather than properties of the world.**

### 4.5 RECOMMENDATION (you rule; I do not)

1. **Rule the PDF exclusion an INSTRUMENT condition on the record**, with §4.2(b)'s census, so that no
   future seat reads it as a finding that PDFs are worse evidence.
2. **Do not drop the exclusion while the reducer cannot read a PDF.** It is doing correct work for the
   wrong stated reason: today, a PDF row genuinely yields no readable evidence, and dropping the limb
   without fixing `excerpt_of` would permit closure on a row whose excerpt is empty. **Keep the limb,
   re-caption it.** Something of the form *"`application/pdf` is insufficient **for so long as
   `excerpt_of` returns the empty string for it** — this is a limit of our reducer, not a judgement
   about the document"* records the same operational bar while naming the cause correctly, and it makes
   the exclusion expire automatically when the instrument is fixed.
3. **Open the reducer fix as its own escalation**, distinct from escalation 1. They are different
   levers on the same problem and **escalation 1 remains strictly cheaper and should stay ahead of it**:
   one line in a set literal versus ten lines plus a dependency.
4. **One catch in escalation 1 that I think is not yet on the record.** Yesterday's §6.9(1) notes that
   `http://www.sice.oas.org/trade/nafta/chap-112.asp` fails `validate()` twice — `scheme_not_https` and

cannot tell you whether the filter is wrong.

would record: for a treaty provision, the productive query names the publishing institution, not the
provision** — `"NAFTA Secretariat" chapter eleven` would likely have found `can-mex-usa-sec.org` in
one call, where three provision-named queries found it incidentally.
