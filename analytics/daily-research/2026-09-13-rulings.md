## RULING 2 — THE TIER-P EXCERPT RULE: ANCHOR AT ¶ 1, TOOL-FREE

**Decision. Option B's first limb, with Option D as the narrow fallback.** Options A and C
are rejected. The `_extract_body` reference and the "after the caption block" step are
struck entirely.

### The amended rule, in the words that go into `SCHEMA.md` (replacing the bullet at :46-51)

> - **P (public primary)** — ICSID, PCA, italaw, Curia, BAILII, UN RIAA, WTO.
>   `text` is a verbatim excerpt **capped at 600 characters**, selected **by position,
>   never by content**: the 600 characters beginning at the first character of
>   **paragraph 1 of the document's own printed numbering** (¶ 1), running forward in
>   the document's printed reading order and across paragraph boundaries where ¶ 1 is
>   shorter than 600 characters. Where a document carries no printed paragraph
>   numbering, **the item is recorded at tier S** — caption and locator, no excerpt —
>   and no substitute anchor is chosen. The anchor is printed on the face of the
>   document: a second reader verifies the excerpt by looking, with no tool, no
>   extraction library and no HTML parse. **Amended 2026-09-13**; the prior rule keyed
>   to `src/enrich.py::_extract_body` "after the caption block" is struck — that
>   function has no caption step and never had one, and on a PDF it returns the file
>   header silently. A coder who picks the passage picks the passage that scores;
>   position selection is the anti-contamination rule that matters most.

### The contamination reasoning

The threat model is one person choosing, per item, the passage that makes the item score
the way he expects — and not noticing. ¶ 1 removes the choice completely: the anchor is a
printed ordinal, unique in the document, **identical for every item**, and
**format-independent**. It is the same rule for an HTML page and a PDF, which is why it
survives the analyst's measured failure that killed the old rule — 4,817,785 characters of
PDF file header returned for *Philip Morris v Australia*, 1,224,008 of compressed stream
for *Eli Lilly*, silently, with the two documents failing *differently*.

Being tool-free is not a convenience; it is the guarantee. A rule anchored to a tool
inherits that tool's bugs and version-to-version reading order, and can only be checked by
re-running it. A rule anchored to a printed numeral is checkable by a second reader with
nothing in hand.

- **A rejected.** The character-0 offset into an extracted text layer lands on the cover
  page — parties, tribunal, date — for nearly every ICSID and PCA award. That is the
  caption block the struck clause existed to skip: Option A preserves the exact bug the
  amendment is for, and pays for it with an undeclared dependency and a per-item tool name.
- **C rejected.** Zero contamination, but it makes the item text an artefact of one fetch
  at one moment, non-reproducible on re-derivation, and for a PDF production's honest
  answer today is the empty string or the title — so C silently collapses tier P into tier
  S while appearing not to. A rule whose failure is invisible is the failure we just fixed.
- **D rejected as the general rule, adopted as the narrow fallback.** As a general rule it
  moves the ICSID/PCA/italaw awards tier P exists for into a caption-only tier, thinning
  positives in precisely the category the research question is about. As the disposition
  for an *unnumbered* document it is the honest answer and it closes B's judgment-call
  escape hatch, which is B's only real defect.

**The cost is accepted and disclosed on the face of the record:** ¶ 1 of an award is
frequently procedural. The set therefore measures, for tier P, how the instrument handles
a document's opening statement of the dispute. That is a stated limitation of the
instrument's reach, not a hidden one.

## RULING 4 — BOTH SWITCHES ON, BOUNDED; THE TAIL AUDIT IS BUILT

### (a) `TRIAGE_ENABLED` — **ON**, capped at 100 calls per run

The configuration's own text says it is "small, recurring, and Emory's to authorise"; he
has now delegated it. At ~$0.0014/call over observed run sizes (median 14, max 80) that is
~$0.02 median and ~$0.11 worst observed — roughly $1–$6 a year at a weekly cadence. The
probe `E5_einarsson_lexical_brittle` is a documented HIGH-band item with `keyword_score` 0
that can never reach enrichment, so it can never reach classification, so the instrument
cannot see it at all. The external audit's point (3) names the lexical gate as the reason
the false-negative rate is unmeasurable. Paying $1 a year to unblock that is not a
judgement call that needed an escalation, and the council should not have sent it as one.

**Bounds, ruled:** hard cap of **100 triage calls per run** (largest observed run is 80).
Above the cap, triage the top 100 by lexical rank and record `triage_skipped`.
`meta.json` gains `triage_calls` and `triage_cost_usd`. Worst case ~$0.14/run.
**Disclosed:** this is a dated configuration change; the 16 archived runs are pre-triage
and any comparison across that boundary states it.
**Seat: systems-designer** (env var, meta.json fields, cap, test).

### (b) `V2_SHADOW_CALLS` — **ON at `sample:3`**, and the data is quarantined

The config gives two reasons for `off`. Reason (1), the zero-cost constraint, is lifted by
the delegation. **Reason (2) — the locked set is empty, so shadow data would be
uncalibrated — still holds, and I am not overruling it.**

I am ruling on a different ground the config did not consider. **All 328 V2 telemetry
records are `lexical_only`: the V2 ring contract's model call has never fired once.** That
is an *unexecuted path* asserting a contract — taxonomy entry 31 exactly. Three calls per
run establishes that the path executes at all, which is a fact about the code, not a
measurement of the world, and reason (2) does not reach it.

**Bound, and it is the whole point:** **no V2 shadow figure may be published, cited or
compared in any digest, memo, brief or site surface until the locked set produces a
calibration.** The lane is instrumented, not consulted. **Seat: systems-designer** (env
var, plus a guard test that no publication path reads a V2 shadow field).

### (c) The tail audit — **BUILT NOW.** Neither deferral ground survives.

**Ground 1 — "its per-call cost is unpriced because the costing table lived in the R2.1
record" — DOES NOT SURVIVE RULING 1.** The number was never only in R2.1:
`TRIAGE_COST_PER_CALL_USD = 0.0014` is a committed constant, and a tail-audit call is a
*classification* call whose real cost is derivable from the instrument's own telemetry —

thing the operator just told it to stop doing.

**Ground 2 — "its premise was unestablished because on the only healthy run with a tail the
tail went to the model, 6/6 `llm ok`" — DOES NOT SURVIVE ITS OWN EVIDENCE BASE.** One run
is not a premise; it is an anecdote, and it is the *positive* limb of the very proposition
the audit says is unmeasured. Worse, it is now stale by construction: with triage ON per
(a), the ranking gate is no longer purely lexical, so the composition of the tail changes
and the question becomes both different and answerable.

**Precondition, council-executable and already assigned:** **row C (SD-2,
`KEYWORD_AFTER_PROVIDER_ERROR`) lands first.** Until it does, a provider failure inside a
tail-audit call is recorded as `keyword_only_by_design` and the audit measures the outage

**The design, adopted as the council recorded it in row N. Seat: systems-designer.**

- **Stratification on `lexical_subtotal`, not rank** — A: `== 0`; B: `0 < s < 12`;
  C: `>= 12`. **N = 2 per stratum; `TAIL_AUDIT_N = 6`.** The stub note's warning that a

- **Sample from the un-enriched tail only** (rank > `ENRICH_TOP_N = 24`), under a

- **Method: paired within-item enriched-vs-unenriched re-classification.** This is the

- **Ledger: `analytics/tail_audit.jsonl`, append-only, NO candidate text.** Fields: run id,

- **Reporting surface: `scripts/telemetry_query.py` ONLY.** Never the digest, never the

  measurement of the instrument, not a finding about ISDS; publishing it to the recipient

- **Cost:** 6 classification calls per run, priced from the telemetry re-derivation, and

**1. The archive-pool rejection is SUSTAINED, and it is precedent.** The seat was right and the

## RULING — G-3: tier P is redefined by criterion, not extended by host list

*Given on the coordinator's referral of gap G-3, raised by the validation-record seat, which
declined to stretch the tier and left nine rows unassigned rather than guess.*

**1. Tier P extends to US federal and state judicial opinions — and the enumeration is the defect,
not the omission.** The seven host families are an artefact of the set's ISDS-only drafting; they are
a list of *places*, and tier P's actual job is a list of *things*. A host list will go stale again
the moment category 3 or 6 needs an IP office, a Canadian court or a national appellate registry.
Replace it:

> **P (public primary)** — the text of a public primary legal instrument (judgment, award, decision,
> order, or their annexes) **issued by a court, tribunal or treaty body and published by that body or
> by an authorised public repository**. Non-exhaustive: ICSID, PCA, italaw, Curia, BAILII, UN RIAA,
> WTO, and the official reporters and public dockets of US federal and state courts. **Authorship
> governs, not host:** anything authored by a commentator — Ferguson, Kim, IAReporter, law-firm or
> journal commentary — is tier C or tier S wherever it is hosted, and no hosting arrangement moves it
> into P.

**2. The basis is stronger for US opinions than for several families already named.** US judicial
opinions are government edicts and carry no copyright — the reasoning of *Banks v. Manchester* and
*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*. Tier P's most exposed members are in fact **BAILII and italaw**,
which impose their own terms of use independent of copyright. **Both citations are mine and are
unverified against a source in hand: the seat verifies them at retrieval and cites them by name until
it has, or the record carries the proposition without them.** The proposition does not depend on the
citations.

**3. The tier-S fallback does NOT do the work on its own, and I will not let it.** US federal
opinions are typically PDF and typically not paragraph-numbered. But a caption-only category-5 item
is *"Acme Corp. v. Beta LLC"* — contentless, and materially worse than a category-8 headline, which
at least carries a proposition. Routing the category the research question's domestic limb depends on
into a tier of bare captions would hollow it out exactly as Option D would have hollowed out tier P.

**The anchor generalises, without reopening the hole. Amending Ruling 2:**

> The excerpt begins at the first character of **the document's first printed structural ordinal**,
> determined in this fixed priority and no other: **(1)** paragraph 1 of the document's own printed
> paragraph numbering (¶ 1), where the document numbers paragraphs; **(2)** failing that, the first
> unit of the document's own first-level part numbering — Part I, or `1.`, or `A.`, whichever series
> the document itself uses at its first level; **(3)** failing both, the item is recorded at tier S,
> caption and locator, no excerpt, and no substitute anchor is chosen.

The priority is fixed by the document, not by the coder, so no choice is created: it is the same
guarantee as ¶ 1, one level coarser. It is printed, unique, ordinal, tool-free, verifiable by
looking. And it has a property ¶ 1 does not need but US opinions do — **anchoring at Part I
mechanically skips the caption, the counsel listing and the reporter's syllabus**, which is
reporter-authored and in Supreme Court practice expressly not part of the opinion. That is the
caption-block problem solved by the document's own structure instead of by anyone's judgment.

**4. The nine rows are unblocked now.** They are committed as **tier P**, with a per-row field
`anchor: undetermined — resolved at retrieval` rather than UNASSIGNED, because which limb governs is
a fact about the document and is knowable only with it in hand. A row may not be moved to tier S
before retrieval: falling to limb (3) is a finding, not a forecast.
