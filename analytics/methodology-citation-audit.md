## B. Spot-check of substantive claims attached to load-bearing citations

- **Paulsson → manifest-injustice standard (Ring 2 anchor).** Supported, not a topical drive-by. The memo attributes to Paulsson the rule that a state is responsible for its judiciary only on a showing of manifest injustice, and that mere error does not suffice. This faithfully states Paulsson's central thesis (denial of justice as a procedural failure; a "manifestly unjust judgment" as evidence of systemic deficiency, with an egregiousness threshold). Accurate attribution.

- **Brown et al. → few-shot in-context learning (the contextual-classifier stage).** Supported. This is the canonical GPT-3 paper that introduced the "few-shot learner" framing; citing it for "few-shot prompting improves task adherence without fine-tuning" is exactly on point, not a stretch.

- **Salini → the four-part "investment" test.** Supported. ARB/00/4 is the origin of the Salini criteria (contribution, duration, risk, contribution to host economy), as the memo states. The memo correctly hedges with "compare," reflecting that the criteria are contested/non-mandatory.

- **Seed-case dispositions (Philip Morris, Eli Lilly, Bridgestone).** All three dispositions described in the memo are accurate against the official records:
  - *Philip Morris*: inadmissible for abuse of right (corporate restructuring into a Hong Kong entity in the face of a reasonably foreseeable dispute) — confirmed; merits never reached.
  - *Eli Lilly*: dismissed on the merits; tribunal found no dramatic change in Canadian patent law and nothing arbitrary/discriminatory in the promise-utility doctrine under NAFTA Art. 1105 — confirmed.
  - *Bridgestone*: covered-investment/standing question resolved largely for claimants at the expedited-objections stage; denial-of-justice and FET claims dismissed on the merits — confirmed.

- **Linos & Carlson → most-similar-case / theory-informed sampling for legal writing.** Supported by the article's own abstract (qualitative case-selection methods imported into legal scholarship).

2. **Qualitative Report title and metadata (cosmetic precision).** Section X.A cites only the subtitle. 3. **Ferguson (optional completeness).** The Ferguson source is cited without journal/volume/page. If the intended reference is the published article, the full pin is: Ferguson, A., *Trade secrets at risk — the protection against expropriation of a trade secret*, 40 Arb. Int'l 337 (2024). (If "the Ferguson memo" is a separate project-internal document, disregard.)

None of these affects the memo's substantive arguments; (1) is a genuine error worth fixing before circulation, (2) and (3) are precision improvements.
