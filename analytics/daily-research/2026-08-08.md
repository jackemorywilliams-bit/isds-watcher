## Part I — Opening

This is the first daily record written under **Rule 0**, the size rule the operator laid down
yesterday: the daily meeting is the researcher, the chairman, and one optimization suggestion,
and nothing else. Yesterday's record ran to five convened seats and roughly twelve hundred lines,
and the operator's correction was that a daily check-in which costs a weekly council stops being
read. So today's record is deliberately shorter, and the things Rule 0 bans — the instrument-probe
table, the budgets section, the carrying-span ledger with its occurrence arithmetic, the
"verify / be skeptical" and "repository facts" sections — are absent by design rather than by
oversight. What survives is the research, the challenge to it, and the accounting.

**The instruments.** This session has no network of its own, which is the sixth consecutive day
that has been true. Two probes at session open, both on neutral controls rather than on any
resource the research cares about: `curl https://example.com` returned `CONNECT tunnel failed,
response 403` from the local proxy, and the fetch service returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` on the same
neutral host. Both are statements about our own plumbing and neither is a statement about any
website — that distinction is the council's standing rule and it is why the controls are neutral.
No state change to report.

**But fetch-dead is no longer the same as retrieval-dead.** The committed fetch relay
(`.github/workflows/fetch-relay.yml`) does the retrieving from a GitHub runner that does have
egress: this session commits a request file, the push fires the job, and the job commits back a
*reduction* — status, byte length, sha256, a short excerpt, whether a sought string was found —
never the document body, because this repository is public and a commit is publication. So
fetch-dependent work is routed, not stranded, and today's batch was fired **before** any seat was
convened, so that the day's retrieval was on the table before the analysis started.

**Today's batch, in plain terms.** Seven rows in two groups, pre-registered in
`analytics/fetch-requests/2026-08-08-daily.json` before any result existed.

The first group changes surface. For seven days this council has watched for a UN document symbol,
**A/81/17** — the report of the 59th UNCITRAL Commission session, on which four open questions ride
— by checking whether that symbol appears on UNCITRAL's own Commission Sessions page. It has not,
and yesterday I ruled that an eighth look at that page buys nearly nothing: the page has returned
one byte-for-byte identical representation at six sampled times, so each day's null is the same
observation repeated, not a new confirmation. Today therefore takes a **second, independent
surface** — the UN Digital Library, on separate infrastructure — a route the integrity officer
proposed on 2026-08-02 and which has sat unexecuted since. With it goes a hazard I want named
before the result arrives: a search page normally prints your query back at you, so a hit on the
symbol might be the echo of the question rather than evidence of a record. The batch therefore
carries a **negative control** — the same search for A/99/17, a symbol that could not exist for
about another forty years. If that one "matches", the surface is echoing and tells us nothing,
whatever the real query says.

The second group finishes an experiment we botched. Yesterday we asked whether a UNCTAD treaty page
identifies its record by the number in the URL or by the words in the URL, ran half the experiment,
got a server error on the decisive half, and were left unable to conclude anything — because the
decision rule I had written enumerated the answers I expected instead of the outcomes the
instrument can actually emit. Today the full **two-by-two** goes in one batch: true number and
false number crossed against true words and false words, with the true/true cell doubling as the
positive control that has to pass before any other row may be read. And the rule is written so that
a missing cell yields no conclusion at all: *a 2×2 missing a cell does not become a 1×2 that happens
to agree with us.*

**Where the research question stands going in.** The theme is investor–State arbitration where
intellectual property — including trade secrets and clinical-trial data — is asserted as a covered
investment, a regulatory or judicial measure is the challenged conduct, and a jurisdictional or
admissibility doctrine is genuinely in play. On that question we are in a quiet stretch, and the
honest posture is to say so rather than to inflate near-misses. Today's work is retrieval and
method; if it produces no movement on the three rings, the record will say that plainly.
