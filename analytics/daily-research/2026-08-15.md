### The one optimization item (Rule 0)

**A digest change is not evidence of anything until you know whether the host's digests are stable, and
this corpus can answer that programmatically — for free — but no seat has ever asked it.**

Yesterday's record reasoned that `uncitral.un.org/en/commission` holding a constant 832,338 bytes
across a sha256 change was "evidence against reading it as per-response digest instability on this
URL." Today's batch put two rows on one PCA page in one run and got **two different digests at
identical byte length**, which made the general form of that inference worth testing rather than
assuming. Derived over the whole of `analytics/fetch-results/*.json` — every group of two-or-more rows
on the same URL in the same run at constant byte length:

| host | within-run groups, digest-IDENTICAL | digest-DIVERGENT |
|---|---|---|
| `uncitral.un.org` | 9 | 0 |
| `example.com` | 8 | 0 |
| `icsid.worldbank.org` | 3 | 0 |
| `pca-cpa.org` | 0 | **2** |
| `investmentpolicy.unctad.org` | 0 | **5** |

**Digest stability is HOST-SPECIFIC, and on two of the five hosts we have ever double-sampled, a
constant-bytes/changed-digest signature is the host's normal behaviour rather than a signal.** The
consequence cuts both ways and I state both: it **corroborates** yesterday's UNCITRAL inference for
that host specifically (9 of 9 within-run groups identical), and it makes the **general** form of that
inference unsafe — on `pca-cpa.org` or `investmentpolicy.unctad.org` the same reasoning would have been
flatly wrong. The item is a **screen, not a feature**: before any seat reasons from a digest change,
derive that host's within-run stability from the corpus and state it. It costs one query and it is
zero-budget. **Nothing was built and nothing was applied**; this is an observation and a proposed
discipline, and the operator's PENDING list is untouched.
