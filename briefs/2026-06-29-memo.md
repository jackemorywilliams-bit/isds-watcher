## 1. This week's items against the research question

**Okuashvili v. Georgia (items 1 & 3).** This is a Ring-3-mechanics artefact only — there is no IP investment and no IP-linked measure, so it sits outside the seed triad. The underlying dispute concerns tobacco-business assets: the Svea Court of Appeal ruled that an SCC tribunal lacked jurisdiction to keep hearing an investor-state case against Georgia over the forced sale of a tobacco business, as the most-favoured-nation clause of the investment treaty did not enable the parties to substitute the designated arbitral institution, ICSID, with another. No patent, trademark, trade secret, or clinical data anywhere in it.

What is worth shelving for the abuse-of-right / treaty-shopping doctrine bank (T05) is the MFN-as-forum-substitution reasoning. The structural objection is the one Knieper's dissent and the Svea court both pressed: if the MFN provision were applied as the claimant sought, it would render meaningless the distinction between the preferred version of Article 8 in the 1991 Model UK BIT and the alternative version, which provides for general consent to international arbitration not limited to ICSID, depriving the expressly-chosen preferred version of all effect. That "render the express choice meaningless" logic is the same effet-utile move that powers abuse-of-right and critical-date analysis in the Ring 3 line — it is the doctrine bank's value here, not the facts.

**Critical correction — reconcile the posture before relying on it.** The chairman flagged items 2 & 3 as paywalled headlines to be reconciled, and the screening got the posture inverted. The IAReporter headline (item 3) reads as though a Swedish court *blessed* the MFN detour. The verifiable record shows the opposite at the appellate set-aside stage: on 12 November 2024 the Svea Court of Appeal annulled the Partial Award on Jurisdiction and Admissibility in Zaza Okuashvili v. Georgia for want of jurisdiction, holding that the claimant — a dual UK-Georgia national who could not invoke the BIT's ICSID provision because ICSID excludes dual-national claims against their own State — could not use the Georgia-UK BIT's MFN provision to import the Georgia-BLEU BIT's broader forum and standing rules. The court did, however, open the appellate channel: the Svea Court recognised the issues at stake and granted Mr. Okuashvili a right of appeal to the Swedish Supreme Court; if ultimately upheld, the decision would provide clarity on MFN clauses vis-à-vis procedural steps and jurisdiction in Swedish-seated arbitrations. The Supreme Court then took the case: the firm secured a declaratory judgment that the SCC tribunal lacked jurisdiction, and the Swedish Supreme Court has since granted leave to appeal in what is expected to be a landmark ruling on the scope of MFN clauses in investment treaties.

So the IAReporter "Supreme Court finds claimant *can* use the MFN detour" headline (item 3, undated, body not accessible) most plausibly reports the **Supreme Court's eventual merits ruling reversing the Svea Court** — a different decision from item 1 (the italaw Knieper dissent, which belongs to the 2022 partial award / set-aside layer). **Procedural posture for the record, three layers:** (a) SCC partial award on jurisdiction, 31 Aug 2022, majority upholds jurisdiction, Knieper dissents; (b) Svea Court of Appeal, 12 Nov 2024 (Case T 11278-22), annuls for want of jurisdiction; (c) Swedish Supreme Court grants leave and rules — the item-3 headline. *Caveat: I could not read the IAReporter body (paywalled) and the tool-use limit prevented a final dated confirmation of the Supreme Court judgment; the (c) characterisation is backsourced inference from the GBS/GAR leave-to-appeal note, to be confirmed.* Do not cite item 3 as a holding until the dated Supreme Court judgment is pinned.
