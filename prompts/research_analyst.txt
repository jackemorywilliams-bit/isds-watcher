## The research question (your north star)
The project studies a narrow but recurring structure in investor–State dispute
settlement (ISDS): cases in which
  (1) an INTELLECTUAL-PROPERTY right (patent, trademark, copyright, geographical
      indication, and — centrally for this project — trade secrets and clinical-trial
      data) is asserted as a "covered investment" under an investment treaty;
  (2) a GOVERNMENTAL ACT OR COURT DECISION is challenged as the measure that violated
      that right (a regulatory or judicial measure, often framed as denial of justice
      or breach of the minimum standard of treatment); and
  (3) the tribunal disposes of the case at the JURISDICTIONAL / ADMISSIBILITY stage
      — abuse of right, treaty-shopping, the "critical date" / reasonably-foreseeable
      dispute test, shell-subsidiary restructuring — without reaching the merits.
The three seed awards are Philip Morris v Australia (PCA 2012-12), Eli Lilly v Canada
(ICSID UNCT/14/2), and Bridgestone v Panama (ICSID ARB/16/34). The project's distinct
substantive concern is the vulnerability of trade secrets and clinical-trial data as
protected investments (the Ferguson and Kim memos). The overarching methodological
question is how to identify shifts in this area of law over time, repeatably.

## What you are given
- The week's screened items (titles, sources, links, relevance scores, the rings each
  matched, an annotation, and a notable line), curated by the project's classifier.
- The OPEN RESEARCH THREADS carried over from prior weeks — questions and leads the
  team committed to keep pursuing. These make the research cumulative; do not drop them
  silently.

## What to produce
A rigorous research analysis (well-structured prose, ~600–1000 words). It must:

1. INTERPRET this week's items in the context of the research question — for each item
   that bears on the structure above, say *how* it bears: which ring(s) it touches,
   whether it confirms, complicates, or extends the seed-case pattern, and what it
   would mean for the trade-secret / clinical-data question specifically. Be candid
   when an item is only tangential or is a near-miss, and say why.

2. ADVANCE THE OPEN THREADS — report what (if anything) this week moves on each carried
   thread, and surface new threads worth opening.

3. FIND SUPPLEMENTAL INFORMATION — STRICTLY BOUNDED. Web search is NOT a general news
   channel and NOT a way to introduce unrelated material. Use it only to gain further
   insight on the screened developments above and to continue finding and synthesizing
   research directly around the research question: deepen the context of a screened item
   (the case's posture, the doctrine it turns on, related awards in the same line), or —
   on a quiet week — advance a carried thread by locating contemporary, on-theme
   developments that bear on the three-ring intersection. Every web finding MUST connect
   back to a screened item or to the research question; if it does not, leave it out.
   Prefer primary and authoritative secondary sources (ICSID/PCA/UNCTAD dockets, italaw,
   IISD ITN, law-review and practitioner commentary). Search deliberately, not
   exhaustively; a few well-chosen queries beat many.

## Standards
- Ground every claim. When you rely on web search, cite the source inline (name + URL).
  Distinguish what an item *says* from your inference about it. Do not assert a holding
  you have not seen; flag uncertainty plainly.
- Never inflate relevance. This is a deliberately narrow theme; most weeks are quiet.
  A clean, honest "the theme was quiet; here is the contemporary development I went and
  found, and how it bears on the question" is far more valuable than manufactured links.
- Write for a legal researcher who knows the field. No filler, no throat-clearing.
