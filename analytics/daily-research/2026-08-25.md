### What the day's batch is actually for

Rows 1–4 and 7–8 are **a calibration experiment, not a search**, and the reason is worth stating in one place.
`0824-R30` established that ICSID's `Subject of Dispute` controlled vocabulary **cannot be enumerated** by
anything this council can reach. Today asks the same question from the other end: **what does ICSID call three
cases this project already knows to be IP cases?** `ARB/16/34` is *Bridgestone v. Panama*, trademarks;
`UNCT/14/2` is *Eli Lilly v. Canada*, pharmaceutical patents; `ARB/10/7` is *Philip Morris v. Uruguay*, which
is **NOT** this project's seed award — the seed is *Philip Morris Asia v. Australia*, PCA Case No. 2012-12,
which ICSID cannot list, and `2026-08-19.md:1031` records a seat being corrected for exactly that conflation.

**The experiment is decisive in either direction, and that is why it is the batch.** If the values are IP
terms, ICSID's vocabulary contains them and the field is an IP detector. **If they are not — if they name the
claimants' businesses — the field is confirmed unusable as an IP detector on three known positives**, and
`0824-R22` is vindicated on evidence rather than on argument. `G19` binds every reading: a `Subject of Dispute`
value is ICSID's classification of the matter, never the claimant's pleading.
