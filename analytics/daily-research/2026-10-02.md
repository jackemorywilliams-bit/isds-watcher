### 3.3 Branch hygiene is not one stale branch, it is roughly two hundred — and the obvious way to measure it is wrong

Yesterday's chair could not delete the merged session branch and recorded it honestly as *"untidiness,
not loss"* and *"a one-command cleanup for the operator."* **The scale is wrong by two orders of
magnitude, and I only found that because I went to check the one branch.**

At session open this repository has **207 remote heads**. **Two of them have open pull requests**
(#184 and #110). The remaining ~205 are leftovers of sessions whose output has already landed,
accumulated continuously since early August — `council/2026-08-06` through
`council/daily-2026-10-01`, the whole `vault/archivist-*` series, and the `feat/`, `fix/` and `docs/`
branches of completed work.

**And here is the part worth more than the number, because it is a trap I walked into and caught.**
The obvious way to ask "which of these are safe to delete" is `git branch -r --merged origin/main`.
It splits the 207 into **154 merged and 52 not merged**, and the 52 include
`council/daily-2026-09-22`, `council/daily-2026-09-25`, and nearly every `-addendum` branch of the
last three weeks. **Read naively, that says three weeks of this council's records never landed —
which is precisely the catastrophe this session exists to prevent, and it is false.**

I tested it on a case whose answer I already knew. `council/daily-2026-10-01-addendum` reports as
**not merged**: `git merge-base --is-ancestor` confirms its tip is **not an ancestor of
`origin/main`**. But its content *is* on `main` — `git show origin/main:analytics/daily-research/2026-10-01.md`
carries the `POST-MERGE ADDENDUM` heading exactly once. **The explanation is in the merge style:
`main`'s first-parent history shows `council: daily meeting 2026-10-01 (post-merge addendum) (#234)`,
the `(#NNN)` form GitHub writes on a SQUASH merge.** A squash merge replays the diff as a new commit,
so the branch tip is never an ancestor of `main`, and an ancestry test reports it as unmerged forever.

**So `--merged` / `--no-merged` measures ancestry and this council has been reading it as landing.**
They are different questions and in a squash-merging repository they give different answers. The safe
cleanup criterion is **pull-request state, not ancestry.** On that criterion ~205 branches have no
open pull request and are deletable; I am not deleting them, because bulk branch deletion is outside
anything this session was asked to do and it is the operator's call. **It is one command for him and
it is not one branch.**

This is the standing tool-status-versus-state rule in a new costume: *a tool's negative answer is a
fact about the tool's question, not about the world.* I very nearly wrote "52 branches of unmerged
work" into an escalation.
