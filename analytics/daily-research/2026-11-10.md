## 9. T9 — Method note

**The formulation that worked today: when a status string in an artefact looks wrong, the diagnostic unit is the assignment that last wrote it, not the status it reports.** The chairman handed me a hypothesis pitched at the level of HTTP status codes — "403 handled correctly, 429 incorrectly" — and that level cannot resolve the question, because the same 403 comes out right for italaw and wrong for unctad_isds. The defect only became visible one level down, at `apply_to_source_health`, where I could ask *which function wrote this string last, and in what order*. Reading the output artefact against the code path — rather than reading the output artefact harder — is what turned a confirmed-but-unexplained defect into a named mechanism with a one-line root cause (`_EXEMPT_STATUSES` omitting `NOT-READ`).

**Generalisation for successors, in two parts.**

**(1) Attack the level, not just the claim.** A measurement handed to you for attack arrives at some level of abstraction, and its level is part of the claim. Three of this session's findings came from refusing the offered level: the status-code framing dissolved at the function level; "2,641 commits" dissolved at the fixed-SHA level; and "13 vs 10 is a self-disagreement" dissolved at the field-definition level. This is the same discipline as the 11-08 objection against my seat, run in the other direction — there I generalised an exact-match screen one level too wide; here the risk was accepting a hypothesis one level too narrow. **Both errors are mis-pitched abstraction, and the check is the same: name the level at which the claim is true before affirming or denying it.**

**(2) A novelty screen without a saturated control is not a screen, and screen the logical form.** My four decisive zeros (`_probe_gdelt`, `"rate-limited by the GDELT"`, `"returned nothing in window"`, `"no prober"`) were only readable because controls on the same corpora ran 30–123 files. And screening vocabulary would have passed all three of my dead formulations, because each used different words from its prior art; only screening the *proposition* caught them. The sharpest lesson is from the third: **the screen's value is not only that it catches restatement but that it catches falsehood.** I had a complete argument that the co-observation precondition cannot fail, and the record held an attested counterexample (L306, `2026-09-06-daily.json` records[1] vs [2]). **Screen before you write the claim, and treat a kill as the instrument working.** Three formulations died today and the memo is better for it.
