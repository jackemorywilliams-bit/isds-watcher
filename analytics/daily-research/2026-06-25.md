### A. Thread T01 — NAFTA Article 1121 Waiver Doctrine: Doctrinal Depth

#### A.1 What Article 1121 actually requires

NAFTA Article 1121 conditions the NAFTA Parties' consent to arbitration on the claimant: (a) waiving the right to initiate or continue proceedings before any domestic court or administrative tribunal concerning the same measures alleged to constitute a NAFTA breach; and (b) **acting consistently with that waiver** — i.e., actually abstaining from initiating or continuing parallel proceedings after submission. A written waiver form is necessary but not sufficient. Sources: [SICE OAS, NAFTA Chapter 11 Part 2](http://www.sice.oas.org/trade/nafta/chap-112.asp); [Fordham Law Review, "No 'Double-Dipping' Allowed"](https://fordhamlawreview.org/issues/no-double-dipping%C2%9D-allowed-an-analysis-of-waste-management-inc-v-united-mexican-states-and-the-article-1121-waiver-requirement-for-arbitration-under-chapter-11-of-nafta/)

#### A.2 Jurisdictional consequence: leading cases

In *Waste Management, Inc. v. United Mexican States* (ICSID Case No. ARB(AF)/98/2, first proceeding), the tribunal found that compliance with Art. 1121 is a *condition precedent* to perfecting the NAFTA Parties' consent to arbitration. Even where the formal requisites were nominally met, the waiver was legally ineffective because the claimant continued parallel proceedings; the tribunal dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. In *Detroit International Bridge Co. v. Canada*, the tribunal likewise held that the claimant's failure to waive rights to continue parallel domestic litigation deprived the tribunal of jurisdiction. Both cases confirm: the Art. 1121 waiver condition is jurisdictional, not a procedural technicality. Sources: [ICSID, *Waste Management* Award](http://icsidfiles.worldbank.org/icsid/ICSIDBLOBS/OnlineAwards/C166/DC570.pdf); [Government of Canada, *Detroit International Bridge*](https://www.international.gc.ca/trade-agreements-accords-commerciaux/topics-domaines/disp-diff/detroit-05.aspx?lang=eng)

#### A.3 Application to Einarsson and significance for the data-as-investment question

Canada's jurisdictional objections in Einarsson include: (1) three-year limitations bar under NAFTA Arts. 1116(2)/1117(2); and (2) that the claimants acted inconsistently with Arts. 1121(1) and (2) by continuing to pursue claims for damages in parallel proceedings. The case remains pending as of June 2026 — no award. Jusmundi records updated party representatives as of April 7, 2025; search results reference testimony from August 2025 post-hearing proceedings. Sources: [UNCTAD Navigator, *Einarsson v. Canada*](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-dispute-settlement/cases/1065/einarsson-v-canada); [Jusmundi, Updated Party Representatives, 7 Apr 2025](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-theodore-david-einarsson-harold-paul-einarsson-and-russell-john-einarsson-geophysical-service-incorporated-v-government-of-canada-updated-party-representatives-monday-7th-april-2025)

**Project significance:** If Canada's Art. 1121 waiver-consistency objection succeeds, Einarsson would be dismissed on a jurisdictional basis entirely independent of the abuse-of-rights/critical-date doctrine that Ring 3 currently centres on (Philip Morris pattern). The tribunal would never reach whether seismic data qualifies as a "covered investment." For the Ferguson/Kim framework, this reveals a *second*, distinct Ring 3 mechanism in data-as-investment cases: the procedural waiver-consistency condition, which turns on the claimant's conduct in parallel domestic proceedings rather than on corporate restructuring or treaty-shopping. This mechanism is equally applicable in a future pharmaceutical clinical data ISDS claim where the claimant simultaneously pursues domestic remedies.

### B. Thread T04 — China BIT Architecture: "Know-How" and the Three-Ring Pathway

#### B.1 Generational structure of Chinese BITs: who gets full ISDS

China's BITs fall into three generations distinguished by ISDS scope:

- **First generation (1980s–late 1990s):** ISDS strictly limited to "disputes over compensation resulting from expropriation and nationalization" — quantum only. In 88 first-generation Chinese BITs, ICSID is listed as an arbitration venue in only 18; the majority provide ad hoc or no venue. Source: [IISD ITN, "A Look into China's Slowly Increasing Appearance in ISDS Cases," 2017](https://www.iisd.org/itn/2017/09/26/a-look-into-chinas-slowly-increasing-appearance-in-isds-cases-dilini-pathirana/)

- **Second generation (1998–~2008):** Beginning with the China-Barbados BIT (1998), China first consented to international arbitration of "any dispute concerning an investment" — the watershed opening of ISDS to all investment disputes, not just quantum. Approximately 50 second-generation treaties follow this pattern. Source: [MSA Asia, "China's Bilateral Investment Treaties"](https://msadvisory.com/chinas-bilateral-investment-treaties-bits/)

- **Third generation (post-2003):** China-Germany BIT (2003), China-France BIT (2007), China-Switzerland BIT (2009) include modern protections: **FET**, **full protection and security**, **MFN**, and **full ISDS** not limited to quantum. These are the most permissive for investor claims from European pharmaceutical actors. Sources: [UNCTAD Navigator, China-Germany BIT (2003)](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/international-investment-agreements/treaties/bit/905/china---germany-bit-2003-); [UNCTAD Navigator, China-Switzerland BIT (2009)](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/international-investment-agreements/treaties/bit/978/china---switzerland-bit-2009-)

#### B.2 "Know-how" in the China-Switzerland BIT (2009): the clearest Ring 1 hook for pharmaceutical data

The China-Switzerland BIT (2009) defines "investment" to include: *"Copyrights, industrial property rights (such as patents, utility models, industrial designs or models, trade or service marks, trade names, indications of origin), know-how and goodwill."*

"Know-how" is the natural treaty characterisation for pharmaceutical clinical trial data: confidential, proprietary, commercially generated technical information protected from disclosure. Unlike "trade secret" (a domestic law concept not standardly used in BIT investment definitions), "know-how" is a recognised term in IP conventions (Paris Convention, TRIPS Art. 39) and bilateral treaties. If clinical trial data is characterised as "know-how," the China-Switzerland BIT expressly covers it as a protected investment — independently of any domestic Chinese law protection, and independently of the NMPA 2026 framework (which establishes the Ring 1 foundation from the domestic law side). The "know-how" definition provides the *treaty* Ring 1 basis.

Switzerland's pharmaceutical sector — Roche, Novartis, Lonza — has substantial exposure to Chinese regulatory measures on pharmaceutical data exclusivity. A Swiss-incorporated pharmaceutical company holding clinical trial data could plausibly assert: (1) the data constitutes "know-how" covered by the BIT (Ring 1); (2) China's NMPA regulatory decisions on data reliance constitute a governmental measure (Ring 2); (3) any Ring 3 objection would be tested under the BIT's ISDS provisions and applicable doctrine. Sources: [EDIT/WTI, China-Switzerland BIT (2009)](https://edit.wti.org/document/show/2e5072e0-92b8-49fa-a32b-4ca1611085bb); [UNCTAD Navigator entry](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/international-investment-agreements/treaties/bit/978/china---switzerland-bit-2009-)

**Calibration note:** The "know-how" provision is confirmed from the treaty text excerpt in the search result, crosschecked against the EDIT and UNCTAD Navigator database entries. No case has applied this provision to pharmaceutical data — the analysis is doctrinal inference, not decided authority.

#### B.3 EU-China CAI: still suspended; no modern EU-China ISDS framework operative

The EU-China Comprehensive Agreement on Investment (agreed in principle December 2020) remains suspended as of June 2026. The European Parliament has conditioned ratification on the lifting of Chinese counter-sanctions imposed in March 2021 (retaliatory response to the EU's Xinjiang sanctions). No lifting has occurred. This means there is no operative modern EU-China investment framework with ISDS; European pharmaceutical investors must rely on individual EU member-state BITs with China. Switzerland, not an EU member, operates the China-Switzerland BIT (2009) independently and is unaffected by the CAI impasse. Source: [European Parliament Legislative Train, EU-China CAI](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-global-europe-leveraging-our-power-and-partnerships/file-eu-china-investment-agreement)

### C. Thread T05 — UNCITRAL WG III: Post-April 2025 Trajectory Updated

The prior record correctly characterised the April 2025 (51st session) as stalled. Subsequent developments update the trajectory:

- **WP.253** (issued mid-2025): Revised draft provisions renumber the denial-of-benefits clause as **Draft Provision 9** (formerly Draft Provision 17 in WP.244). The provision still addresses shell companies (non-contracting-party owned/controlled), impermissible third-party funding, and illegally-made investments. No confirmed extension to natural-person nationality planning. Source: [UNCITRAL, WP.253](https://uncitral.un.org/sites/uncitral.un.org/files/media-documents/uncitral/en/wp.253_draft_provisions_initial_draft_for_website.pdf)

- **9th Intersessional Meeting, 5–7 November 2025 (Santiago, Chile):** Focused on procedural and cross-cutting issues. WP.263 records the meeting's outcome. States bundled procedural draft provisions as a "concrete deliverable" for the Commission. Source: [UNCITRAL, 9th Intersessional Meeting](https://uncitral.un.org/en/content/ninth-inter-sessional-meeting)

- **53rd Session, 12–16 January 2026 (New York):** Described in EJIL:Talk! commentary as "another modest but revealing step." A July 2026 Commission deadline is driving convergence on what is "feasible" — procedural bundles — rather than what is "transformative" (substantive DOB doctrine extension). Risk: WG III may present only some reform elements in July 2026, leaving the deeper reform agenda unfinished. Sources: [EJIL:Talk!, "Time, Form, and Coalitions: 53rd Session"](https://www.ejiltalk.org/time-form-and-coalitions-reflections-on-the-53rd-session-of-uncitral-working-group-iii/); [CCSI, same](https://ccsi.columbia.edu/news/time-form-and-coalitions-reflections-on-the-53rd-session-of-uncitral-working-group-iii/)

**Assessment:** The WG III process has continued since April 2025 and is converging on a July 2026 Commission deliverable. But the trajectory confirms: reform is procedural-first, not substantive-extension-first. The natural-person DOB gap (Jason Yu Song) is very unlikely to be closed by the July 2026 package. The Ring 3 doctrine architecture remains unchanged pending at least a 54th session (scheduled March 2026) and potentially further sessions.
