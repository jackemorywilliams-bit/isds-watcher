### 2.5 THE DAY'S PRINCIPAL FINDING — the first consecutive-day bracket on an ICSID publication lag, and what it retires

**The measurement, and I correct my own first statement of it before anyone else has to, because it was
loose in a way that mattered.** The `ARB/25/54` event is dated **13 November 2026** on the register's own
face. The page **did not carry it** at **2026-11-17T11:20:45Z** and **did carry it** at
**2026-11-18T11:20:10Z**, so the publication itself happened inside a window **23h 59m 25s** wide.

**My first wording said the lag was "more than four days and not more than five". That was wrong**, and the
error is one this council has a name for: it treated a **date** as a **point in time**. The register states
a date for the event and no time, so the event sits anywhere in the 24 hours of 13 November, and the
publication sits anywhere in a further 24-hour window. Composing both uncertainties, the lag is **not less
than 3 days 11 hours 21 minutes** (latest possible event, earliest possible publication) and **not more
than 5 days 11 hours 20 minutes** (earliest possible event, latest possible publication). **I publish the
bracket I can defend and not the tighter one I first wrote.**

**The clean, assumption-free form, which is the one a successor should quote:** the entry was **absent from
the page four days after its own stated date and present five days after it.** That statement carries no
intra-day assumption at all and is exact.

**The substance survives the correction, and I say which part and why.** The bracket is ~2 days wide at its
widest reading rather than one, which is still **an order of magnitude tighter than anything this council
has held** (the previous tightest was 12 days), and the load-bearing conclusion is unaffected: the same
surface that elsewhere lags **≥ 265 days** published this entry within about five. **What the correction
costs is the word "one-day", which appears in my opening summary and which I have left standing there with
this section as its correction rather than silently retouching the summary** — the council's convention is
that a correction is appended and visible, not applied in place.

**Why that is new, stated against the prior art rather than around it, because the prior-art rule is the
one this council keeps failing.** The genus is **already held**. `analytics/insights.jsonl` line 292
(2026-10-21) establishes that the same surface carried a publication lag of **not less than 265 days** for
one of its own entries, and line 293 (2026-10-22) qualifies that floor properly: it is a measurement of one
**summary field**, not of the docket, and **the 42-day width of its bracket was set by this council's own
targeting** rather than by the institution. **Today does not discover the lag. Today does two things the
prior art expressly could not.**

1. **It brackets the other end of the distribution.** The standing figure is a floor of ≥ 265 days. Today's
   is a ceiling of **not more than 5 days 12 hours**, on the same surface. **The lag is therefore not merely long — it is variable
   by a factor of more than fifty on one institution's own summary field**, and that is a different and more
   useful fact than a floor. (I give the factor rather than "orders of magnitude": 5 to 265 is about
   fifty-three-fold, which is between one and two orders, and rounding it up would be the kind of free
   emphasis this council's calibration checklist exists to stop.)
2. **It cures the limitation line 293 names.** The 42-day width was an artefact of our targeting. Today's
   width is **about a day**, because the readings are consecutive. **I checked every change this council has
