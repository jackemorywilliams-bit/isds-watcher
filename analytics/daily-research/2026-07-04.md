### D. Thread T04 — China Pharmaceutical: NEW — SAMR Order No. 126 (Effective June 1, 2026)

**New finding, not previously in the project record.**

The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issued the *Provisions on the Protection of
Trade Secrets* (Order No. 126) on February 24, 2026; the Provisions took effect June 1, 2026. This
is the most significant overhaul of China's administrative trade secret enforcement framework in
approximately thirty years, expanding the prior 1995 Provisions from 12 articles to 31.

**Key Ring 1–relevant provisions:**

*Expanded coverage of data assets:* The Provisions explicitly include **"data, algorithms, and
computer program code"** as categories of technical information eligible for trade secret protection,
alongside traditional categories (formulas, manufacturing processes, materials, technical methods).
Pharmaceutical clinical trial data — compilations of efficacy, safety, and quality data generated to
support regulatory submissions — falls within the broadened "data" category. No provision
specifically addresses pharmaceutical data; this coverage is by inclusion of "data" generally.

*Administrative enforcement mechanism:* The Provisions establish a dedicated administrative
enforcement channel for trade secret protection, parallel to civil and criminal enforcement:
injunctions and fines up to 5 million RMB are available; damages are not. Article 23 authorizes local
market regulatory authorities to enter business premises, subpoena parties, access documents and data,
and seal assets. Article 3 establishes jurisdiction at the city level with districts or above.

*Extraterritorial reach:* Article 29 explicitly establishes extraterritorial effect: where an act of
trade secret infringement occurs outside China but produces competitive harm within the Chinese market,
SAMR asserts administrative jurisdiction.

**Connection to the research question:**

The SAMR Provisions run parallel to the NMPA Implementation Measures for Drug Trial Data Protection
(effective May 15, 2026): where the NMPA framework provides pharmaceutical-specific data exclusivity
(a 6-year blocking period for innovative drugs), the SAMR framework provides general administrative
trade-secret protection. For a European pharmaceutical investor advancing a BIT claim under the
China-Germany BIT (2003)'s "trade and business secrets" investment definition:

- China's own administrative law (SAMR Order No. 126) now explicitly characterizes **data** as a
  protectable trade secret, corroborating the characterization of pharmaceutical clinical trial data
  as "trade and business secrets" within the meaning of Article 1 of the China-Germany BIT.
- A governmental action that allows unauthorized access to pharmaceutical trial data would now
  potentially trigger violations of both the NMPA exclusivity framework (Ring 2 trigger #1) and the
  SAMR trade secret protections (Ring 2 trigger #2).
- The SAMR framework's administrative remedies are separate from the NMPA's regulatory mechanism;
  a state that fails to enforce SAMR trade-secret protections for pharmaceutical data could face a
  separate FET/minimum-standard-of-treatment argument.

**Confidence assessment:** Medium-high for the regulatory facts (multiple practitioner secondary
sources: National Law Review, Sheppard Mullin, Morgan Lewis, CMS Law, Faegre Drinker — none directly
read; facts recovered from search engine summaries). **Medium** for the BIT/ISDS connection: this is
a research inference; no practitioner source makes this connection explicitly. The SAMR Provisions
do not address investment treaties or ISDS. The coverage of pharmaceutical data is by inclusion of
"data" generally, not by specific mention of clinical trial data.

Sources: [(National Law Review, China SAMR trade secrets)](https://natlawreview.com/article/chinas-state-administration-market-regulation-releases-regulations-protection-trade); [(chinaipr.com, SAMR trade secret rules)](https://chinaipr.com/2026/03/12/new-trade-secret-protection-rules-in-china/) [page 403-blocked; facts from search engine summary]; [(Sheppard Mullin, trade secret stakes)](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/china-raises-the-stakes-on-trade-secret-protection-what-companies-and-counsel-need-to-know-about-the-new-rules/) [page 403-blocked; facts from search engine summary]; [(Morgan Lewis, trade secret modernization)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/with-new-regulations-china-modernizes-the-administrative-regime-to-protect-trade-secrets) [page 403-blocked; facts from search engine summary].

### E. Drug Registration Certificate Tradability — Source Confirmation

The existing STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER.md entry states that the NMPA Implementation Measures "create formal,
protectable, and tradable entitlements in clinical trial data and marketing authorisation certificates."
Today's research provides a more specific source and legal basis for the "tradable" characterization.

The State Council Implementing Regulations of the Drug Administration Law (issued January 27, 2026;
effective May 15, 2026) establish "the legal basis for the acquisition, re-registration and assignment
of drug registration certificates, making them an integral part of M&A transactions in the pharmaceutical
field." Certificate assignment is subject to NMPA approval within 20 working days. Drug registration
and chemical active ingredient approval certificates have a five-year validity period, with re-registration
upon expiration.

This is a statutory provision in the State Council regulations, not merely a policy statement: the formal
legal basis for certificate assignment strengthens the characterization of drug registration certificates
(and associated data protection entitlements) as formal, assignable assets under Chinese domestic law —
which in turn supports the "covered investment" characterization under BIT definitions.

Sources: [(IAM/Lexology, drug registration certificates as tradable assets)](https://www.iam-media.com/article/china-overhauls-drug-administration-regime-through-introduction-of-market-exclusivity-data-safeguards-and-registration-certificates-tradable-assets) [page 403-blocked; facts from search engine summary]; [(Lexology reprint)](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=888c3377-3f3b-4b65-b097-d0c21fbc3921) [page 403-blocked; facts from search engine summary].
