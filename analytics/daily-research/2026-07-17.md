### 2B. China-Germany BIT Protocol — Article 9(b) Newly Identified: Complete Three-Condition Architecture Recovered (GENUINE NEW INSIGHT)

**Major advance — completes the Protocol Article 9 gate architecture.**

Today's targeted search for the full China-Germany BIT Protocol Article 9 text recovered, for the first time in the project record, all three conditions of the pre-ICSID arbitration gate:

> *Article 9 of the China-Germany BIT (2003): with respect to investments in the People's Republic of China, an investor of the Federal Republic of Germany may submit a dispute for arbitration under the following conditions only:*
> *(a) the investor has referred the issue to an administrative review procedure according to Chinese law,*
> *(b) the dispute still exists three months after he has brought the issue to the review procedure, and*
> *(c) in case the issue has been brought to a Chinese court, it can be withdrawn by the investor according to Chinese law.*

**What is new: Ad Article 9(b) — the three-month waiting period.** Article 9(b) is not previously documented anywhere in the project record. It establishes a mandatory three-month waiting period: the investor must not only have initiated Chinese administrative review (9(a)) but must also wait until three months have elapsed after initiating that review before ICSID arbitration may be filed. This is a *temporal gate* — separate from the procedural gate of 9(a) and the concurrent-court-proceedings gate of 9(c) — that was present in the BIT the entire time but was not surfaced in any of the project's prior searches.

**Revised complete pre-ICSID architecture under the China-Germany BIT (2003) Protocol:**

| Step | Ad Article | Condition | Effect |
|------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | 9(a) | Refer issue to Chinese administrative review procedure | Mandatory first step; no ICSID access without it |
| 2 | 9(b) | Three months must have elapsed since referral to administrative review | Temporal gate: ICSID cannot be filed immediately after exhausting admin review; must wait 3 months |
| 3 | 9(c) | If issue brought to Chinese court, investor must withdraw before ICSID filing | Concurrent-court-proceedings bar; cure = withdrawal under Chinese law |

**Implication for the pharmaceutical China-Germany BIT scenario:** A European pharmaceutical investor challenging an NMPA data exclusivity decision under the China-Germany BIT (2003) must: (1) submit to Chinese administrative review (NMPA-level appeal); (2) wait three months; (3) if any concurrent Chinese court proceedings were also filed (e.g., from court review of the administrative decision), withdraw them before ICSID filing. The three-step sequence imposes both a procedural track and a minimum elapsed time on the investor's pre-ICSID pathway — with 9(c)'s withdrawal requirement sitting at the end of a process that 9(a) and 9(b) require the investor to have fully initiated. The structural tension between satisfying 9(a)/(b) (which requires engagement with domestic review) and then satisfying 9(c) (which requires withdrawal of any court filings that such engagement may produce) is now sharply visible.

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.** The three-condition text was recovered from a search-engine snippet synthesizing the Protocol Article 9 provisions; the most likely primary source is one or more of: (a) the UNCTAD BIT PDF download link (investmentpolicy.unctad.org/international-investment-agreements/treaty-files/736/download) — which appeared as a direct URL in the search results and may be the actual BIT/Protocol PDF; (b) the SMU Law Review article (scholar.smu.edu) already known to address Chinese BIT MFN and Protocol provisions; (c) the EDIT/WTI database entry. The 9(b) condition is consistent with the broader pattern of Chinese BIT "three-month cooling-off" requirements documented in academic literature. The 9(a) text is consistent with the MEDIUM-confidence reconstruction from JIDS 2020 (July 15). The 9(c) text is consistent with the MEDIUM-HIGH confirmation from JIDS 2020 (July 11). Primary BIT Protocol text not directly read (PDF blocked throughout).
