The "standalone set" characterisation is the most precise pre-session framing in the project record (slightly more specific than the Rabat workshop's "optional additional document to the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules"). This does not change the no-adoption-claim rule — it refines the expected instrument form for when adoption is confirmed. ### 2B. Okuashvili v. Georgia — Svea Court of Appeal Remand Identified (New Finding)

**Westerberg & Partners article identified (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence for snippet content; body 403-blocked):** A new practitioner source has been identified: "Hallmark victory before the Swedish Supreme Court," Westerberg & Partners [URL: https://westerberg.com/hallmark-victory-before-the-swedish-supreme-court/]. Westerberg & Partners is the winning party's counsel (representing Okuashvili). The article body is 403-blocked, but search-engine snippets from two independent queries consistently reproduce the following language:

> "In a unanimous decision issued on 26 June 2026, the Supreme Court held that Mr Okuashvili may rely on the MFN clause in the applicable BIT to access a more favourable dispute settlement clause offered to other foreign investors. The ruling of the Supreme Court underscores the importance of the treaty text adopted by the States parties and confirms that an MFN clause can establish the State's unequivocal consent to arbitration. The decision marks a significant jurisdictional victory for the client, with **the proceedings now remanded to the Court of Appeal to address legal points left unresolved in the lower instance proceedings.**"

Two elements are new:

**(1) Decision characterized as unanimous (NEW, MEDIUM-HIGH).** The prior project record did not confirm unanimity of the SC ruling. This is now at MEDIUM-HIGH confidence from the winning party's counsel firm. Caveat: "unanimous" language appears in a winning party's announcement, not from a neutral source.

**(2) Svea Court of Appeal remand (NEW AND MATERIALLY SIGNIFICANT, MEDIUM-HIGH).** The Swedish Supreme Court did not directly restore the SCC arbitration. Instead, it reversed the Svea Court of Appeal's annulment ruling (November 12, 2024) and **remanded the case back to the Svea Court of Appeal** to address "legal points left unresolved in the lower instance proceedings." This materially corrects the project record's prior framing of the Okuashvili posture. The July 5 entry in STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER states "confirmed posture: jurisdiction restored by Supreme Court; case proceeds on merits at SCC" — this is now inaccurate. The correct posture is: **jurisdiction on the MFN-forum-access question established by the SC; case remanded to Svea CoA for further proceedings on unresolved points; SCC arbitration does not proceed until the Svea CoA completes its remand work.**

The "legal points left unresolved in the lower instance proceedings" language is consistent with Swedish appellate practice: when the Supreme Court reverses a lower court on a dispositive question but other grounds of annulment remain unaddressed by the lower court (because the lower court resolved the case on the MFN question alone), it remands for the remaining issues to be adjudicated. The November 2024 Svea CoA annulment was based specifically on the MFN-scope holding (the SC reversed this) — but the Svea CoA may have left other grounds of challenge unaddressed. The remand resolves the SC-level question while requiring the Svea CoA to dispose of any remaining annulment grounds.

**Implication for Ring 3 architecture:** The Okuashvili holding — that an explicit MFN clause extending to all BIT provisions including dispute settlement can import an alternative forum — is now definitively established by the Swedish Supreme Court. However, the SCC arbitration remains suspended pending Svea CoA remand proceedings. This is not a merits-stage win; it is a jurisdictional holding in the context of ongoing set-aside proceedings, with further Swedish court work required before the SCC arbitration can proceed.

**China BIT closure confirmed (no new information):** The architectural implication documented July 2 — that this pathway is closed for Chinese BITs because Chinese BIT MFN clauses explicitly exclude procedural ISDS rights — is not affected by the remand finding.
