- **The original proceeding.** Row 2 (`Date Registered`): *"ant(s)/Nationality(ies): (i) Veolia

**The ruling.** Thread 66's hypothesis is **CONFIRMED on its central limb and the technique
survives**: each entry on `/cases/recent` renders its date **before** its name, and the date is
the date of the docket event that put the entry there. The apparent contradiction that thread 66
called "fatal to any uniform reading" — a 2026 date on a 2018 designator — is **dissolved rather
than explained away**: the matter's own registration is `June 20, 2018`, which matches its
designator to the year, and the 2026 date is the registration of a **subsidiary proceeding** — an
annulment, brought by the **Respondent** against an award the tribunal had already rendered on
September 26, 2025.

**And it corrects my own pre-registration in the direction of a stronger rule.** I wrote, before
the rows fired, that outcome (A) would merely *harden* the standing prohibition "because the row
itself still does not say WHICH feed listed any given entry". That was too pessimistic, and the
result shows why: the `Registered` feed **does** account for this entry, so the hedge can be
replaced with something precise and usable rather than merely reaffirmed. The rule I adopt, which
replaces three weeks of "no date on this page may be read as a registration date":

> A date on ICSID's *Recent Cases* page is the date of a docket event in the named matter. Where
> that event is a registration, **it may be the registration of a subsidiary proceeding —
> annulment, revision, rectification, supplementary decision — rather than of the case.** So a
> date on that page may never be read as *the matter's* registration date, and a designator-year
> mismatch is positive evidence of a subsidiary proceeding rather than evidence that the page is
> incoherent. Which of the four feeds listed any given entry still cannot be read off the row, and
> that limit is unchanged.
