#### A.2 A/CN.9/1246 temporal scope — material clarification (today's key finding)

Although the PDF remains blocked, a targeted search query combining the document symbol, known subject-matter terms, and the "1 April 2027" date returned a search-engine snippet that appears to reproduce language from the document body. The snippet reads, in relevant part:

> "A provision similar to article 1 of the Transparency Rules detailing the scope of application of the supplementary provisions (including the temporal scope, possible opt-out and conflicts) **may need to be developed**. In the case of the supplementary provisions, they could apply, **for example**, to treaties concluded on or after 1 April 2027, if [certain conditions relating to] UARs [are met]."

This is materially more informative than yesterday's finding and changes the character of the open thread:

- **"For example":** The "1 April 2027" date is explicitly framed as an *illustrative example*, not a confirmed or finalized temporal cutoff. The document is proposing what such a provision *could* look like, not recording what it *does* say.
- **"May need to be developed":** The scope-of-application provision was identified in the April 15, 2026 draft as something still to be developed — meaning the temporal scope was not finalized in the document submitted to the Commission. The 59th session itself may have been the forum for deciding this.
- **Mauritius Convention analogy:** The snippet also references making the supplementary provisions applicable to pre-existing investment treaties "through a mechanism akin to the United Nations Convention on Transparency in Treaty-based Investor-State Arbitration" (the Mauritius Convention). The Mauritius Convention is an opt-in instrument under which states party to pre-April 2014 BITs can consent to the application of the Transparency Rules to arbitrations under those treaties. A parallel mechanism for the ISDS Protocol would mean that the China-Germany BIT (2003), China-Switzerland BIT (2009), and China-France BIT (2007) — the identified Ring 1 vehicles for pharmaceutical trade secret claims — could be brought within the Protocol's scope if Germany, Switzerland, France, and China all opt in via a future Mauritius-type convention.

**Calibration:** This language is from a search-engine snippet of A/CN.9/1246 (April 15, 2026 advance copy), not from a directly readable PDF. The language is specific and self-consistent, and the source URL in the search result confirms it points to the correct document; however, I have not verified it against the primary text. Mark as MEDIUM confidence. This is one step above yesterday's partial finding because the contextual language ("for example," "may need to be developed") is sufficiently specific to change the character of the finding.

**Significance for the research question:** The previous research framing — that the Protocol is non-retroactive because it applies to treaties concluded on or after April 1, 2027, leaving legacy BITs outside its scope — requires revision. The accurate framing is: (a) the Protocol's temporal scope is exemplified in the draft as "after April 1, 2027," but this is illustrative, not finalized; (b) the scope provision was flagged as requiring development at the Commission level; (c) the Mauritius Convention mechanism is proposed as a pathway for legacy BIT inclusion — making opt-in coverage of the China-Germany, China-Switzerland, and China-France BITs a live possibility if parties agree. The Ring 3 implication: UNCITRAL reform could reach legacy BITs, but only through a separate opt-in instrument (analogous to the Mauritius Convention) that no party appears to have committed to join.
