### Finding 4 — China-France BIT (2007) Protocol Administrative Exhaustion: Permissive Formulation Indicated [LOW-MEDIUM confidence; unverified; security flag]

Two separate search queries targeting the China-France BIT (2007) Protocol administrative exhaustion provision yielded search-engine summaries that characterize the provision as **permissive**. The most specific characterization: the China-France BIT (2007) provides that arbitration may be submitted to ICSID, "subject to the right of the Contracting Party which is a party to the dispute to request the investor concerned to use the domestic administrative review procedures specified by the laws and regulations of that Contracting Party before the dispute is submitted to ICSID." This formulation — "right to request" — is discretionary host-state language, not mandatory investor-side language.

If accurate, this is consistent with the China-Switzerland BIT (2009) Protocol permissive "may require" formulation (confirmed MEDIUM-HIGH, July 19) and the inferred China-Sweden BIT (1982/2004) Protocol permissive structure (UNCONFIRMED structural inference). It would mean that among the four European China BITs in scope, **three use permissive administrative exhaustion formulations** (China-Switzerland, China-Sweden, China-France) and two use mandatory language (China-Germany (2003) Protocol Art. 9(a); China-Netherlands (2001) Exchange of Notes).

Additionally, the Springer / Netherlands International Law Review article ("China's Stance on Investor-State Dispute Settlement") is summarized as characterizing "18 second-generation Chinese BITs" as using optional host-state-discretion administrative exhaustion language. The China-France BIT (2007) fits the second-generation BIT profile (full ISDS, broad investment definition, post-2000 signing).

**Caution (see Security vetting, below):** The EDIT/WTI database entry for the China-France BIT and the Springer Netherlands ILR article were both 403-blocked on direct fetch; neither body was read. The provision text characterization above is from a search engine summary, which may be paraphrasing rather than quoting the treaty text. This is the 23rd session in which this gap has been pursued; the security officer classifies this at LOW-MEDIUM confidence. It does not meet the threshold for a new ledger entry. The operator's UNCTAD IIA Mapping Navigator query and JIDS 12(1):125 (Manjiao Chi, library access) remain the two confirmed resolution routes.

`GAP-UNRESOLVED: china-france-bit-2007-protocol-exhaustion — China-France BIT (2007) Protocol administrative exhaustion formulation (mandatory vs. permissive): not confirmed from any named, accessible body-text source; 23+ sessions.`
