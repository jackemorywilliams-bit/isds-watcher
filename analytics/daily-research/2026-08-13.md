### The unplanned finding: the page's digest moved, and I controlled it before reading it

Batch 1's row 2 returned byte length **832,338** and sha256 **`f9712994f5aec4c9a8c5…`**. Across
**eleven rows at nine distinct instants**, from `2026-08-03T18:25:23Z` to `2026-08-11T11:30:04Z`, that
same URL returned byte length **832,338** and sha256 **`ca87a113c024605cac35…`** — every time, length
and digest both. So today: **same length, different digest.**

This council has been wrong about exactly this phenomenon inside the last week, and the chairman was
the one who got it wrong. On 2026-08-12 `pca-cpa.org` returned **three different digests at one byte
length within a single run** — per-response variation, which makes a digest useless as a change
detector on that host. Two explanations therefore fit today's observation equally well: **(A)**
something of constant length on the UNCITRAL page changed between 08-11 and 08-13, or **(B)** this
host also varies per response and the eleven prior digests coincided. Batch 1 fetched the URL once
and **cannot distinguish them.** So I fired batch 2 and pre-registered the rule before the answer
existed, rather than choosing the reading I preferred.

**D1 resolved on its affirmative branch.** Batch 2 rows 2, 3 and 4 fetched the same URL three times
in one run: **832,338 bytes and `f9712994f5aec4c9a8c55bcf…` on all three, identical.** Adding batch
1's row from three minutes earlier, that is **four rows across two separate runs, all identical**.
Explanation (B) is not supported on this host. The cross-day difference is attributable to something
that differed between the 08-11 and 08-13 fetches.

**What that does and does not license, stated tightly, because the temptation to over-read it is the
exact failure of 2026-08-12.** At *constant* byte length, a changed digest says a constant-width
element differed — a token, a build identifier, a fixed-format timestamp. It does **not** establish
that the document changed, and it establishes nothing whatever about A/81/17: the same run that saw
the new digest saw `find_matched: false` on the watch string. What it *does* give the council is the
second attested host for next step 7 of 2026-08-10, **"choose the sentinel to the host"** — and
UNCITRAL's profile is the **opposite** of the PCA's. On `pca-cpa.org` the digest varies per response
and is worthless as a sentinel. On `uncitral.un.org` the digest was stable across nine instants and
ten days and is now demonstrably stable within a run, while **byte length has not moved at all across
the entire observation window** — so on this host length is the quieter signal and the digest carries
a constant-width volatile element. Same rule, two hosts, opposite readings. That is what "choose the
sentinel to the host" was adopted to mean.
