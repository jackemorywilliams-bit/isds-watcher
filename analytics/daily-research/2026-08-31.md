## Part I — Agenda, instrument state, and the shape of this meeting

### What this record is, for a reader who has none of its history

This project watches for a narrow intersection: **intellectual property — including trade secrets and
clinical-trial data — asserted as a covered investment**, against **a regulatory or judicial measure** as the
disputed conduct, with **a live, litigated jurisdictional or admissibility doctrine** such as abuse of right or
treaty-shopping. The intersection is deliberately narrow, and a genuinely quiet day is an honest result rather
than a failure. The daily council is a check-in, not a weekly council: one researcher, one chairman, one
optimization item, and a binding integrity challenge heard inside the discussion.

For the last week this council's substantive work has run on a single docket — **ICSID Case No. ARB/12/1,
*Tethyan Copper Company Pty Limited v. Islamic Republic of Pakistan*** — because that docket is where the
project's three rings actually meet in a live post-award posture, and because it is the one place where this
council's own instrument can read primary material that no secondary source carries. The instrument in question
needs one sentence of explanation for a new reader. **This session has no internet access of its own.** What it
has instead is a *relay*: it writes a small JSON file naming a handful of URLs and a literal string to look for
in each, pushes that file to GitHub, and a GitHub Actions runner — which does have network access — fetches each
page and commits back a *reduction*: the HTTP status, the byte length, a SHA-256 digest of the page, and a
single 400-character excerpt centred on the first place the literal occurs. **The document itself never travels
and is never stored.** So every factual question this council can answer from the primary docket has to be
answered through a 400-character window, and most of the methodological machinery below exists to keep the
council honest about what a 400-character window can and cannot establish.

### The member return-path protocol

Every member of this council is a **real subagent**, spawned with the repository's own committed definitions.
No seat's words are written by me, and no seat is performed. Each member files its return as its final message
and that text enters this record **verbatim** — I rule on it, I do not edit it. Where a seat's actual model
differs from the operator's directive, the seat reports the discrepancy unasked and the discrepancy is recorded
as a procedural caveat rather than silently absorbed. **If a spawn fails, the seat is recorded as a procedural
failure for the day and nothing is written in its place.**
