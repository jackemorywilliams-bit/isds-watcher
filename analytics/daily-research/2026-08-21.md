## Part I — Agenda, instrument state, and the shape of this meeting

### What this record is, for a reader who has none of its history

This project watches one narrow question: investor–State arbitration in which **intellectual property**
— patents, trade secrets, clinical-trial data — is asserted as a protected investment, a **government or
court measure** is the conduct complained of, and a **live jurisdictional or admissibility doctrine** is
actually being litigated. Three rings, and an item counts only if it touches all three. The council that
watches it meets daily and is deliberately small: a research analyst, a chairman, an integrity challenge,
and one improvement suggestion.

Two habits of this council are worth knowing before reading further. It treats **a genuinely empty day as
a real result** and says so rather than manufacturing significance. And it spends a large share of its
effort auditing **its own instruments** — because a research record that cannot say what its tools can and
cannot see will eventually state a tool failure as a fact about the world. That is why a record like this
one contains as much about `sha256` digests and character encodings as about arbitration.

### Member return-path protocol

Stated at the top of the meeting so that a later reader can tell what this record is evidence *of*:

1. Council members are **real subagents**, spawned from the committed definitions in `.claude/agents/`.
   The chairman **directs and never writes** a member's contribution.
2. A member's return enters this record **verbatim**, under a heading naming it as that member's text. It
   is not summarised, paraphrased, trimmed or corrected — where it is wrong, the correction is a *ruling*
   elsewhere in the record, never an edit to the member's words.
3. If a spawn fails or the Agent tool is unavailable, that seat is recorded as **PROCEDURAL FAILURE** for
   the day. **The chairman does not perform the missing voice.** This is the project's cardinal rule and
   the reason the integrity officer is convened as a real agent despite Rule 0's seat count.
4. The integrity officer's objections are **binding**. The chairman rules on **every one** in Part VI —
   sustained, overruled, or sustained-in-part — and an unruled objection is itself a defect.
5. Any discrepancy between a seat's directed model and its self-reported model is recorded as a
   **procedural caveat**, never silently substituted.
