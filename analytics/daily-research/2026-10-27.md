**The prior art, read before I wrote this and not after.** `analytics/daily-research/2026-10-14.md:1753-1757`

chrome change, or a navigation edit… the page changed; what changed in it is not established,

and occurrences counted separately and labelled as such). Yesterday's analyst named them prior
art and declined to log them, and it was right to.

**What is new is not the measurement but the discriminator the 10-14 carrier says it lacks.** A
single page's delta cannot be told apart from content; **two unrelated dockets moving by the
identical signed delta in the same batch can**, because an entry added to a Kenyan case's
chronology cannot also subtract exactly 335 bytes from an unrelated Canadian one. The content
hypothesis is strongly disfavoured and chrome is left. `"same magnitude"` and
`"identical magnitude"` return **0 files / 0 occurrences**; `"common delta"` and `"same delta"`
return **0 files / 0 occurrences**.

**And I tested it against the one batch in this corpus that could have refuted it.** On
`2026-09-04` eight docket observations moved at once: **+35, +34, +238, −16, +439, +34, +542,
+33**. That is **not** a common delta — it is a cluster of three near-equal small deltas among
much larger ones, and the large ones sit on dockets last observed eleven days earlier, where
content had time to accumulate. **So the general form of this signal is "a common magnitude
among dockets observed on a short interval", and today's exact equality is the clean case
rather than the law.** `n = 2` dockets in one batch. I state it at that strength and no higher.
