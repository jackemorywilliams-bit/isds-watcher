### A. China BIT MFN Architecture — Key Finding

#### A.1 Question

The June 30 session confirmed that the Okuashvili MFN bypass works where a BIT's MFN clause explicitly extends to dispute-settlement provisions (UK-Georgia BIT Article 3(3) covering Articles 1–11, including Article 8). The question today is whether the same mechanism could apply to Chinese BITs — specifically the China-Germany (2003), China-Switzerland (2009), and China-France (2007) BITs, which are the primary Ring 1 vehicles for a pharmaceutical trade-secret ISDS claim.

#### A.2 Finding — Chinese BITs explicitly exclude MFN extension to ISDS rights

Research confirms: Chinese BITs have been deliberately drafted to exclude MFN extension to procedural ISDS rights. Academic and practitioner literature is unambiguous on this point. One SMU Law Review article specifically addressing China BIT MFN treatment notes that "recent Chinese BITs have eliminated potential controversies over the scope of the MFN clause by making clear that the MFN treatment does not cover the procedural rights to dispute settlement." Arbitral tribunals in Chinese BIT disputes have "ruled consistently against the extension of the MFN clause to procedural rights in ISDS, with tribunals holding that the MFN clause does not extend to a state's consent to arbitrate with investors nor to temporal limitation periods for investor-state arbitration." Sources: [(SMU Law Review, "BITs, MFN Treatment and the PRC")](https://scholar.smu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1305&context=til); [(Aceris Law, "MFN Clauses in Investment Arbitration")](https://www.acerislaw.com/mfn-clauses-in-investment-arbitration/).

Confidence: MEDIUM. Both sources are practitioner and academic secondary. The China-Germany BIT 2003 primary text was not directly read (PDF 403-blocked as previously documented); the China-Switzerland 2009 and China-France 2007 treaty texts were also not accessed for the MFN article specifically. The characterisation is consistent with China's documented conservative ISDS posture (Hela Schwarz, Jason Yu Song, AsiaPhos contexts) and the specific legislative design noted in the literature. Mark as confirmed from secondary; primary BIT text verification remains an open desideratum.

#### A.3 Significance — negative architectural finding

This is a **negative architectural finding**: the Okuashvili MFN-forum-bypass pathway is closed for European pharmaceutical investors seeking ISDS access under Chinese BITs. A German pharmaceutical investor (Bayer, Merck KGaA) under the China-Germany BIT (2003), or a Swiss investor (Roche, Novartis) under the China-Switzerland BIT (2009), cannot use MFN to import a broader arbitral forum from another Chinese BIT — even if the investor is locked out of the designated forum for some reason. The pathway that worked in Okuashvili (explicit Article 3(3) MFN-to-dispute-settlement language) is not available in the Chinese BIT drafting architecture.

#### A.4 Ring 3 four-mechanism taxonomy — now complete

This finding allows the council to assemble the documented Ring 3 mechanisms into a definitive, structured form:

| # | Mechanism | Key Case | China Pharma BIT Applicability |
|---|-----------|----------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Abuse-of-rights / critical-date (restructuring) | *Philip Morris v. Australia* (PCA 2012-12, 2015) | Applicable — any corporate restructuring to gain BIT access |
| 2 | NAFTA Art. 1121 waiver-consistency | *Einarsson v. Canada* (UNCT/20/6; objection pending) | NAFTA/CUSMA-specific; not applicable to China BITs |
| 3 | Concurrent-proceedings / court-withdrawal | *Hela Schwarz v. China* (ICSID ARB/17/19, Dec. 2025) | Directly applicable to China-Germany BIT claims |
| 4 | MFN-scope-based forum bypass | *Okuashvili v. Georgia* (Swedish SC, June 2026) | **CLOSED** for China BITs — MFN excludes ISDS explicitly |

For the pharmaceutical IP / China BIT scenario: mechanisms 1 and 3 are the operative Ring 3 gates. Mechanism 2 is treaty-specific (NAFTA/CUSMA). Mechanism 4 is closed. The Ring 3 architecture for a future three-ring claim under the China-Germany BIT is therefore: (a) Philip Morris abuse-of-rights risk if the investor restructured to gain BIT access; and (b) Hela Schwarz concurrent-proceedings requirement — investor must withdraw any concurrent Chinese domestic proceedings before filing at ICSID. No additional Ring 3 bypass route via MFN exists.
