### 2B. China-Switzerland BIT (2009) Protocol — Permissive vs. Mandatory Administrative Exhaustion: Genuine New Architectural Insight

**NEW INSIGHT.** Today's cross-BIT Protocol scan of the China-Switzerland BIT (2009) reveals a material architectural difference from the China-Germany BIT (2003) and China-Netherlands BIT (2001) administrative exhaustion provisions previously documented.

**China-Switzerland BIT (2009) Protocol — dispute settlement structure:**
- **Article 11 (ISDS):** Six-month consultation period → investor choice of domestic courts OR ICSID/UNCITRAL arbitration.
- **Protocol:** China "**may require**" (not "shall require" or the mandatory formulation used in China-Germany/Netherlands BITs) the investor concerned to exhaust the domestic administrative review procedure specified by Chinese laws and regulations before submission to international arbitration. Any required review "shall not exceed three (3) months."
- **Court withdrawal:** Investor may submit to ICSID on condition that the investor "has withdrawn its case from the domestic court before a final judgement has been delivered on the subject matter."

**The critical architectural distinction:** "May require" is a permissive formulation — administrative exhaustion is at China's option in any specific dispute, not an automatically imposed mandatory pre-ICSID gate. This differs from:

| BIT | Administrative Exhaustion Formulation | Nature |
|-----|--------------------------------------|--------|
| China-Germany BIT (2003) Protocol Art. 9(a) | "has referred the issue to an administrative review procedure according to Chinese law" | **Mandatory** |
| China-Netherlands BIT (2001) Exchange of Notes | "requires investors to exhaust the domestic administrative review procedure…maximum period of three months" | **Mandatory** |
| China-Switzerland BIT (2009) Protocol | "may require the investor to exhaust the domestic administrative review procedure…not exceed three months" | **Permissive** (China's option) |
| China-Sweden BIT (1982) Protocol (2004) | "may require the investor concerned to exhaust the domestic administrative review procedure" | **Permissive** (China's option) |

**Cross-BIT architectural pattern:** The mandatory/permissive distinction appears to track treaty vintage and partner. China-Germany (2003) and China-Netherlands (2001) — both in the transition from second- to third-generation Chinese BITs — use mandatory language. China-Switzerland (2009) and China-Sweden (1982/2004) use permissive "may require" language. Academic literature on Chinese BIT practice notes this distinction: "the wording 'may' indicates that the exhaustion of an administrative review procedure is not a treaty requirement established by the BIT at issue, but only a possible request by the host state." [(Netherlands ILR, Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40802-020-00182-3)

**Implication for pharmaceutical Ring 1 scenario — material Ring 3 refinement:** Swiss pharmaceutical investors (Roche, Novartis, Lonza) challenging an NMPA pharmaceutical data exclusivity decision under the China-Switzerland BIT (2009) face a potentially simpler pre-ICSID gate than German investors under the China-Germany BIT (2003):
- No automatically mandatory administrative exhaustion pre-condition under the China-Switzerland BIT
- China would need to affirmatively invoke its "may require" right in the specific dispute
- The three-condition mandatory sequence — (9(a)) mandatory administrative referral → (9(b)) mandatory three-month wait → (9(c)) court withdrawal — documented for the China-Germany BIT is NOT present in the same mandatory form under the China-Switzerland BIT
- The China-Switzerland BIT's Article 11 six-month consultation period is a cooling-off window, not an administrative exhaustion mandate
- **Practical caveat (security officer flag):** Whether China exercises its "may require" right in any specific pharmaceutical dispute is unknown; the permissive formulation reduces the investor's mandatory compliance burden but does not eliminate the possibility of China invoking administrative exhaustion in a specific case

**Relevance to China-Switzerland BIT as the leading Ring 1 pathway:** The China-Switzerland BIT already had the clearest Ring 1 investment definition for pharmaceutical data ("know-how" expressly included). The permissive administrative exhaustion finding now also makes it the most investor-accessible pre-ICSID pathway among the three European China BITs for the pharmaceutical scenario — the China-Germany BIT's "trade and business secrets" Ring 1 advantage is partially offset by its more complex mandatory pre-ICSID gate. The China-Switzerland BIT combines a viable Ring 1 basis with a simpler Ring 3 access path.

**China-Sweden BIT (1982) Protocol (2004) — parallel permissive formulation confirmed:** "The Peoples Republic of China, when acting as a Contracting State involved in a dispute, may require the investor concerned to exhaust the domestic administrative review procedure specified by the laws and regulations of the Peoples Republic." Same structure as China-Switzerland, with three-month cap and court withdrawal condition. This is the treaty under which Huawei v. Sweden (ICSID ARB/22/2) proceeds; the permissive administrative exhaustion provision apparently did not present a jurisdictional bar in that case (the case registered and reached a full merits hearing). Source: [(Jusmundi, China-Sweden Protocol 2004)](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/treaty/zh-protocol-china-sweden-bit-1982-protocol-china-sweden-bit-1982-monday-27th-september-2004)

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Confirmed from multiple search results consistently describing the "may require" formulation; Netherlands ILR (Springer) explicitly characterizes the permissive vs. mandatory distinction across Chinese BITs; SMU Law Review discusses Chinese BIT administrative exhaustion architecture. Primary China-Switzerland BIT Protocol text (Aceris Law PDF) 403-blocked; text recovered from search-engine summaries and independent secondary descriptions consistent across multiple queries.

### 2C. Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. v. Kingdom of Sweden (ICSID ARB/22/2) — New Case Entered Project Record

**Significant new case.** *Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. v. Kingdom of Sweden* (ICSID Case No. ARB/22/2) is not in the project record and is the most advanced publicly confirmed Chinese-investor BIT claim against a European state involving technology/IP assets.

**Facts:**
- Claimant: Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. (China)
- Respondent: Kingdom of Sweden
- Treaty: China-Sweden BIT (1982) + Protocol (2004)
- Forum: ICSID (full Convention jurisdiction)
- Notice of Dispute: December 31, 2020; Request for Arbitration filed: January 7, 2022
- Dispute: Sweden's Post and Telecom Agency (PTS) conducted a 5G spectrum auction with licensing requirements prohibiting use of Huawei equipment in 5G networks and requiring phase-out of existing Huawei 3G/4G network equipment by 2025. Sweden invoked national security grounds; the Swedish Security Police and Swedish Armed Forces were consultation authorities.
- European Commission intervened as non-disputing party (Procedural Order No. 6, June 26, 2024)
- **Merits hearing: London, November 25 – December 5, 2024** (10 days)
- **Status: Awaiting award as of July 2026**

**Ring assessment:**
- **Ring 1:** Technology assets — Huawei's investments in Swedish 5G infrastructure, equipment supply/service relationships, and related interests as covered investments under the China-Sweden BIT. NOT pharmaceutical trade secrets or clinical data.
- **Ring 2:** Regulatory measure — Sweden's licensing requirements (5G auction prohibition + equipment phase-out) as the challenged measure. The state measure directly targets the company's technology assets/market position.
- **Ring 3:** No jurisdictional dismissal. No known preliminary objections that disposed of the case at the jurisdictional stage. The national security exception is an affirmative defense on the merits, not a pre-ICSID jurisdictional gate. The case cleared all preliminary stages and reached a full merits hearing.
- **Pre-ICSID gate:** The China-Sweden BIT (1982) Protocol (2004) uses the permissive "may require" administrative exhaustion formulation. The provision apparently did not prevent the case from registering and proceeding to merits.

**Significance for the project:**
1. The most advanced Chinese-investor-vs.-European-state BIT case involving technology/IP assets currently in the project record — post-hearing, awaiting award.
2. Structural parallel to Wingtech/Nexperia v. Netherlands (notice October 2025, pre-registration) — confirms the pattern: Chinese technology companies using European China BITs to challenge investment screening / market exclusion decisions on national security grounds.
3. The Gáspár-Szilágyi JIDS 2024 article ("When the Dragon Comes Home to Roost," JIDS vol. 15(2), pp. 195–220) cites Huawei v. Sweden as evidence that Chinese investors are already using BITs to challenge EU investment screening decisions. The article identifies BIT-level variables for EU respondent risk: modern ISA clauses, pre-establishment coverage, and absence of non-precluded measures clauses.
4. The European Commission's non-disputing party intervention signals that Brussels views the case as having systemic implications for EU investment screening under member state BITs.
5. **Award implications for the research question:** The award may be the first under a Chinese BIT involving technology assets challenged against a European state's national security regulation. Key questions the award will resolve: (a) what constitutes a "covered investment" in technology assets under the China-Sweden BIT; (b) whether a national security defense succeeds on the merits of a Chinese BIT claim; (c) how the tribunal interprets the BIT's investment definitions and protections. These are analogically relevant for the forward pharmaceutical scenario (European investor, Chinese respondent) — tribunal reasoning on (a) and (b) may inform how a future tribunal handles a pharmaceutical data claim against China.
6. **NOT on theme:** No pharmaceutical, trade-secret, or clinical-data nexus. Inverse scenario. Near-miss.

**Confidence: HIGH.** ICSID case number ARB/22/2, parties, treaty basis, hearing dates (November–December 2024, London), and post-hearing awaiting-award status confirmed from multiple independent authoritative sources: UNCTAD Navigator (ICSID docket confirmed), italaw (case page confirmed), IAReporter (initial filing article confirmed), Global Arbitration Review, CELIS Institute, Jusmundi (Procedural Order No. 6 June 26, 2024 confirmed).
