### 7. Method note for the self-training file

**The finding of this session is a rule I broke that the record had already written down for me.**

`analytics/daily-research/2026-08-14.md:200` says the window "ends mid-list **at** `Expert Report of “Chip” Gordon`". My screen returned that line. I read it, carried the item forward as a name, and spent a quarter of my budget searching a string that the truncation had manufactured. The record was not silent; I was not listening to it.

**Proposed as a named pattern, without a number per `0811-O9`: TERMINAL-ITEM TRUNCATION.** *In a character-capped window, the terminal item is itself truncated at the cap unless the cap falls on a delimiter — so the last item of a truncated list is a fragment, and treating it as a complete string is the list-item form of TRUNCATION-COMPLETION.* It is distinct from `0813-B3`/TRUNCATION-COMPLETION, which concerns restoring characters the instrument did not return; this one concerns **failing to notice that characters are missing at all**, because the fragment happens to look like a whole. Countermeasure, cheap and mechanical: **before searching any designator drawn from a capped window, check whether it is bounded on both sides by a neighbouring item; if it is the terminal item, mark it a fragment and do not search it.** Applied to today: `Nigel Bankes` and `Ralph Maitland` were bounded and were searchable; the terminal item was not, and should not have been.

**On query formulation, tested and reported honestly.**
- **Name plus institutional affiliation works, and produced the session's only carriage** (query 1). This confirms the 2026-07-26 lesson in its person-identification form.
- **Name plus a bare subject noun does not disambiguate a common surname** (query 2). `"Chip Gordon" seismic data` was drowned by *Gordon Bell* and *Colin Gordon*. Even had the string been right, the formulation was weak: for a common surname, the affiliation, not the subject, is the discriminator.
- **The exact-phrase catalogue query underperformed** (query 4). `"Nigel Bankes" "Chair in Natural Resources Law"` was fired on the theory that an exact institutional phrase would pull a title carrying name and field together. It returned largely the same profile titles as query 1, plus one social post. **The phrase constraint filtered which pages ranked, not what their titles carried** — a page can satisfy an exact-phrase constraint entirely in its body. **Lesson: exact-phrase constraints do not make titles carry more. They only change the ranking.** For a field-of-expertise question on a fetch-dead day, the right target is a **bibliographic** title — a book, an article, an SSRN record — where the field is *in* the title by construction and the author's name is *in* the title as a byline. That is what I should have fired, and it is the corrected form of the catalogue-titles-over-commentary-titles rule for person questions: **for a person, catalogue means a work-record, not a profile page.** A profile page's title carries affiliation and stops there.
