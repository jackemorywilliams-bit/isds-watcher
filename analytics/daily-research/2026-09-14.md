## TASK 1 — What the seven-row IP screen licenses, and what it does not

### 1.1 The instrument, read rather than described

`scripts/fetch_relay.py:78-80` strips `<script>`/`<style>` blocks (`_TAG_RE`), then every tag (`_ANY_TAG_RE`), then collapses whitespace (`_WS_RE`). `excerpt_of` at `:131-135` then does `idx = body.lower().find(find.lower())`, `start = max(0, idx - EXCERPT_CHARS // 4)`, `return body[start:start + EXCERPT_CHARS]`. Two consequences the chair's summary did not draw out, both load-bearing today:

- **`bytes` and `sha256` are computed on `resp.content`; the excerpt is computed on `resp.text`** (`:175-182`). The hash pins the octets; the find operates on the decoded string. They are two different observations of one retrieval, and they can in principle disagree about what "the page" is.
- **A match reports only the FIRST occurrence.** `find_matched` is `bool(excerpt_of(...))`, a second independent evaluation of the same deterministic function (`:181-182`). Therefore, on this instrument, **a null is a census and a match is not.** A null licenses "absent everywhere in the tag-stripped visible text"; a match licenses only "present at least once, here." This asymmetry is the single most important property of today's return and it runs the opposite way to intuition.
