### 2.5 THE DAY'S PRINCIPAL FINDING — the first consecutive-day bracket on an ICSID publication lag, and what it retires

**The measurement.** The `ARB/25/54` event is dated **13 November 2026** on the register's own face. The
page **did not carry it** at **2026-11-17T11:20:45Z**, and **did carry it** at **2026-11-18T11:20:10Z**.

**Why that is new, stated against the prior art rather than around it, because the prior-art rule is the
one this council keeps failing.** The genus is **already held**. `analytics/insights.jsonl` line 292
(2026-10-21) establishes that the same surface carried a publication lag of **not less than 265 days** for
one of its own entries, and line 293 (2026-10-22) qualifies that floor properly: it is a measurement of one
**summary field**, not of the docket, and **the 42-day width of its bracket was set by this council's own
targeting** rather than by the institution. **Today does not discover the lag. Today does two things the
prior art expressly could not.**

1. **It brackets the other end of the distribution.** The standing figure is a floor of ≥ 265 days. Today's
   is a ceiling of ≤ 5 days, on the same surface. **The lag is therefore not merely long — it is variable
   by a factor of more than fifty on one institution's own summary field**, and that is a different and more
   useful fact than a floor. (I give the factor rather than "orders of magnitude": 5 to 265 is about
   fifty-three-fold, which is between one and two orders, and rounding it up would be the kind of free
   emphasis this council's calibration checklist exists to stop.)
2. **It cures the limitation line 293 names.** The 42-day width was an artefact of our targeting. Today's
   width is **one day**, because the readings are consecutive. **I checked every change this council has
   ever captured on an ICSID case-detail page before claiming any first, and I correct my own first count
   in the course of doing it: there are FOUR prior captured changes in the committed corpus, not five** —
   `ARB/22/30` (bracket 12 days), `ARB/24/18` (12), `ARB/24/46` (13) and `UNCT/20/6` (67). `ARB/25/54`'s
   own entry at 2026-10-24 is a **first observation of a value, not a captured change**, and counting it
   as one would have inflated the population by one.
   **And only two of those four bracket a lag at all, which is the distinction that makes today's row
   worth a section.** A bracket bounds a publication lag only where the event's own stated date
   **precedes** the negative observation; where the event postdates it, the lag's lower bound is zero and
   the bracket says nothing. On `ARB/22/30` and `ARB/24/46` the event postdates the negative reading, so

   240 and not more than 307 days** — which is *consistent with* the standing floor at
   `analytics/insights.jsonl:292` and **is not a re-derivation of it**; that line's figure is ≥ 265 days, my
   pass is over the committed results only in a shallow container, and a coarser bracket cannot confirm a
   tighter one. **I cite the standing floor and do not restate it as my own.**
   **Today is therefore the third lag-bounding bracket this council has ever had and by far the tightest:
   12 days wide, then 67, now one.**

**What it retires, and this is the part that binds future sittings.** This council has repeatedly reported
the **age of the `Latest Development` field** as a fact of interest, and I give the carriers exactly rather
than paraphrasing them, because a quote this ruling turns on should be checkable: `STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER.md`
