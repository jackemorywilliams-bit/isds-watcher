### 1.3 What this does and does not license about the "one observation, not many" caveat

The caveat as the chairman states it — *repeated fetches of one unchanged page are one observation, not many* — is right, and today shows it needs one distinction drawn inside it that it does not currently draw.

**What it licenses.**

- **Today's A/81/17 null adds essentially nothing to yesterday's.** The two nulls were produced from a byte-identical representation. Day 7 is not an independent confirmation of day 6; it is the same page state read again. The seven daily nulls in thread A1 are **not** seven pieces of evidence, and any future seat computing confidence from a count of nulls is double-counting.
- **Net persistent change is excluded at each of the six instants.** A page that had been edited and left edited would have moved the digest.

**What it does not license, and these are the load-bearing ones.**

- **It does not collapse six sample points into one observation.** This is the distinction I think the caveat is missing. Two fetches *inside one batch* at one timestamp are genuinely one observation with zero marginal information — that is the redundancy the caveat was written for. Two fetches at *different* timestamps returning identical bytes are **two observations that agree**, and agreement is a result, not an absence of information. Collapsing them into "one observation" would throw away the temporal spread, which is the only thing the daily row is buying. The correct count is: **six observations of one state**, not one observation, and not eleven.
- **It does not establish a continuous negative interval.** A posting and reversion between samples is unobserved and unexcluded. At 832,338 bytes an exact restoration is improbable, but "improbable" is not "excluded", and 0806-B3 forbids me converting the sample set into a duration.
- **It does not establish that the origin's underlying holdings are unchanged.** The reduction captures no response headers — no `Age`, `ETag`, `Cache-Control` or `Last-Modified` — so a cached or statically-built response is not separable from a fresh render of unchanged data at this reduction level. "The bytes are identical" and "UNCITRAL has posted nothing" are different propositions and only the first is observed.
- **It does not license importing the UNCTAD digest finding.** The record's twelve-distinct-digests result was measured on `investmentpolicy.unctad.org` and its mechanism is expressly undiagnosed (`0804-B8`). Nothing about it transfers to `uncitral.un.org`, and I am not transferring it. The inverse also holds: UNCITRAL's digest stability is not evidence that UNCTAD's digests should have been stable.

### 2.4 Is a 500 weakly probative either way?

There is an available reading that would rescue the discharge, and it is seductive: *a well-behaved application returns 404 for an id it does not have; a 500 suggests the application tried to resolve id 999999, failed, and threw — which shows the id is being consumed, i.e. is load-bearing, i.e. branch (a) in substance, i.e. the discharge survives.*

**I reject it. Four reasons, in descending strength.**

1. **It is not diagnostic between the two competing accounts.** Both accounts predict a 500 is possible. Under the id-keyed account, the lookup fails and the handler throws. Under the competitor account — the head is generated from whatever is in the URL — the template can still throw while assembling the *rest* of the page from a record that does not exist, and the head-generation logic is simply never reached. A datum both hypotheses predict has a likelihood ratio near 1. It is not weak evidence; it is close to no evidence.
2. **It is post hoc.** The whole value of the pre-registration is that the mapping from outcome to conclusion was fixed before the outcome was known. Constructing a fourth branch *after* seeing the result, and constructing it in the direction that preserves a conclusion we already like, is exactly the move pre-registration exists to make visible. Having named the defect, I do not get to also enjoy the rescue.

### 2.6 What the pre-registration got wrong — a finding about our method

Three defects, of which the first is the general one.

**D1 — the decision rule partitioned the researcher's imagined answer space, not the instrument's declared outcome space.** The rule enumerated `{200 + head X, 200 + head Y, 404-or-redirect}`. But our own reduction schema has an `outcome` field whose declared values include at minimum `ok`, `refused`, `no_contact`, `robots_disallowed`, `not_attempted`, and the three `validate()` rejections (`scheme_not_https`, `private_address`, `host_not_allowed`) — I read them at `fetch_relay.py:139-155` and `base.py:249-277`. **D2 — no same-host positive control.** Every UNCITRAL row for five consecutive days has carried one; the discipline is by now automatic on that host. This row, on a different host and asking a different *kind* of question — a control on our own method rather than a watch — carried none. **D3 — the factorial was split across two days and two page states.** Yesterday's cell (true id + false slug) and today's cell (false id + true slug) are two cells of a 2×2 fired 23 h 44 m apart. Any cross-day comparison is hostage to the origin changing in between — and this is the host whose responses the record already knows to vary in ways it cannot explain (`0804-B8`, undiagnosed). All four cells belong in **one** batch: true+true, true+false, false+true, false+false. That costs three extra rows, which on this instrument is free.

*A defect I checked for and did not find:* branch (a)'s two limbs are not, as I first suspected, mutually unrealizable. Limb 1 (404) is observable under `outcome: "refused"`; limb 2 (differing `final_url`) is observable only under `outcome: "ok"`. They are realizable in different regimes, so the disjunction is sound — but the rule nowhere says so, and a reader could reasonably have expected `final_url` to be available to corroborate a 404. That is a latent trap, not the one that fired today, and I record it as latent.

### 2.7 Proposed amended decision rule (PROPOSAL ONLY — nothing built, nothing committed)

For any future control row, structured so a 5xx cannot be an unhandled branch:

**P0 — Admissibility precondition, evaluated first.** The batch is informative about a host only if (i) `control_ok: true`, **and** (ii) a *same-host positive control* row — a URL on that host known to return 200 carrying a known string — returned `outcome: "ok"` with `find_matched: true`. If P0 fails, **no branch is entered** and the batch is recorded as uninformative for that host. This is the P0 that was missing today.

**Then, partitioning `outcome` exhaustively:**

| `outcome` / status | Ruling |
|---|---|
| `ok`, 200 | Enter the substantive branches (b′)/(c′) on the head, plus (a′): `final_url` differs from requested → id load-bearing. |
| `refused`, **404 / 410** | The origin distinguishes the id. Discharge survives; residual closes. |
| `refused`, **5xx** | **NO BRANCH.** Uninformative about the research question. Re-fire once at ≥24 h separation *with* the same-host positive control. Two independent 5xx at separated timestamps establish only that the URL is *stably* erroring — still not evidence about head generation; escalate to the full 2×2 in one batch. |
| `refused`, **403 / 429** | Uninformative. Access control, not identification. (A2's standing rule: a 403 carries no existence information.) |
| `refused`, other non-200 | Uninformative; record the code and stop. |
| `no_contact`, `robots_disallowed`, `not_attempted`, `scheme_not_https`, `private_address`, `host_not_allowed` | Uninformative. The row did not reach the resource. |

**And the standing generalisation I would like adopted as a house rule, because it is cheap and mechanical:**

> **A pre-registered decision rule must partition the instrument's own declared outcome space, not the researcher's imagined answer space. Before firing, check that every `(outcome, status-class)` pair maps to a named branch — including at least one branch that says "this result is uninformative and no conclusion is drawn."** A rule with no such branch will, on the day it is needed, be read as though its nearest textual clause applied.

## §5 — INSIGHTS

Three, all sourced, none padded.

1. **"Repeated fetches of one unchanged page are one observation, not many" conflates two redundancies and should be split.** Redundancy *within* a batch (two fetches, one timestamp) is genuinely one observation with zero marginal information. Redundancy *across* batches (identical bytes, different timestamps) is N observations that **agree**, and agreement is a result. Today's data are eleven fetches at six timestamps returning one digest: **six observations of one state.** Collapsing to "one" discards the temporal spread that is the entire product of running a daily row; inflating to "eleven" double-counts. Source: the six-row enumeration at §1.2, `@bb0228df` and `@a8c5ad9c`.

2. **A pre-registered decision rule must partition the instrument's declared outcome space, not the researcher's imagined answer space — and this is checkable in seconds before firing.** Today's rule named three branches over `{200, 200, 404}` while the reduction schema has at least eight `outcome` values. One of the unhandled seven fired, and the day's control was consumed for nothing. Source: `analytics/fetch-requests/2026-08-07-daily.json` `note`, committed at `133c152`; outcome space at `scripts/fetch_relay.py:139-155` and `src/sources/base.py:249-277`.

3. **Our control discipline travels with habit, not with need.** Five consecutive days of UNCITRAL rows carried a same-page positive control; the one row on a different host, asking a harder question, carried none — which is why "this id errors" and "this host was erroring" are today inseparable. A control that is applied where it is automatic and omitted where it is novel is a ritual. Source: `analytics/fetch-requests/2026-08-07-daily.json` (three `urls` entries, two of them the UNCITRAL pair, none a UNCTAD control); result rec 3 `@bb0228df`.

**Method note for the self-training file.** The 2026-07-26 lesson (rigid designator, quoted) held again: quoting `"A/81/17"` produced a clean nine-result set with zero disambiguation cost and zero false leads — same shape as 2026-08-06's quoted-docket-number result. The **new** method observation is on the other side of the query: *the search layer's synthesised preamble is now a distinct hazard class from its snippets.* The no-search-synthesis rule as written targets **figures in snippets**; today's hazard was a **prose paragraph asserting venue and outcomes**, generated above the results, containing no figure at all and therefore not caught by the rule's literal terms. Proposed extension: **no assertion of any kind — figure, date, venue, characterisation or holding — enters the record from search-layer generated text, as distinct from a result title or a retrieved body.**
