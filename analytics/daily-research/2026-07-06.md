**New, sourced corrective finding.** The Statute of the Advisory Centre on International Investment Dispute Resolution was adopted "in principle" at the **57th UNCITRAL Commission Session in 2024** — not at the 59th session (July 2026). The 16-article statute (covering creation, objectives, governance, membership, services, financing, legal status) was finalized and adopted in principle in 2024. The 2025 work focused on operationalization (three operationalization meetings; calls for host-country expressions of interest). The 59th session's Advisory Centre work is further operationalization and final statute updates, with formal UN General Assembly adoption targeted for December 2026.

**Correction to STATE_OF_THE_ANSWER:** The prior framing stated "The Commission agreed to finalize the Advisory Centre Statute at the 59th session." This overstates the 59th session's role: the statute was adopted "in principle" one year earlier (57th session, 2024). The December 2026 GA adoption is a formal adoption of an already in-principle adopted instrument, not a first-time adoption. This is a precision correction, not a material change to the research question analysis.

**Ring relevance:** The Advisory Centre is a secondary track within the UNCITRAL WG III reform package, providing legal aid to developing country respondents in ISDS. Its adoption timeline is background context for the ISDS reform landscape (T1/T2). No direct Ring 1/2/3 pharmaceutical IP consequence, but it completes the picture of the 57th–59th session deliverable sequence: (1) Code of Conduct for Arbitrators — 56th session (July 2023, Vienna, UNGA Res. 78/105); (2) Advisory Centre Statute — adopted in principle 57th session (2024); (3) Procedural Protocol (A/CN.9/1246) — 59th session (expected July 2026). ### 2D. Santiago Salmon Case — Joyvio/Australis Backsource (digest item 2)

Fully backsourced. The digest item "Court of Appeal of Santiago sets aside salmon venture award on ultra petita grounds" (June 29, score 25, Ring 2 match) is the dispute of **Joyvio Group Co. Ltd.** (Chinese agribusiness, subsidiary of Legend Holdings) v. **Isidoro Quiroga** (former owner of Australis Seafoods SA).

Background: In 2018, Joyvio acquired Australis for USD 921 million. Joyvio subsequently alleged Quiroga deliberately concealed and falsified salmon production data during due diligence (overproducing salmon to inflate valuation). The domestic arbitration tribunal (three-attorney panel, Santiago, applying Chilean commercial law) found for Joyvio and awarded USD 292 million (plus interest of ~USD 75 million). The Court of Appeal of Santiago set the award aside in June 2026 on *ultra petita* grounds: the tribunal awarded relief that was not sought in the pleadings.

**Classification:** This is a **domestic commercial arbitration** (not ISDS). No bilateral investment treaty; no state as party; no intellectual property as covered investment; no investor-state treaty framework. The "judicial_or_regulatory_measure" Ring 2 match in the digest was a **classifier false positive**: the court's role here is setting aside a private commercial award, not a state act against an investor's treaty-protected rights. The case does not reach the research question on any ring.

This backsource is diagnostic: the digest classifier is treating any court decision touching an arbitration award as a potential Ring 2 match, without requiring the measure-taker to be a state acting in its public capacity against a treaty-protected investment. See Part 5 for the resulting optimization proposal.

## PART 5: COUNCIL OPTIMIZATION / BREAKTHROUGH IDEA

**New proposal:** Tighten the Ring 2 classifier sub-criterion to require "state-as-measure-taker."

**Trigger:** The Joyvio/Australis backsource (today) exposed a persistent Ring 2 false-positive class. The digest scored "Court of Appeal of Santiago sets aside salmon venture award" at 25 — enough for a WATCH-level flag — on the sole basis that a court issued a decision affecting an arbitration award. The Ring 2 concept in the research question requires a *governmental or judicial measure taken by or attributable to the host state in its public capacity, affecting an investor's rights under a treaty*. A domestic court setting aside a private commercial arbitration between two private parties is not this.

**Proposal:** Add a mandatory sub-criterion to the Ring 2 classifier: **the "measure" must be taken by or attributable to a state or state body (executive, legislative, or judicial) acting in its public capacity, within a treaty or investment law framework** — not a domestic court adjudicating a private commercial dispute between private parties. Implementation: add `state_as_measure_taker: required` sub-criterion to the Ring 2 entry in `fingerprint.yaml`, with exemplar false-positive cases (Joyvio/Australis) and exemplar true-positive cases (Chile v. Phillip Morris; Canada petroleum disclosure in Einarsson).

**Dedup check:** Not in optimization log. Distinct from:
- MFN-scope tag (2026-07-02): adds a Ring 3 sub-tag; does not address Ring 2 false positives
- Tagged-case posture tracker (2026-07-03): addresses temporal depth, not Ring 2 precision
- Pre-filing dispute notice monitor (2026-07-05): addresses intake timing
- Host-state domestic law monitor (2026-07-04): addresses intake breadth
- Cross-model verifier (2026-06-23): addresses quality via model architecture

This proposal addresses Ring 2 *definition precision* — reducing false positives from private commercial disputes misclassified as state measures. **Genuinely new.** Effort: low (single fingerprint.yaml revision).
