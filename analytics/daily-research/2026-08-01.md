### Task 1 — China–Switzerland BIT (2009) Forum Relationship

**Finding A (candidate — proposed CLOSED-PROVISIONAL):** Multiple secondary sources converge on the China–Switzerland BIT (2009) forum-relationship architecture. The most authoritative accessible source is the Prager Dreifuss commentary on China-Switzerland BIT (citing SR 0.975.224.9, the Swiss federal law compilation reference for the treaty), which explicitly addresses Article 11 of the China-Switzerland BIT as the dispute resolution provision. The architecture is as follows:

- **Article 11(2)** gives the investor a choice of domestic courts or international arbitration — the investor selects the forum.
- **Article 11(4)** imposes a no-U-turn requirement: before submitting a dispute to international arbitration, the investor must withdraw any pending domestic court proceedings.

This is structurally parallel to the China-Germany BIT (2003) Protocol Article 9(c) no-U-turn mechanism confirmed in *Hela Schwarz v. China*, but located in the main BIT text (Article 11) rather than in a Protocol supplement.

**Corroboration from Montenero pleadings (OUT-OF-CHANNEL — see B4):** The Montenero v. People's Republic of China arbitration (UNCTAD Navigator case 1154), reached via Jusmundi and IAReporter, invokes Arts. 6, 4(1), and 8 of the China-Switzerland BIT — consistent with Art. 11 being the ISDS provision (not Art. 9), since the pleadings' BIT article citations skip directly to Arts. 6 and 8 for substantive standards and Art. 4(1) for investment definition, leaving Art. 11 as the natural home for procedural ISDS access. This is a structural inference, not a verbatim quote from the pleadings.
