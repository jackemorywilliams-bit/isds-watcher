Okuashvili v. Georgia — the MFN thread moves, with verification caveats

The week's material advance is contemporary reporting whose headline indicates the Swedish Supreme Court found the claimant in Okuashvili v. Georgia can use an MFN detour to access SCC arbitration. Read against the confirmed procedural chain (SCC Partial Award Aug 2022 → Svea annulment Nov 2024 → Supreme Court decision 26 June 2026), this appears to confirm the direction of a reversal restoring SCC-forum access. Verification is limited: the holding and its jurisdiction-restored direction are drawn from the IAReporter title and a GAR headline corroborating the earlier Svea posture; the full Swedish Supreme Court judgment text and NJA citation were not accessed. The holding should therefore be treated as headline-only and the direction as inferred, not confirmed from disposition text.

If borne out, the case complicates our carried thread. We had carried, at medium confidence, that the MFN-forum-bypass pathway is architecturally closed for China BITs because Chinese BITs explicitly exclude MFN from ISDS. The reported result does not overturn that carve-out logic, but reframes it: to the extent the Supreme Court upheld the detour, Okuashvili would be authority for the vitality of MFN forum-access where the base treaty does not exclude it, making the 'closed for China' point a narrow, treaty-text-contingent corollary rather than a doctrinally settled hostility to the detour.

Net for the research question: the China-BIT-closure claim should stay at medium confidence and be relabelled treaty-text-contingent. On Ring 3 this keeps MFN a plausible route into a forum for an IP-as-investment claimant under a non-Chinese BIT. Whether any Svea remand remains open, or the merits arbitration is fully restored, is not resolved by what could be accessed; carry forward.

Wingtech / Nexperia — still no confirmed ICSID registration

The highest-priority live thread yields the same honest answer: registration remains unconfirmed. This session surfaced only Jan–May 2026 press repeating the claim announcement (Tekedia, AMS Advocaten, IDNFinancials, Techzine), none supplying an ICSID case number or registration page; these were reviewed as headlines/characterizations and the docket status is explicitly unconfirmed. The verbs remain pre-registration ('seeks,' 'announces,' 'demands'). The six-month cooling-off from the 15 Oct 2025 notice expired around 15 April 2026, with roughly twelve weeks elapsed and no confirmed registration in indexed sources. Status: filed-not-confirmed, possibly-not-yet-registered, or possibly under another forum.

A parallel Dutch-court track (reported by Asia Times, 17 Feb 2026) is relevant because that domestic litigation is itself the kind of Ring-2 judicial measure that could later support a denial-of-justice framing if a treaty claim is registered. Wingtech remains the single most on-theme item on the standing agenda — an IP/technology-transfer intervention meeting an emergency regulatory act under a China–Netherlands BIT with no national-security carve-out — but it cannot be upgraded from notice/announcement to pending arbitration absent a docket.

UNCITRAL 59th session and off-theme cleanup

No post-session A/CN.9/1246 report is indexed as of Day 8 (session closes 10 July); the agenda PDF is confirmed present but access-blocked, so no adoption, referral, or noting can be asserted. The precision correction stands: the Advisory Centre Statute was adopted 'in principle' at the 57th session (2024), with the December 2026 step being formal GA adoption of an already-in-principle instrument. The scope-of-application and opt-in question (temporal cutoff; application to pre-existing China–Germany/Switzerland/France BITs) remains open and unread in primary text.

Separately, the 'Santiago salmon award set aside' item was backsourced as Joyvio Group v. Isidoro Quiroga — a domestic Chilean commercial arbitration annulled on ultra petita grounds, with no treaty, State party, or IP-as-covered-investment. It is not ISDS and was a classifier false positive; a court touching an arbitral award is not a State measure against a treaty-protected investment. Its concealed-data element is thematically adjacent to the trade-secret/clinical-data sub-question but the purely private posture means it does not bear on the research question.
